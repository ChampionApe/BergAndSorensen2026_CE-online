O.8 The planner’s entry margin against open access

Proposition 12 of the paper, stated in Appendix C, compares at one price vector the margin that decides whether the planner’s economy treats waste with the margins of the two economies without instruments, open access and the owned stockpile. It splits the difference into what the instruments and the property right add, says when the planner’s threshold is lower, and shows that the conditions for it are needed. This page proves it in full. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

The statement

Appendix C states the proposition after this passage, which defines the identity it cites:

The three entry margins are functions of one price vector \(\left(C^N_N,p^S,F_K,p^P,p^W,\zeta,\Omega\right)\), and subtracting them at a common vector shows, term by term, what the instruments and the property right add. With \(V\) the virgin displacement value (V), \(V_0\equiv C^N_N+p^S\) its open-access counterpart, and \(\tilde F_K\equiv F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\), \[ E^{\mathrm{pl}}-E^{\mathrm{OA}} =\Big[\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V,\tilde F_K\right)-\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V_0,F_K\right)\Big]+p^P\left(1-d^W\right)-\zeta\Omega^Wc^c , \qquad V-V_0=\zeta\Omega^NC^N_N+\left(\Omega^{N,S}+d^W\right)p^P . \tag{I} \]

Proposition 12 (the planner’s entry margin against open access). General primitives. Take a vector at which both economies extract, with \(F_K>0\), \(p^P>0\), the wedge inequalities \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y>0\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^j>0\) for \(j\in\left\{N,W\right\}\), and \(\zeta=\sigma\left(p^W-p^M\right)\) for some \(\sigma\in\left[0,1\right]\) and some \(p^M\), as (Z) requires. There the identity (I) holds and its bracket is bounded on both sides by the value of the recovery gap at either intensity. At \(\phi^I=0\), so that \(\zeta=0\), the planner’s threshold is lower, \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}>E^{\mathrm{OA}}\), whatever the sign of \(p^W\). At \(\phi^I>0\) it is lower when \(\zeta\ge0\), which gives \(V\ge V_0\), and the diversion gain covers the collection wedge, \(p^P\left(1-d^W\right)>\zeta\Omega^Wc^c\). Each condition is needed: there are such vectors with \(\zeta<0\) in the liability phase (\(p^W>0\)), or with a large collection wedge, at which the ordering reverses. Against the owned stockpile, at a fee with \(1-\tau^W\Omega^Y>0\) and \(1+\tau^W\Omega^W>0\), the same identity holds with \(\zeta-\tau^W\) in place of \(\zeta\) in the three wedges and the overburden term \(\Omega^{N,S}p^P\) unchanged.

  1. and (Z) are the virgin displacement value and the first-order condition in \(\Omega\) of Appendix A, restated in What the proof uses with the other terms of the statement.

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions. The proposition holds for general primitives, with no functional form: it compares functions of one price vector at one date, so its proof is algebra on the three margins, a lemma on the recovery value and two families of vectors built for the purpose. It uses no forward form of any price, and of the first-order conditions only those that put the three margins in terms of the vector. Like the entry condition of Section 4.2, the proposition “orders thresholds at a common point of the price space, not entry dates along paths whose states and prices differ”.

What the proof uses

From Section 2 of the paper. Within a date we suppress the time argument and write \(a'\) for \(a_x\). We read the yield as continuous on \(\left[0,\infty\right)\) and twice differentiable on \(\left(0,\infty\right)\), as its derivatives presume. With \(a\left(0\right)=0\), \(a'>0\), \(a''<0\) and \(a\to\bar a\in\left(0,1\right]\), the yield rises strictly from zero towards \(\bar a\), so \(0\le a\left(x\right)<\bar a\le1\); its slope \(a'\) is positive and strictly decreasing, and it tends to zero at infinity, since a limit \(\ell>0\) would make \(a\left(x\right)\ge a\left(1\right)+\ell\left(x-1\right)\) unbounded. The collection charge is positive, \(c^c>0\); \(d^W\in\left(0,1\right)\), \(\Omega^{N,S}\ge0\) and \(C^N_N>0\). The intensities \(\Omega^j=\Omega\omega^j\) are nonnegative, as \(\omega^j\ge0\) and Problem 1 has \(\Omega\ge0\), and the durables coefficient enters as \(\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I\). Section 2 states \(p^W=-p^{\mathcal W}\), “the social cost of waste is minus the value of a tonne in the waste stock”, and we read the statement’s liability phase as \(p^W>0\), a waste stock in which a tonne has negative value.

From Section 4 of the paper. Raw material trades at \(p^R\) per tonne, capital rents at \(r^K\), the gate fee is \(\tau^W\) and the material-content price \(z\); a unit of the final good put to the current use \(j\) costs the user price \(P^j\equiv1+\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega\omega^j\). The emission tax \(\tau^P\) is levied per tonne of \(\Xi\) “on the agent that emits it, the waste sector on the emission \(\Xi^h\) of its handled stream and the mining sector on the mass \(\mathcal N\) it displaces”, and \(\tilde p\equiv p^R+d^W\tau^P\) is the waste sector’s value of a recovered tonne (Section 4.1). Of the mining sector’s conditions “at interior extraction and exploration” the first is the extraction condition \[ p^R=P^NC^N_N+\tau^P\Omega^{N,S}+p^S , \tag{M} \] and we read “an economy extracts” as (M) holding, as Proposition 9 does for open access: “where it extracts, \(F_R=C^N_N+p^S\)”.

Under open access (Section 4.2) “dumping is free, \(\tau^W=0\), and with \(\tau^P=z=0\) every user price is one, a recovered tonne sells at \(p^R=F_R\) and capital rents at \(r^K=F_K\)”. At the owned stockpile the waste stock is owned and \(\tau^P=z=0\), “and the sector faces \(p^R=F_R\left(1-\tau^W\Omega^Y\right)\) and \(r^K=F_K\left(1-\tau^W\Omega^Y\right)\)”. “In both economies the mining sector emits its displaced mass untaxed.” The recovery value of a treated tonne and the entry margin, “the net gain from the first treated tonne”, are (Section 4.2) \[ \mathcal G^{x}\!\left(\tilde p,r^K\right)\equiv\max_{x\ge0}\left[a\left(x\right)\tilde p-r^Kx\right], \qquad E\equiv\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(\tilde p,r^K\right)+\tau^P\left(1-d^W\right)-P^Wc^c , \tag{E} \] and \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}\) and \(E^{\mathrm{OS}}\) are \(E\) under open access and at the owned stockpile. Proposition 9 defines \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}\) as “\(E\) at the planner’s prices, \(\tilde p=\mathcal G^K\), \(r^K=F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\), \(\tau^P=p^P\) and \(P^W=1+\zeta\Omega^W\)”.

From Appendix A of the paper. With the gross material value \(\Psi\equiv F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)+\zeta\) and the storage share \(\sigma\equiv\Phi^IG/R\in\left[0,1\right]\), the first-order condition in \(\Omega\) is \[ \zeta=\sigma\left(p^W-p^M\right), \tag{Z} \] and “At \(\phi^I=0\), \(\zeta=0\)”, since \(\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I\) vanishes and with it \(\sigma\). The wedge conditions are \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y>0\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^j>0\) for every use \(j\), and “Every flow in final goods is grossed up by \(1\pm\zeta\Omega^j\) for the material it carries”. The gain on the recycling-capital margin is \(\mathcal G^K\equiv\Psi-p^W+d^Wp^P\). At interior extraction the extraction condition is \[ \Psi=C^N_N\left(1+\zeta\Omega^N\right)+p^S+p^W+p^P\Omega^{N,S}, \tag{N} \] and “While extraction is interior, (N) gives \(\Psi\)”, so that with the virgin displacement value \[ V\equiv\Psi-p^W+d^Wp^P=C^N_N\left(1+\zeta\Omega^N\right)+p^S+\left(\Omega^{N,S}+d^W\right)p^P, \qquad \mathcal G^K=V, \tag{V} \] the second form being (N) substituted into the first, in which \(p^W\) cancels.

The margins at a vector. Where the planner extracts, (V) gives \(\mathcal G^K=V\), and the planner’s prices in (E) give \[ E^{\mathrm{pl}}=\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V,\tilde F_K\right)+p^P\left(1-d^W\right)-\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)c^c . \] Under open access \(\tilde p=p^R=F_R\), \(r^K=F_K\), \(\tau^P=0\) and \(P^W=1\); where it extracts, (M) with \(P^N=1\) and \(\tau^P=0\) gives \(F_R=C^N_N+p^S=V_0\), so \[ E^{\mathrm{OA}}=\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V_0,F_K\right)-c^c . \] At the owned stockpile with the fee \(\tau^W\), \(\tilde p=p^R\), \(r^K=r^{\mathrm{OS}}\equiv F_K\left(1-\tau^W\Omega^Y\right)\), \(\tau^P=0\) and \(P^W=1+\tau^W\Omega^W\); where it extracts, (M) with \(P^N=1+\tau^W\Omega^N\) and \(\tau^P=0\) gives \(p^R=V^{\mathrm{OS}}\equiv C^N_N\left(1+\tau^W\Omega^N\right)+p^S\), so \[ E^{\mathrm{OS}}=\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V^{\mathrm{OS}},r^{\mathrm{OS}}\right)-\left(1+\tau^W\Omega^W\right)c^c . \] A vector of the statement is one at which the planner and open access extract, with \(F_K>0\), \(p^P>0\), the wedge inequalities \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y>0\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^j>0\) for \(j\in\left\{N,W\right\}\), and (Z) for some \(\sigma\in\left[0,1\right]\) and some \(p^M\). In the statement’s last sentence the owned stockpile extracts in place of open access, at the same vector extended by the fee. The yield, \(c^c\), \(d^W\), \(\Omega^{N,S}\) and the relative intensities \(\omega^j\) are primitives, common to the three economies.

The recovery value

Lemma R. Let \(r>0\) and \(\tilde p\) be real, and \(f\left(x\right)\equiv a\left(x\right)\tilde p-rx\) on \(\left[0,\infty\right)\).

(a) \(f\) attains its maximum at exactly one point \(x^*\left(\tilde p,r\right)\), and the maximum, \(\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p,r\right)\) of (E), is nonnegative and at least \(a\left(x\right)\tilde p-rx\) for every \(x\ge0\).

(b) For real \(\tilde p_0,\tilde p_1\) and \(r_0,r_1>0\), with \(x_i\equiv x^*\left(\tilde p_i,r_i\right)\), \[ a\left(x_0\right)\left(\tilde p_1-\tilde p_0\right)-x_0\left(r_1-r_0\right)\;\le\;\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p_1,r_1\right)-\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p_0,r_0\right)\;\le\;a\left(x_1\right)\left(\tilde p_1-\tilde p_0\right)-x_1\left(r_1-r_0\right). \]

(c) If \(\tilde p>0\), \(\hat x>0\) and \(r\ge\bar a\tilde p/\hat x\), then \(\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p,r\right)\le a\left(\hat x\right)\tilde p\).

Proof.

  1. \(f\) is continuous with \(f\left(0\right)=0\). If \(\tilde p\le0\), then \(f\left(x\right)\le-rx<0\) for \(x>0\), as \(a\ge0\), so the maximiser is \(0\). Let \(\tilde p>0\). On \(\left(0,\infty\right)\), \(f'=a'\tilde p-r\) is continuous and strictly decreasing, with limit \(-r<0\) at infinity. If \(f'<0\) throughout, \(f\), continuous at \(0\), falls strictly on \(\left[0,\infty\right)\) and the maximiser is \(0\). Otherwise \(f'\) is nonnegative somewhere and negative far out, so by the intermediate value theorem it vanishes at exactly one point \(x^*>0\), positive before it and negative after; \(f\) rises strictly on \(\left[0,x^*\right]\) and falls strictly on \(\left[x^*,\infty\right)\), and the maximiser is \(x^*\). Either way it is unique. The maximum is at least \(f\left(0\right)=0\) and at least \(f\left(x\right)\) for every \(x\).

  2. By (a), \(\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p_1,r_1\right)\ge a\left(x_0\right)\tilde p_1-r_1x_0\), while \(\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p_0,r_0\right)=a\left(x_0\right)\tilde p_0-r_0x_0\); subtracting gives the lower bound. Likewise \(\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p_0,r_0\right)\ge a\left(x_1\right)\tilde p_0-r_0x_1\) and \(\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p_1,r_1\right)=a\left(x_1\right)\tilde p_1-r_1x_1\) give the upper bound.

  3. For \(x\le\hat x\), \(f\left(x\right)\le a\left(\hat x\right)\tilde p\), as \(a\) is increasing and \(rx\ge0\). For \(x>\hat x\), \(f\left(x\right)\le\bar a\tilde p-rx<\bar a\tilde p-r\hat x\le0\le a\left(\hat x\right)\tilde p\).

Proof of Proposition 12

Proof. Step 1: the identity (I) holds. At a vector of the statement \(F_K>0\) and \(\tilde F_K=F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)>0\), so both maxima exist by Lemma R(a). Subtracting \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}\) from \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}\), the collection terms leave \(-\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)c^c+c^c=-\zeta\Omega^Wc^c\), which is the first part of (I), and (V) less \(V_0=C^N_N+p^S\) is its second part. Neither margin depends on \(p^W\).

Step 2: the bracket lies between the recovery gap at the two best intensities. Let \(x^{\mathrm{OA}}\equiv x^*\left(V_0,F_K\right)\) and \(x^{\mathrm{pl}}\equiv x^*\left(V,\tilde F_K\right)\) be the best intensities of open access and of the planner, and call the recovery gap at an intensity \(x\) the amount \(a\left(x\right)\left(V-V_0\right)-x\left(\tilde F_K-F_K\right)\) by which the planner’s objective in (E) exceeds open access’s when both use \(x\). Lemma R(b) at \(\left(\tilde p_0,r_0\right)=\left(V_0,F_K\right)\) and \(\left(\tilde p_1,r_1\right)=\left(V,\tilde F_K\right)\) gives \[ a\left(x^{\mathrm{OA}}\right)\left(V-V_0\right)-x^{\mathrm{OA}}\left(\tilde F_K-F_K\right) \;\le\;\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V,\tilde F_K\right)-\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V_0,F_K\right)\;\le\; a\left(x^{\mathrm{pl}}\right)\left(V-V_0\right)-x^{\mathrm{pl}}\left(\tilde F_K-F_K\right), \tag{B} \] with \(\tilde F_K-F_K=-\zeta\Omega^YF_K\).

Step 3: with \(\zeta\ge0\) and the collection condition the planner’s threshold is lower, and at \(\phi^I=0\) it is lower with no condition. Let \(\zeta\ge0\). Each term of \(V-V_0\) is nonnegative and \(d^Wp^P>0\), by \(\Omega^N\ge0\), \(C^N_N>0\), \(\Omega^{N,S}\ge0\), \(d^W>0\) and \(p^P>0\), so \(V>V_0\). And \(-x\left(\tilde F_K-F_K\right)=x\zeta\Omega^YF_K\ge0\) for \(x\ge0\). So the left side of (B) is nonnegative, as \(a\ge0\), and (I) gives \[ E^{\mathrm{pl}}-E^{\mathrm{OA}}\;\ge\;p^P\left(1-d^W\right)-\zeta\Omega^Wc^c . \] Hence \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}>E^{\mathrm{OA}}\) when the diversion gain covers the collection wedge, which is the claim at \(\phi^I>0\). At \(\phi^I=0\), \(\zeta=0\) by Appendix A, the collection wedge vanishes and the bound is \(p^P\left(1-d^W\right)>0\), by \(p^P>0\) and \(d^W<1\). So the planner’s threshold is lower at every vector of the statement with \(\zeta=0\), whatever \(p^W\), which appears in neither margin.

Step 4: with \(\zeta<0\) in the liability phase the ordering can reverse. A condition is needed when dropping it lets the conclusion fail at some vector of the statement. Each of the two families of steps 4 and 5 fixes the relative intensities \(\omega^j\), which Section 2 leaves free in \(\left[0,\infty\right)\), and works whatever the yield, \(c^c\), \(d^W\) and \(\Omega^{N,S}\); each has \(\sigma>0\), so it belongs to an economy with \(\phi^I>0\).

Take \(\omega^Y>0\) and every other relative intensity zero, so \(\Omega^j=0\) for \(j\ne Y\). Choose \(p^W>0\), \(F_K>0\), \(p^S>0\), \(p^P>0\) with \(p^P\left(1-d^W\right)<c^c\), and \(C^N_N>0\) with \(a\left(1\right)V_0-F_K>c^c\), possible as \(a\left(1\right)>0\). Then \(\varepsilon\equiv c^c-p^P\left(1-d^W\right)>0\) and \(V=V_0+\left(\Omega^{N,S}+d^W\right)p^P>0\), whatever \(\zeta\) and \(\Omega\). As \(a\) is continuous at zero with \(a\left(0\right)=0\), there is \(\hat x>0\) with \(a\left(\hat x\right)V<\varepsilon\). Set \(\zeta=-1\), which (Z) gives at \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) and \(p^M=p^W+2\), and \(\Omega=\bar aV/\left(\hat xF_K\omega^Y\right)\), so that \(\tilde F_K=F_K\left(1+\Omega^Y\right)>\bar aV/\hat x\). The wedge conditions hold for every use, \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y=1+\Omega^Y\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^j=1\) for \(j\ne Y\), and so does the collection condition, \(\zeta\Omega^Wc^c=0<p^P\left(1-d^W\right)\). By Lemma R(c), \(\mathcal G^x\left(V,\tilde F_K\right)\le a\left(\hat x\right)V<\varepsilon\), so \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}<\varepsilon+p^P\left(1-d^W\right)-c^c=0\), while Lemma R(a) at \(x=1\) gives \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}\ge a\left(1\right)V_0-F_K-c^c>0\). So \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}<0<E^{\mathrm{OA}}\): the ordering reverses, and the two margins have opposite signs. Both prices of raw material are positive, as at a date at which production runs: \(F_R=V_0\) under open access, and for the planner, by the definition of \(\Psi\), \(F_R=\left(\Psi-\zeta\right)/\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)=\left(\Psi+1\right)/\left(1+\Omega^Y\right)\), with \(\Psi=C^N_N+p^S+p^W+p^P\Omega^{N,S}>0\) by (N).

Step 5: with a large collection wedge the ordering can reverse. Take \(\omega^N=\omega^Y=0<\omega^W\), so \(\Omega^N=\Omega^Y=0\) and \(\tilde F_K=F_K\). Choose \(C^N_N\), \(p^S\), \(F_K\), \(p^P\) and \(p^W\) positive; set \(\zeta=p^W/2\), which (Z) gives at \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) and \(p^M=0\); and choose \(\Omega\) with \(\zeta\Omega^Wc^c>\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)p^P\), possible as \(\omega^W>0\) and \(c^c>0\). The wedge conditions hold for every use, \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y=1\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^j\ge1\), as \(\zeta>0\), and \(\zeta\ge0\) gives \(V\ge V_0\) by step 3; only the collection condition fails. Here \(V-V_0=\left(\Omega^{N,S}+d^W\right)p^P\) and \(\tilde F_K-F_K=0\), so the right side of (B) is at most \(\left(\Omega^{N,S}+d^W\right)p^P\), as \(a<1\), and (I) gives \[ E^{\mathrm{pl}}-E^{\mathrm{OA}}\;\le\;\left(\Omega^{N,S}+d^W\right)p^P+p^P\left(1-d^W\right)-\zeta\Omega^Wc^c=\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)p^P-\zeta\Omega^Wc^c<0 . \] The planner’s price of raw material is \(\Psi-\zeta=C^N_N+p^S+p^P\Omega^{N,S}+p^W/2>0\), and open access’s is \(V_0>0\).

Step 6: against the owned stockpile the identity holds with \(\zeta-\tau^W\) in the three wedges. Take a vector of the statement with the owned stockpile extracting in place of open access, extended by a fee with \(1-\tau^W\Omega^Y>0\) and \(1+\tau^W\Omega^W>0\). With \(F_K>0\), the first condition on the fee makes \(r^{\mathrm{OS}}\) positive, and \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y>0\) makes \(\tilde F_K\) positive, so both maxima exist; the second condition makes the handling wedge \(P^W=1+\tau^W\Omega^W\) positive, and the identity does not use it. Subtracting \(E^{\mathrm{OS}}\) from \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}\), the collection terms leave \(-\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)c^c+\left(1+\tau^W\Omega^W\right)c^c=-\left(\zeta-\tau^W\right)\Omega^Wc^c\), and \[ E^{\mathrm{pl}}-E^{\mathrm{OS}}=\Big[\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V,\tilde F_K\right)-\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(V^{\mathrm{OS}},r^{\mathrm{OS}}\right)\Big]+p^P\left(1-d^W\right)-\left(\zeta-\tau^W\right)\Omega^Wc^c, \] \[ V-V^{\mathrm{OS}}=\left(\zeta-\tau^W\right)\Omega^NC^N_N+\left(\Omega^{N,S}+d^W\right)p^P, \qquad \tilde F_K-r^{\mathrm{OS}}=-\left(\zeta-\tau^W\right)\Omega^YF_K . \] In (I) the three wedges are those on the extraction effort, \(\zeta\Omega^NC^N_N\) in \(V-V_0\), on the rental, \(\tilde F_K-F_K=-\zeta\Omega^YF_K\) inside the bracket, and on the collection charge, \(\zeta\Omega^Wc^c\). Against the owned stockpile each carries \(\zeta-\tau^W\) in place of \(\zeta\), while the overburden term \(\Omega^{N,S}p^P\) is unchanged, as are \(d^Wp^P\) and the diversion gain: the owned stockpile’s mine, like open access’s, pays nothing on the mass it displaces. At \(\tau^W=0\) the owned stockpile’s margin is open access’s, and the identity is (I).

O.9 The planner’s allocation as a competitive equilibrium
