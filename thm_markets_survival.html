O.11 A laissez-faire collapse where the planner survives

Theorem 1 of the paper (a laissez-faire collapse where the planner survives) states that the floor and soft-ceiling cell contains primitives under which no optimal path of the planner collapses, while every open-access path does. This page proves it in full by constructing such primitives. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used. Three lemmas about the economy the proof constructs, Lemmas Z, W and E, are proved on the continuation of this page.

The statement

Section 3.7 of the paper calls the inequality \[ \left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}<1+\rho \tag{R} \] the collapse ranking condition.

Theorem 1 (a laissez-faire collapse where the planner survives). There are primitives in the floor and soft-ceiling cell, in the workhorse family of Section 3.6 with the exponential yield at \(\bar a=1\), \(\phi^I=0\), \(\eta>1\) and the collapse ranking condition (R) failing, under which the planner’s problem has an optimal path; every optimal path has a finite value and an infinite operating set, so is not in state A, and keeps \(\mu\) times its remaining budget at or above \(\bar R\) at every date; while every open-access path recycles nothing before a date \(t^*\), holds its remaining budget below \(\bar R/\mu\) from then on, and is in state A. The open-access part uses no property of preferences, of the household or of the mining sector.

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions. Problem 1 takes the initial stocks as given, so the primitives we exhibit include them. In the economy we construct, all the material left is in the waste stock, enough to carry the floor if the loop is closed from the first period. The collection charge lies above any price an operating final-goods sector pays for a tonne of material at date \(0\), and below what a path that closes the loop from the first period can afford. The open-access part is then accounting on the constraints of Problem 1, with the two sector conditions that define an open-access path applied at date \(0\). The planner’s part combines Proposition 5 of the paper with Lemmas B, W and E and uses no first-order condition. What the proof uses restates what the proof takes from the paper beyond its Section 2.

What the proof uses

From Section 2 of the paper. A feasible path meets every constraint of Problem 1: the goods constraint, the ledger \(W_t=R_t-\Delta M^K_{t+1}\), the intensity condition and the transitions, with the flows as Section 2 defines them, the domains \(C,N,D,K^R,W,\Omega\ge0\), \(K_{t+1}\ge0\) and \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\), and the mass restrictions \(S_t\ge0\), \(X_t\le\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t\ge0\). Capital is split between production and recycling, \(K=K^Y+K^R\), so \(K^R_t\le K_t\), and \(K^Y_t=K_t-K^R_t\) is the capital input of \(F\). The waste-stock transition \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+W_t\), with \(W_t\ge0\) and \(\mathcal W_0\ge0\), keeps \(\mathcal W_t\ge0\), so the handled flow \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\) is nonnegative. The treated flow is \(T_t\equiv\varpi_tH_t\). Where \(T_t>0\), recovered material is \(R^R_t=a\left(x_t,t\right)T_t\) with \(x_t=K^R_t/T_t\); where \(T_t=0\), nothing is recovered. Either way \(0\le R^R_t\le\bar aT_t\), as \(a<\bar a\), and with \(T_t\le H_t\) this is the throughput cap of Section 2.3, \(R^R_t\le\bar a\mu\mathcal W_t\). Raw material input is \(R_t=N_t+R^R_t\). Every cost flow is nonnegative: \(C^N\left(0,S,t\right)=0\) with \(C^N_N>0\), likewise \(C^D\), and \(c^c>0\) and \(c^T\ge0\), as \(c^T\left(0\right)=0\) with \(c^{T\prime}>0\).

Welfare is \(U_0=\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^t\left[u\left(C_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right]\), with \(\beta\equiv1/\left(1+\rho\right)\) and \(\rho>0\). The value of a feasible path is the limit, as \(n\to\infty\), of the partial sums \(\sum_{t=0}^{n}\beta^t\left[u\left(C_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right]\), where that limit exists in \(\left[-\infty,\infty\right]\); the value is finite when the limit is a real number. An optimal path is a feasible path whose value exists and is at least the value of every feasible path whose value exists.

From Section 3. The material budget (Section 3.2) is \(\mathcal B\equiv\bar N+M^K_0+\mathcal W_0\), with the virgin budget \(\bar N\equiv S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0\). The operating set (Section 3.3) is \(\mathcal O\equiv\left\{t:R_t\ge\bar R\right\}\), and Definition 1 calls a path in state A, “collapse if \(\mathcal O\) is finite: production ceases after a finite total number of operating periods, output is zero thereafter, and consumption, financed from the capital stock alone, converges to zero”. A feasible path with a finite operating set meets the whole clause: after its last operating date output is zero, so the goods constraint, with every cost flow nonnegative, \(C_t\ge0\) and \(K_{t+1}\ge0\), gives \(K_{t+1}\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\) and \(C_t\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\), and capital and consumption tend to zero. So a feasible path is in state A exactly when its operating set is finite.

The workhorse (Section 3.6) “is the Cobb-Douglas case of the specification the quantitative model of Section 5 implements: output \(A_te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR^e\right)^{\gamma}\) with \(\beta_K+\gamma<1\), a marginal extraction cost rising with the elasticity \(\mu_N\) as the reserve falls, the exponential yield \(a\left(x\right)=\bar a\left(1-e^{-\xi x}\right)\), and CRRA utility”. Appendix B.6 takes “the marginal extraction cost proportional to \(\left(S_{\mathrm{ref}}/S\right)^{\mu_N}\) as the reserve falls”. The trends are those of Assumption 1(i), \(A_{t+1}/A_t=1+g_A\) and \(B_{t+1}/B_t=1+g_B\) with \(g_A,g_B\ge0\) and \(g_A+g_B>0\), and Section 3.6 writes \(\Gamma\equiv\left(1+g_A\right)\left(1+g_B\right)^{\gamma}>1\). CRRA utility with elasticity \(\eta>1\) is \(u\left(C\right)=C^{1-\eta}/\left(1-\eta\right)\), negative and increasing on \(\left(0,\infty\right)\), with \(u\left(0\right)=-\infty\).

Proposition 5 (selection against collapse) is stated in Section 3.7 and proved in full in O.4 of this appendix. We use it as the paper states it, and only this part of it: “Let \(\bar R>0\) and let \(u\) be CRRA with elasticity \(\eta\). […] (ii) Where (R) fails and some feasible path has a finite value, every optimal path has a finite value and an infinite operating set.” Value and optimal path are read in it as defined in From Section 2 of the paper.

From Section 4 and Appendix C. A unit of the final good put to the current use \(j\) costs the user price \(P^j\equiv1+\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega\omega^j\), with \(\tau^W\) the gate fee and \(z\) the material-content price; \(p^R\) is the price of a tonne of raw material, \(r^K\) the rental of capital, \(\tau^P\) the emission tax, and \(\tilde p\equiv p^R+d^W\tau^P\) the waste sector’s value of a recovered tonne (Section 4.1). Section 4.2: “Under open access the waste stock has no owner: dumping is free, \(\tau^W=0\), and with \(\tau^P=z=0\) every user price is one, a recovered tonne sells at \(p^R=F_R\) and capital rents at \(r^K=F_K\); since nobody values the stock, the waste sector chooses its treated share and recycling capital against its operating profit alone.” Appendix C.1 writes that operating profit as \(\Pi^W\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\equiv\tilde p\,\mathcal R\left(\varpi H,K^R\right)+\tau^P\left(1-d^W\right)\varpi H-P^W\left[c^c\varpi+c^T\left(\varpi\right)\right]H-r^KK^R-\tau^PH\). At open access’s prices, \(\tilde p=p^R\), \(\tau^P=0\) and \(P^W=1\), so at date \(t\) \[ \Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)=p^R_t\,\mathcal R\left(\varpi H_t,K^R,t\right)-\left[c^c\varpi+c^T\left(\varpi\right)\right]H_t-r^K_tK^R . \tag{$\Pi^W$} \] The final-goods sector “rents \(K^Y\), buys \(R\) tonnes and sells \(Y\). The \(\Omega^YY\) tonnes that diffuse in production go to the waste sector at the fee, and the rest leaves inside the good and sells at \(z\)” (Section 4.1). The fee and \(z\) are zero at open access, so there its profit at date \(t\) is \[ \Pi^Y_t\left(K,R\right)=F\left(K,R,P_t,t\right)-p^R_tR-r^K_tK . \tag{$\Pi^Y$} \] Definition 2 has the sector either operate, “choosing \(\left(K^Y,R\right)\) at a maximum of its profit over \(\left[0,\infty\right)^2\)”, or be idle, “\(Y=R=0\), with \(r^K=0\)”.

Appendix C.9 defines the class of paths the theorem speaks of: “An open-access path is a feasible allocation with prices that takes every generated tonne into the waste stock at a zero fee, has the waste sector maximising its operating profit at open access’s prices, and has the final-goods sector either operating at a global maximum of its profit, hence participating, or idle; nothing is required of the household or the mine”. We read the definition as three conditions on a path with prices \(\left(p^R_t,r^K_t\right)_{t\ge0}\), at every date \(t\):

  • (OA1) the allocation is a feasible path, its waste-stock transition taking every generated tonne into \(\mathcal W\), at a zero fee;
  • (OA2) the waste sector’s pair \(\left(\varpi_t,K^R_t\right)\) maximises (\(\Pi^W\)) over \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,\infty\right)\);
  • (OA3) either the date is idle, \(Y_t=R_t=0\), or the final-goods sector operates: \(\left(K^Y_t,R_t\right)\) maximises (\(\Pi^Y\)) over \(\left[0,\infty\right)^2\).

The proof uses no price at an idle date, so it covers the reading of idle with \(r^K=0\) as well. At an operating date it derives open access’s prices, \(p^R=F_R\) and \(r^K=F_K\), and participation, from (OA3).

Appendix C.9 also defines the remaining budget: “The remaining budget is the virgin material still to be extracted, \(S_t+\bar X_{\max}-X_t\), plus the two anthropogenic stocks; it falls each period by exactly the leakage flow and, once below \(\bar R/\left(\bar a\mu\right)\), leaves the economy only finitely many operating periods.” We write it \[ \mathcal B_t\equiv S_t+\bar X_{\max}-X_t+M^K_t+\mathcal W_t , \tag{B} \] so that \(\mathcal B_0=\mathcal B\). The leakage flow is the left side of the collected ledger of Section 2.3, \(\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t=H_t-R^R_t\). Lemma B proves the two claims of the definition.

The remaining budget

Lemma B. On every feasible path:

(a) \(\mathcal B_{t+1}=\mathcal B_t-\left(H_t-R^R_t\right)\) with \(H_t-R^R_t\ge0\), so the remaining budget is nonincreasing; and \(0\le\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal B_t\);

(b) if \(\bar a\mu\mathcal B_{t_1}<\bar R\) at some date \(t_1\), then at most \(\left(S_{t_1}+\bar X_{\max}-X_{t_1}\right)/\left(\bar R-\bar a\mu\mathcal B_{t_1}\right)\) dates \(t\ge t_1\) have \(R_t\ge\bar R\), and the operating set is finite. Equivalently, a feasible path with an infinite operating set has \(\bar a\mu\mathcal B_t\ge\bar R\) at every date.

Proof.

  1. The waste-stock transition and the ledger give \(\mathcal W_{t+1}-\mathcal W_t=W_t-H_t=R_t-\left(M^K_{t+1}-M^K_t\right)-H_t\), and the transitions of reserves and discovery give \(\left(S_{t+1}-X_{t+1}\right)-\left(S_t-X_t\right)=\left(D_t-N_t\right)-D_t=-N_t\). Adding the change in \(M^K\) and using \(R_t=N_t+R^R_t\), \(\mathcal B_{t+1}-\mathcal B_t=-N_t+R_t-H_t=R^R_t-H_t\), which is nonpositive as \(R^R_t\le\bar aT_t\le H_t\). The mass restrictions make \(S_t\), \(\bar X_{\max}-X_t\) and \(M^K_t\) nonnegative, so \(\mathcal B_t\ge\mathcal W_t\ge0\).

  2. Let \(t\ge t_1\). The throughput cap and (a) give \(R_t=N_t+R^R_t\le N_t+\bar a\mu\mathcal W_t\le N_t+\bar a\mu\mathcal B_t\le N_t+\bar a\mu\mathcal B_{t_1}\), so at a date with \(R_t\ge\bar R\) extraction is at least the positive gap \(\bar R-\bar a\mu\mathcal B_{t_1}\). Summing the transitions of reserves and discovery from \(t_1\) to any \(n\ge t_1\) gives \(\sum_{t=t_1}^{n}N_t=S_{t_1}-S_{n+1}+X_{n+1}-X_{t_1}\le S_{t_1}+\bar X_{\max}-X_{t_1}\), by \(S_{n+1}\ge0\) and \(X_{n+1}\le\bar X_{\max}\). As \(N_t\ge0\), the gap times the number of dates \(t\ge t_1\) with \(R_t\ge\bar R\) is at most this sum, which is the bound; finitely many dates precede \(t_1\). The second sentence is the contrapositive of the first.

The economy

We prove the theorem for the primitives and initial stocks that follow. Every primitive is constant in time except the two trends, and the constants left open in the list are fixed in The constants that separate the two economies.

  • Technology. \(\beta_K,\gamma>0\) with \(\mu_F\equiv\beta_K+\gamma<1\); \(g_A,g_B\ge0\) with \(g_A+g_B>0\), and \(B_0=1\); for \(R\ge\bar R\) the workhorse’s output \(\mathcal F=A_te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR^e\right)^{\gamma}\), so that \(A_tB_t^{\gamma}=A_0\Gamma^t\), with \(\kappa>0\) and \(A_0>0\) left open.
  • Floor and yield. \(\bar R>0\), and the exponential yield at \(\bar a=1\), \(a\left(x\right)=1-e^{-\xi x}\) with \(\xi>0\).
  • Waste and handling. \(\mu\in\left(\mu_F,1\right)\); \(d^W\in\left(0,1\right)\); any treatment cost \(c^T\) with the properties of Section 2, for instance \(c^T\left(\varpi\right)=\varpi^2\); and a collection charge \(c^c>0\) left open.
  • Materials accounting. \(\phi^I=0\); \(\omega^j\ge0\) for every use, with \(\omega^C>0\); any \(\Omega^{N,S},\Omega^{D,S}\ge0\).
  • Pollution. Any decay rate \(\theta\) with the properties of Section 2, for instance \(\theta\left(P\right)=\left(1+e^{-P}\right)/4\).
  • Extraction and exploration. The workhorse’s extraction cost and any exploration cost with the properties of Section 2, each zero at a zero flow for every \(S\ge0\) and \(X\le\bar X_{\max}\). By Lemma Z they enter only there.
  • Preferences. \(\delta\in\left(0,1\right)\); CRRA utility with \(\eta>1\); \(\rho\in\left(0,\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}-1\right]\), an interval that is not empty as \(\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}>1\), so that (R) fails; and the workhorse’s disutility \(v\left(P\right)=\psi_vP^{1+\varphi}/\left(1+\varphi\right)\) with \(\psi_v,\varphi>0\), or any \(v\) with the properties of Section 2 that is real-valued on \(P\ge0\).
  • Initial stocks. \(S_0=0\), \(X_0=\bar X_{\max}\), \(M^K_0=0\), \(\mathcal W_0=\bar R/\left[\mu\left(1-\mu_F\right)\right]\), \(P_0>0\), and \(K_0\) left open.

With \(\bar R>0\) and \(\bar a=1\), the economy is in the floor and soft-ceiling cell. Its output function has the signs of Section 2.1 wherever \(K^Y,R^e>0\), \(\mathcal F_P=-\kappa\mathcal F<0\) among them. It is concave in \(\left(K^Y,R^e\right)\), its Hessian at \(K^Y,R^e>0\) having a negative diagonal and a determinant proportional to \(\beta_K\gamma\left(1-\beta_K-\gamma\right)>0\), and \(\mathcal F_K=\beta_K\mathcal F/K^Y\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\) at any \(R^e>0\): these are the two clauses Section 2.1 states after the output function. Where \(K^Y,R^e>0\), \(F_K=\beta_KY/K^Y\) and \(F_R=\gamma Y/R^e\). Pollution is positive at every date of every feasible path, as \(P_{t+1}\ge\left[1-\theta\left(P_t\right)\right]P_t\) and \(P_0>0\), and at a positive stock the workhorse’s \(v\) has \(v'>0\) and \(v''>0\). Every \(v\) admitted is continuous and nondecreasing on \(P\ge0\).

The constants that separate the two economies. Write \(\bar P\equiv P_0+\mathcal W_0\), and take \(\kappa>0\) so small that \[ s\;\equiv\;\left(\frac{\gamma}{\mu_F}\right)^{\gamma}e^{\kappa\bar P}\;<\;1 , \tag{S} \] which is possible because \(\beta_K>0\) makes \(\mu_F>\gamma\). Then choose, in this order:

  1. \(x_0\ge1/\xi\) so large that \(b\equiv\mu e^{-\xi x_0}/\left(1-\Gamma^{-1}\right)<1\), that \(\underline R\equiv\left(1-e^{-\xi x_0}\right)\left(1-b\right)\mu\mathcal W_0>\bar R\), and that \(\left[\left(1-\mu_F\right)\left(\underline R-\bar R\right)/\left(\mu_F\bar R\right)\right]^{\gamma}>\sqrt s\). This is possible: as \(x_0\to\infty\), \(b\to0\) and \(\underline R\to\mu\mathcal W_0=\bar R/\left(1-\mu_F\right)\), so the bracket tends to one, while \(\sqrt s<1\).
  2. \(k>0\) so large that \(\left[k/\left(k+x_0\mu\mathcal W_0\right)\right]^{\beta_K}>\sqrt s\), which is possible as the ratio tends to one, and \(K_0\equiv k+x_0\mu\mathcal W_0\).
  3. \(A_0\), given the rest. Write \[ \underline Y\equiv A_0e^{-\kappa\bar P}\left(\underline R-\bar R\right)^{\gamma}k^{\beta_K}, \qquad \bar p^R\equiv\gamma A_0K_0^{\beta_K}\left(\frac{\gamma\bar R}{1-\mu_F}\right)^{\gamma-1}, \] both proportional to \(A_0\). Substituting \(\mu\mathcal W_0=\bar R/\left(1-\mu_F\right)\) and collecting powers of \(\gamma\bar R\), then using (S), \[ \frac{\underline Y}{\mu\mathcal W_0\,\bar p^R} =e^{-\kappa\bar P}\left[\frac{\left(1-\mu_F\right)\left(\underline R-\bar R\right)}{\gamma\bar R}\right]^{\gamma}\left(\frac{k}{K_0}\right)^{\beta_K} =\frac1s\left[\frac{\left(1-\mu_F\right)\left(\underline R-\bar R\right)}{\mu_F\bar R}\right]^{\gamma}\left(\frac{k}{K_0}\right)^{\beta_K} \;>\;\frac{\sqrt s\,\sqrt s}{s}=1 , \] by steps 1 and 2. So \(\underline Y-\mu\mathcal W_0\bar p^R\) is positive and proportional to \(A_0\), and we take \(A_0\) so large that \[ \underline Y-\mu\mathcal W_0\,\bar p^R\;>\;\delta k+\mu\mathcal W_0\left(\delta x_0+\frac{\ln\Gamma}{\xi}\right)+c^T\left(1\right)\mu\mathcal W_0 . \]
  4. \(c^c\equiv\bar p^R\), which is positive.

By steps 3 and 4, \[ \underline C\;\equiv\;\underline Y-\delta k-\mu\mathcal W_0\left(\delta x_0+\frac{\ln\Gamma}{\xi}\right)-\left[c^c+c^T\left(1\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W_0\;>\;0 . \] So the economy is in the floor and soft-ceiling cell and in the workhorse family, with the exponential yield at \(\bar a=1\), \(\phi^I=0\), \(\eta>1\) and (R) failing, and with \(\kappa>0\), so that output falls with pollution as Section 2.1 requires.

Proof of Theorem 1

Take the economy of The economy and let \(t^*\equiv1\). It is one of the economies (E) of the continuation page: above the floor its output is \(A_0\Gamma^te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(R^e\right)^{\gamma}\), as \(A_tB_t^{\gamma}=A_0\Gamma^t\), and its other primitives and initial stocks are among those (E) admits. So Lemma Z applies to its feasible paths: none extracts or explores, \(S_t=0\), \(X_t=\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t=0\) at every date, so the remaining budget (B) is the waste stock, \(\mathcal B_t=\mathcal W_t\).

Proof. Step 1: a feasible path has a finite value. The constants \(x_0\ge1/\xi\) and \(k>0\) of The economy, with \(K_0=k+x_0\mu\mathcal W_0\), \(b<1\), \(\underline R>\bar R\) and \(\underline C>0\), meet the hypotheses of Lemma W. So the economy has a feasible path with \(C_t\ge\underline C>0\) at every date, and its value is finite.

Step 2: the planner’s problem has an optimal path. By step 1 and Lemma E.

Step 3: every optimal path has a finite value and an infinite operating set, so is not in state A. Here \(\bar R>0\), \(u\) is CRRA with elasticity \(\eta>1\), (R) fails, and by step 1 some feasible path has a finite value. Part (ii) of Proposition 5 gives the two claims, and a path in state A has a finite operating set.

Step 4: every optimal path keeps \(\mu\mathcal B_t\ge\bar R\) at every date. Its operating set is infinite by step 3, so Lemma B(b), at \(\bar a=1\), gives \(\mu\mathcal B_t\ge\bar R\) at every date.

Step 5: an open-access path recycles nothing before \(t^*\). Take an open-access path and suppose \(R^R_0>0\). By Lemma Z, \(N_0=0\), so \(R_0=R^R_0>0\): the date is not idle, and by (OA3) \(\left(K^Y_0,R_0\right)\) maximises \(\Pi^Y_0\) over \(\left[0,\infty\right)^2\).

Both prices are positive. If \(r^K_0\le0\), then \(\Pi^Y_0\left(K,\bar R+1\right)\ge A_0e^{-\kappa P_0}K^{\beta_K}-p^R_0\left(\bar R+1\right)\), which grows without bound in \(K\) as \(\beta_K>0\). If \(p^R_0\le0\), then \(\Pi^Y_0\left(1,R\right)\ge A_0e^{-\kappa P_0}\left(R-\bar R\right)^{\gamma}-r^K_0\), which grows without bound in \(R\) as \(\gamma>0\). Either way \(\Pi^Y_0\) would have no maximum.

The sector produces and participates. The choice \(\left(0,0\right)\) gives \(\Pi^Y_0\left(0,0\right)=0\), output being zero below the floor, so \(\Pi^Y_0\left(K^Y_0,R_0\right)\ge0\). Were \(Y_0=0\), this profit would be \(-p^R_0R_0-r^K_0K^Y_0<0\). So \(Y_0>0\), which in the workhorse needs \(K^Y_0>0\) and \(R^e_0=R_0-\bar R>0\).

Open access’s prices. \(\Pi^Y_0\) is differentiable on the open set where \(K>0\) and \(R>\bar R\), and its maximiser lies there, so its partial derivatives vanish: \(r^K_0=F_K=\beta_KY_0/K^Y_0\) and \(p^R_0=F_R=\gamma Y_0/R^e_0\).

Participation caps the price of material. At these prices \[ 0\;\le\;\Pi^Y_0\left(K^Y_0,R_0\right)=Y_0-\frac{\gamma Y_0}{R^e_0}\left(R^e_0+\bar R\right)-\beta_KY_0=Y_0\left[\left(1-\mu_F\right)-\frac{\gamma\bar R}{R^e_0}\right], \] so \(R^e_0\ge\gamma\bar R/\left(1-\mu_F\right)\). As \(\kappa P_0\ge0\), \(K^Y_0\le K_0\) and \(\gamma<1\), \[ p^R_0=\gamma A_0e^{-\kappa P_0}\left(K^Y_0\right)^{\beta_K}\left(R^e_0\right)^{\gamma-1}\;\le\;\gamma A_0K_0^{\beta_K}\left(\frac{\gamma\bar R}{1-\mu_F}\right)^{\gamma-1}=\bar p^R . \]

The recycler cannot cover the collection charge. As \(R^R_0>0\) and nothing is recovered without a treated flow, \(T_0=\varpi_0H_0>0\), and \(R^R_0=\left(1-e^{-\xi x}\right)T_0<T_0\) with \(x=K^R_0/T_0\). By (OA2) the pair does at least as well as \(\left(0,0\right)\), at which (\(\Pi^W\)) is zero. With \(p^R_0>0\), \(c^T\ge0\) and \(r^K_0K^R_0\ge0\), \[ 0\;\le\;\Pi^W_0\left(\varpi_0,K^R_0\right)=p^R_0R^R_0-c^cT_0-c^T\left(\varpi_0\right)H_0-r^K_0K^R_0\;<\;\left(p^R_0-c^c\right)T_0 , \] so \(c^c<p^R_0\le\bar p^R=c^c\), a contradiction. Hence \(R^R_0=0\): the path recycles nothing at date \(0\), the one date before \(t^*\). The inequality \(c^c<p^R_0\) is the necessary condition in item 2 of Proposition 9 of the paper, that the open-access economy treats only if \(\bar aF_R>c^c\), reached here from the operating profit alone.

Step 6: from \(t^*\) on the remaining budget stays below \(\bar R/\mu\). As \(\mathcal B_t=\mathcal W_t\) and \(R^R_0=0\), Lemma B(a) gives \(\mathcal B_1=\mathcal B_0-H_0=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_0\). So \[ \mu\mathcal B_1=\left(1-\mu\right)\mu\mathcal W_0=\frac{1-\mu}{1-\mu_F}\,\bar R\;<\;\bar R , \] as \(\mu>\mu_F\), and by Lemma B(a), \(\mathcal B_t\le\mathcal B_1<\bar R/\mu\) at every date \(t\ge t^*\).

Step 7: the open-access path is in state A. At date \(0\), \(R_0=N_0+R^R_0=0<\bar R\). At every date \(t\ge1\), \(N_t=0\), so the throughput cap and step 6 give \(R_t=R^R_t\le\mu\mathcal W_t=\mu\mathcal B_t<\bar R\). No date operates: the operating set is empty, hence finite, and the path is in state A.

What the open-access part uses. Steps 5 to 7 use the constraints of Problem 1, the conditions (OA2) and (OA3), the output function, the yield, the collection and treatment charges and the initial stocks, and of extraction and exploration only that the initial stocks leave nothing to extract or discover, by Lemma Z(a). They use nothing about \(u\), \(v\), \(\rho\) or \(\eta\), and no condition on the household’s or the mine’s choices: the open-access part holds for every preference pair.

Three further facts show how the economy works; the theorem does not use them. First, the class of open-access paths is not empty. The path with, at every date, \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=0\), \(K^R_t=0\), \(\Omega_t=0\), \(K_{t+1}=\tfrac12\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\), \(C_t=\tfrac12\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\) and \(p^R_t=r^K_t=0\), the states following the transitions, is one. Its material input, output and cost flows are zero, so \(C_t+K_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)K_t=0\) is its goods constraint, \(W_t=0\) its ledger and \(\Omega_t=0\) its intensity condition; every date is idle; and at zero prices (\(\Pi^W\)) is at most zero, its value at \(\left(0,0\right)\). Second, every open-access path of the economy is idle at every date: a date with \(R_t>0\) operates, and the argument of step 5 then gives \(R_t>\bar R\), which step 7 excludes. Third, the open-access economy fails for want of a recycler, not of a producer. By the choice of \(x_0\), \(\left(1-\mu_F\right)\left(\underline R-\bar R\right)/\left(\mu_F\bar R\right)>s^{1/\left(2\gamma\right)}\ge\left(\gamma/\mu_F\right)^{1/2}>\gamma/\mu_F\), so along the path of Lemma W, \(R^e_t\ge\underline R-\bar R>\gamma\bar R/\left(1-\mu_F\right)\): a final-goods sector paid its marginal products there would participate, by the computation of step 5. Yet at date \(0\) no recycler covers the collection charge that this path pays on every handled tonne.
