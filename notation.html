Notation

This page collects the paper’s symbols, with their meanings and units, and the rules by which they are formed. It is to be consulted, not read: each symbol is defined in the paper where it is introduced, and the pages of this appendix use it with that meaning.

Rules

  1. A superscript names a use or a process; a subscript names time. \(K^Y\) is capital in production, \(K^R\) capital in recycling, \(C^N\) the cost of extraction, \(W^Y\) the waste from production. The period subscript is suppressed wherever timing carries no content and written wherever it does, which in practice means the transition equations. The exogenous time argument of a primitive (\(F\), \(a\), \(C^N\), \(C^D\), \(c^c\), \(c^T\)) is suppressed on the same terms.
  2. A stock is dated at the beginning of its period; a flow occurs during it. A transition equation therefore states what the period-\(t\) flows leave behind for \(t+1\). This is capital-style dating and it fixes, once, which period a costate attaches to: the period in which the transition it prices is chosen.
  3. A calligraphic capital is a function, a base or an aggregate, never a quantity of the same kind as its Roman counterpart. \(\mathcal F\) is the technology whose value is \(Y\); \(\mathcal R\) is the recycling technology whose value is \(R^R\); \(\mathcal W\) is the waste stock whose per-period outflow is \(H\); \(\mathcal D\) is the base of the intensity condition and \(\mathcal N\) the displaced mass; \(\mathcal G^{\varpi}\) and \(\mathcal G^{K}\) are the two recycling margins.
  4. \(\lambda\) is the multiplier on the goods constraint, in utils per final good, and every shadow price is stated net of it. Dividing by \(\lambda\) is what puts \(p^S\), \(p^X\), \(p^P\), \(p^{\mathcal W}\), \(p^M\), \(p^W\), \(\zeta\) and \(\Psi\) into final goods per tonne. Sign conventions are carried in the definitions, not in the symbols.
  5. \(\Omega\) is the material intensity of the final good and \(\omega^j\) the relative intensity of use \(j\), so that \(\Omega^j=\Omega\omega^j\) carries units and \(\omega^j\) is a pure number. The same split applies to the durables coefficient: \(\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I\). The nature-attributable intensities \(\Omega^{N,S}\) and \(\Omega^{D,S}\) convert tonnage to tonnage and are pure numbers despite the capital letter, because the mass they carry never passes through \(Y\). The two families are not comparable.
  6. A bar is a bound, and it is the same kind of object in every case. \(\bar R\) the material floor, \(\bar a\) the yield ceiling, \(\bar X_{\max}\) the exhaustion point of discovery.
  7. A per-period rate lies in \((0,1)\): \(\delta\), \(\theta\), \(d^W\) and the handling share \(\mu\), whose limit \(\mu=1\) is the one-period buffer \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=W_t\). An elasticity and a discount rate do not, and never take those letters.
  8. Growth is stated through positive gross factors. A variable grows at rate \(g\) when \(Z_{t+1}/Z_t\to1+g\) with \(1+g>0\), and decays at rate \(\nu\in(0,1)\) when \(Z_{t+1}/Z_t\to1-\nu\), so a decay rate is a share lost per period, like \(\delta\) and \(\mu\), and a growth factor of a product is the product of the factors. A variable whose ratio \(Z_{t+1}/Z_t\) tends to zero falls faster than at every geometric rate and has no rate. Where growth factors must be solved for, the equations are linear in their logarithms, and the solution is reported back as a factor.
  9. One symbol, one meaning. A symbol on this page has the meaning given here wherever the paper and this appendix use it; a symbol the paper needs and this page does not carry is defined where the paper introduces it.
