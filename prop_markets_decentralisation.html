O.9 The planner’s allocation as a competitive equilibrium

Proposition 6 of the paper (decentralisation), in Section 4.3, states that where the planner’s economy operates in every period and its final-goods sector breaks even at the supporting prices, the planner’s allocation is a competitive equilibrium once the waste stock is owned and an emission tax, a discovery tax and a material-content price are set at the planner’s shadow prices. This page proves it in full. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

The statement

Proposition 6 (decentralisation). Let a path solve Problem 1 with multipliers \(\left\{\lambda,\zeta,p^W\right\}\) and costates (CS), and suppose that in every period the planner operates, \(R_t\ge\bar R\), with \(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t>0\), and that the final-goods sector participates at the prices (SP), \[ \left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\left(Y-F_RR-F_KK^Y\right)\Big|_t\;\ge\;0 , \tag{PA} \] which for the workhorse of Section 3.6, with output elasticities \(\beta_K\) and \(\gamma\) summing to \(\mu_F\), reads \(R^e_t\ge\gamma\bar R/\left(1-\mu_F\right)\). Suppose the waste stock is owned, displaced mass is emitted by the mining sector and charged the emission tax as part of \(\Xi\), and the instruments are \[ \tau^P_t = p^P_t, \qquad \tau^X_t = -p^X_t\;\ge\;0, \qquad z_t = \zeta_t-p^W_t . \tag{IN} \] Then the planner’s allocation is a competitive equilibrium of Definition 2, supported by \[ \tau^W_t = p^W_t=-p^{\mathcal W}_t, \qquad p^R_t = \Psi_t-p^W_t, \qquad r^K_t = F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\Big|_t, \tag{SP} \] with the private valuations \(\left(q,p^S,p^M,p^{\mathcal W}\right)\) equal to the planner’s costates, the household’s multiplier equal to \(\lambda\) and the same interest rate. Under unified ownership of the exploration frontier \(\tau^X\equiv0\), and \(p^X\) arises inside the mining sector.

The costates (CS), the planner’s first-order conditions, the market economy and Definition 2 are restated in What the proof uses.

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions: we substitute (IN) and (SP) into each agent’s problem and show that the planner’s choices solve it, each market condition becoming a condition of the planner’s, and that the markets clear because the planner’s constraints hold. No functional form is used except in step 8, the workhorse’s threshold.

What the proof uses

From the paper’s model (Section 2). Problem 1 chooses \(C,I,N,D,\varpi,K^R,W,\Omega\) subject to the goods constraint, the ledger, the intensity condition \(R=\Omega\left(\mathcal D+\phi^IG\right)\) and the transitions, with \(K^Y=K-K^R\), \(R=N+R^R\), \(R^R=\mathcal R\left(\varpi H,K^R\right)\), \(H=\mu\mathcal W\), \(G=I+\delta K\), \(C^W=c^W\left(\varpi\right)H\) with \(c^W\left(\varpi\right)\equiv c^c\varpi+c^T\left(\varpi\right)\), and \(\Xi=\Xi^h+\mathcal N\). Appendix A.2 writes the costates of the six transitions as \[ \underbrace{\lambda q}_{K},\quad \underbrace{\lambda p^S}_{S},\quad \underbrace{\lambda p^X}_{X},\quad \underbrace{-\lambda p^P}_{P},\quad \underbrace{-\lambda p^M}_{M^K},\quad \underbrace{\lambda p^{\mathcal W}}_{\mathcal W}, \tag{CS} \] each attached in the period in which the transition it prices is chosen, with \(\lambda\) the multiplier on the goods constraint, \(\lambda p^W\) that on the ledger written with \(W\) on the left, and \(\lambda\zeta\) that on the intensity condition written with \(R\) on the left. Of the primitives we use: \(C^N_{NN}\ge0\) and \(C^D_{DD}\ge0\), so each cost is convex in its flow; \(C^D_X>0\) where \(D>0\) and \(C^D\left(0,X,t\right)=0\), so \(C^D_X\ge0\), with \(C^D_X=0\) at \(D=0\); \(c^{T\prime\prime}>0\), so \(c^W\) is convex; \(\mathcal F_K>0\) and \(\mathcal F_{R^e}>0\), “with \(\mathcal F\) concave in \(\left(K^Y,R^e\right)\) at each \(\left(P,t\right)\) and the Inada condition in capital, \(\mathcal F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\) at any \(R^e>0\), maintained”, two clauses Section 2.1 states with the output equation; and the yield \(a\), increasing and concave with \(a\left(0\right)=0\) and \(a<\bar a\), with \(\mathcal R\left(T,K^R\right)=a\left(K^R/T\right)T\) at \(T>0\) and \(\mathcal R\left(0,K^R\right)=0\), a treated flow of zero recovering nothing.

From the paper’s Appendix A: what the hypothesis provides. We read the hypothesis as Appendix A reads a solution with its multipliers and costates, and as the page of shared lemmas reads a candidate: at every date the first-order system of Appendix A holds, with its wedge conditions \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y>0\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^j>0\) for every use \(j\) maintained, and for each stock \(Z\) with costate \(m\) in (CS) the transversality condition \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta^t\lambda_tm_tZ_{t+1}=0\) holds. The system uses \(\Psi\equiv F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)+\zeta\), the storage share \(\sigma\equiv\Phi^IG/R\), the discount rate \(1+r_{t+1}\equiv\lambda_t/\left(\beta\lambda_{t+1}\right)\), the material-adjusted marginal product of capital \(\tilde F_K\equiv F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\), where “\(F_R\) is \(\mathcal F_{R^e}\) at the prevailing \(R\ge\bar R\)”, and the gains (G), \(\mathcal G^K\equiv\Psi-p^W+d^Wp^P\) and \(\mathcal G^\varpi\equiv\alpha\mathcal G^K+\left(1-d^W\right)p^P\). In Appendix A’s order:

  • the four unbounded controls: \(\zeta=\sigma\left(p^W-p^M\right)\), condition (Ω); \(u'\left(C\right)=\lambda\left(1+\zeta\Omega^C\right)\), condition (C); \(q=1-\Phi^I\left(1-\sigma\right)\left(p^W-p^M\right)\), condition (I); and \(p^W_t=-p^{\mathcal W}_t\), condition (W);
  • extraction and exploration, as branch systems, \[ \Psi=C^N_N\left(1+\zeta\Omega^N\right)+p^S+p^W+p^P\Omega^{N,S}\ \text{ where }N>0, \qquad p^S+p^X=C^D_D\left(1+\zeta\Omega^D\right)+p^P\Omega^{D,S}\ \text{ where }D>0, \tag{ND} \] of which Appendix A says that “each corner holds when its inequality goes the other way”: where \(N=0\) the right side of the first, at \(N=0\), is at least \(\Psi\), and where \(D=0\) the right side of the second, at \(D=0\), is at least \(p^S+p^X\);
  • the conditions on the treated share and recycling capital, which we read together with Appendix A’s remarks on the pair;
  • the costate recursions \[ \begin{aligned} \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)q_t&=\tilde F_{K,t+1}+\left(1-\delta\right)q_{t+1}, & \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^S_t&=\left|C^N_S\right|\left(1+\zeta\Omega^N\right)\big|_{t+1}+p^S_{t+1}, \\ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^X_t&=-C^D_X\left(1+\zeta\Omega^D\right)\big|_{t+1}+p^X_{t+1}, & \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^M_t&=\delta\,p^W_{t+1}+\left(1-\delta\right)p^M_{t+1}, \\ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^{\mathcal W}_t&=\mu\,h_{t+1}+\left(1-\mu\right)p^{\mathcal W}_{t+1}, & h&\equiv\alpha\varpi\left(\Psi-p^W\right)-c^W\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)-p^P\left[1-\varpi\left(1-d^W\right)-d^W\alpha\varpi\right]. \end{aligned} \tag{CR} \]

Appendix A states the conditions on the pair \(\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\), (T) and (KR) where the pair is interior, as branch systems, notes that “The upper bound \(K^R\le K\) never binds, since \(F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\)”, and adds that “At \(\varpi=0\) the ratio \(x=K^R/\left(\varpi H\right)\) is undefined, so entry into treatment and into recycling capital is one joint decision, tested over the best available pair \(\left(\varpi,x\right)\).” We read these first-order conditions as one requirement on the pair: at the planner’s pair \(\left(\varpi_t,K^R_t\right)\), with every other control and every multiplier at the planner’s values, the one-sided derivative of the braces of the shared page, \[ B_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\equiv Y-C^W-p^W_tR+\zeta_t\left[R-\Omega_t\left(\mathcal D+\phi^IG_t\right)\right]-p^P_t\Xi , \] is nonpositive along every direction \(\left(d\varpi,dK^R\right)\) that keeps the pair in \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,K_t\right]\) for small steps, \(\mathcal F\) being differentiable at the date’s input, as the shared page’s derivatives presume. In \(B_t\), \(Y=F\left(K_t-K^R,N_t+\mathcal R\left(\varpi H_t,K^R\right),P_t,t\right)\), and \(C^W\), \(R\), \(\mathcal D\) and \(\Xi\) move with the pair through \(C^W\), \(R^R\) and \(Y\). Where the pair is interior the requirement is (KR) and (T), at a bound the inequality of its branch, and at \(\varpi=0\) the joint test. By (C), \(u'>0\) and the wedge condition \(1+\zeta\Omega^C>0\), \(\lambda_t>0\) at every date.

Production capital is positive. At every date \(K^Y_t>0\), so \(K^R_t<K_t\), as Appendix A’s sentence on the upper bound states. Section 2’s Inada clause is its reason: \(\tilde F_{K,t}\) is a real number in (CR) and (KR), while \(\mathcal F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\) at any \(R^e>0\).

From the paper’s market economy (Section 4.1 and Appendix C). Four price-taking agents with perfect foresight share the technology, preferences and accounting of Section 2: a household, which consumes, owns the capital stock and rents it out, owns the three sectors and receives all profits and net tax revenue as lump sums; a final-goods sector; a mining sector that owns the reserve \(S\); and a waste sector that owns the waste stock \(\mathcal W\). The final good is the numeraire. Raw material sells at \(p^R\) per tonne, one price for a secondary and a virgin tonne; capital rents at \(r^K\); handing over a tonne of waste costs the gate fee \(\tau^W\); and the matter embodied in the final good is priced at \(z\) per tonne, the material-content price. A unit of the final good put to a current use \(j\in\left\{Y,N,D,W,C\right\}\) releases \(\Omega\omega^j\) tonnes, on which its buyer pays the fee at once, and put to capital formation it stores \(\Phi^I\) tonnes, so the user prices are \[ P^j\equiv1+\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega\omega^j, \qquad P^I\equiv1+z\,\Phi^I . \tag{UP} \] The instruments are an emission tax \(\tau^P\) per tonne of \(\Xi\), levied on the agent that emits it, the waste sector on \(\Xi^h\) and the mining sector on the mass \(\mathcal N\) it displaces; a discovery tax \(\tau^X\) per tonne discovered; and \(z\), “with revenue returned as a lump sum”. Agents discount at the market interest rate \(r\) and take the paths of prices, instruments, \(P\) and \(\Omega\) as given; nobody chooses \(\Omega\).

  • The final-goods sector rents \(K^Y\), buys \(R\) tonnes and sells \(Y\); the \(\Omega^YY\) tonnes that diffuse in production go to the waste sector at the fee, and the rest leaves inside the good and sells at \(z\). Its profit is \[ \Pi^Y\equiv Y+z\left(R-\Omega^YY\right)-p^RR-\tau^W\Omega^YY-r^KK^Y=\left[1-\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega^Y\right]Y-\left(p^R-z\right)R-r^KK^Y , \] with the first-order conditions \(F_R\left[1-\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega^Y\right]=p^R-z\) and \(F_K\left[1-\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega^Y\right]=r^K\), together (FY).
  • The mining sector sells what it extracts at \(p^R\), buys extraction and exploration effort at \(P^N\) and \(P^D\), and pays the emission tax on the mass it displaces and the discovery tax, so its cash flow is \(\Pi^N\equiv p^RN-P^NC^N-P^DC^D-\tau^P\mathcal N-\tau^XD\). With \(p^S\) its valuation of a tonne left in the ground, at interior extraction and exploration \[ p^R=P^NC^N_N+\tau^P\Omega^{N,S}+p^S, \qquad p^S=P^DC^D_D+\tau^P\Omega^{D,S}+\tau^X, \qquad \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^S_t=\left|C^N_S\right|P^N\big|_{t+1}+p^S_{t+1}. \tag{M} \] Cumulative discovery has no owner, the common-pool reading, “so that \(p^X\) enters no private condition”; under unified ownership of the exploration frontier the sector owns \(X\) as well.
  • The waste sector takes in \(W\) at the fee, draws \(H=\mu\mathcal W\) from its stock, chooses \(\varpi\in\left[0,1\right]\) and \(K^R\ge0\), rented at \(r^K\), sells \(R^R\) at \(p^R\) and pays the emission tax on \(\Xi^h\). Its operating profit (Appendix C) is \[ \Pi^W\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\equiv p^R\mathcal R\left(\varpi H,K^R\right)-P^Wc^W\left(\varpi\right)H-r^KK^R-\tau^P\Xi^h, \qquad \Xi^h=\left(1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\right)H-d^W\mathcal R\left(\varpi H,K^R\right). \] Its value \(J^W_t\) “is the fee on what it accepts, plus its operating profit, plus the discounted value of the stock it carries into the next period”, with \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+W_t\). With \(p^{\mathcal W}_t\equiv\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)^{-1}\partial J^W_{t+1}/\partial\mathcal W_{t+1}\) its valuation of a tonne taken in at \(t\), acceptance at an interior quantity requires \(\tau^W_t=-p^{\mathcal W}_t\), condition (GF), and the valuation follows \[ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^{\mathcal W}_t=\mu\,h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t+1}+\left(1-\mu\right)p^{\mathcal W}_{t+1}, \qquad h^{\mathrm{mkt}}\equiv\alpha\varpi\,p^R-P^Wc^W-\tau^P\left[1-\varpi\left(1-d^W\right)-d^W\alpha\varpi\right]. \tag{WS} \] The value of a recovered tonne to the sector is \(\tilde p\equiv p^R+d^W\tau^P\).
  • The household (Appendix C) maximises \(U_0\) subject to, at every date, \[ P^C_tC_t+P^I_tG_t+\tau^W_t\delta M^K_t=r^K_tK_t+\Pi_t+T_t , \tag{HB} \] with \(G_t=I_t+\delta K_t\), the transitions of \(K\) and \(M^K\) of Section 2, \(\Pi_t\) the profits of the three sectors and \(T_t\) the lump sum. With \(\Lambda_t\) its multiplier on (HB), \(q_t\) the value of a unit of \(K_{t+1}\) in goods and \(p^M_t\) the cost of a tonne of \(M^K_{t+1}\), its conditions are \[ u'\left(C_t\right)=\Lambda_tP^C_t, \quad q_t=P^I_t+\Phi^I_tp^M_t, \quad 1+r_{t+1}\equiv\frac{\Lambda_t}{\beta\Lambda_{t+1}}, \quad \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)q_t=r^K_{t+1}+\left(1-\delta\right)q_{t+1}, \quad \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^M_t=\delta\,\tau^W_{t+1}+\left(1-\delta\right)p^M_{t+1}, \tag{H} \] “with the usual transversality conditions”.

Definition 2 (competitive equilibrium with instruments). “Given the initial stocks, instrument paths \(\left\{z_t,\tau^P_t,\tau^X_t\right\}\) and the rule that displaced mass is emitted by the mining sector as part of \(\Xi\), a competitive equilibrium is a set of quantity paths, price paths \(\left\{p^R,\tau^W,r^K,r\right\}\) and private valuations \(\left\{q,p^S,p^X,p^M,p^{\mathcal W}\right\}\) such that each agent solves its problem taking prices, \(P\) and \(\Omega\) as given; in each period the final-goods sector either operates, choosing \(\left(K^Y,R\right)\) at a maximum of its profit over \(\left[0,\infty\right)^2\), or is idle, \(Y=R=0\), with \(r^K=0\) and capital not rented held idle; the government budget balances period by period; the stocks follow” the transitions of Problem 1; “and in every period the markets for goods, capital, material, disposal and embodied material clear,” \[ Y=C+I+\delta K+C^N+C^D+C^W, \quad K^Y+K^R=K, \quad R=N+R^R, \quad W=\Omega\mathcal D+\delta M^K, \quad R-\Omega\omega^YY=\Omega\left(\omega^NC^N+\omega^DC^D+\omega^WC^W+\omega^CC\right)+\Phi^IG , \tag{MC} \] “capital clearing as \(K^Y+K^R\le K\) at an idle date.”

What it is for an agent to solve its problem. Definition 2 asks each agent to solve its problem at given prices and counts the private valuations among the objects of an equilibrium. The final-goods sector’s problem is static, and Definition 2 states it: a maximum of its profit over \(\left[0,\infty\right)^2\). The household, the mining sector and the waste sector each carry a stock into the next date, and Section 4.1 and Appendix C state their problems through their valuations of those stocks, in (M), in (GF) with (WS), and in (H). Section 4.1 says, in the sentence after Definition 2, what solving its problem means for these three: “For the household, the mining sector and the waste sector, which carry a stock from one date to the next, solving its problem means meeting the conditions that Section 4.1 and Appendix C state for it: at each date its choices attain the maximum of its payoff plus the stocks it carries forward at its own valuations, its valuations follow its asset equations, and each stock it carries meets its transversality condition.” We call that maximum, over the choices open to the agent at the date, its date problem; its asset equations are the last of (M), (WS) and the last two of (H). The payoff is the mining sector’s cash flow \(\Pi^N_t\), the waste sector’s \(\tau^W_tW_t+\Pi^W_t\), and the household’s \(u\left(C_t\right)\) under its budget (HB) at \(t\), its stocks valued at \(\Lambda_tq_t\) and \(-\Lambda_tp^M_t\). For the waste sector the date problem is the maximum that defines \(J^W_t\), with the stock it carries valued at \(p^{\mathcal W}_t\) a tonne. This is the sense of the paper’s text after Proposition 6, that the proof “finds a planner condition of Appendix A in each, so the proposition reaches as far as those conditions characterise the optimum”.

Lemma Q. The recovery \(\mathcal R\left(T,K\right)\), on \(T,K\ge0\), is concave, nondecreasing in \(K\), and \(0\le\mathcal R\left(T,K\right)\le\bar aT\).

Proof. Take \(\left(T_i,K_i\right)\), \(i=1,2\), and \(\vartheta\in\left(0,1\right)\), with \(T\equiv\vartheta T_1+\left(1-\vartheta\right)T_2\) and \(K\equiv\vartheta K_1+\left(1-\vartheta\right)K_2\). If \(T_1,T_2>0\), the weights \(w_1\equiv\vartheta T_1/T\) and \(w_2\equiv\left(1-\vartheta\right)T_2/T\) sum to one and \(K/T=w_1K_1/T_1+w_2K_2/T_2\), so concavity of \(a\) gives \(T\,a\left(K/T\right)\ge\vartheta T_1a\left(K_1/T_1\right)+\left(1-\vartheta\right)T_2a\left(K_2/T_2\right)\). If \(T_2=0<T_1\), then \(T=\vartheta T_1\) and \(K\ge\vartheta K_1\), and as \(a\) is increasing, \(\mathcal R\left(T,K\right)\ge\vartheta T_1a\left(K_1/T_1\right)=\vartheta\mathcal R\left(T_1,K_1\right)+\left(1-\vartheta\right)\mathcal R\left(T_2,K_2\right)\); the case \(T_1=0<T_2\) is the same, and if \(T_1=T_2=0\) both sides are zero. The rest holds because \(a\) is increasing with \(0=a\left(0\right)\le a<\bar a\).

Proof of Proposition 6

Proof. Step 1: the supporting prices. As \(\lambda_t>0\), the interest rate \(1+r_{t+1}=\lambda_t/\left(\beta\lambda_{t+1}\right)\) is positive and \(\beta^t\lambda_t>0\). Take the household’s multiplier \(\Lambda_t\equiv\lambda_t\), so its interest rate in (H) is the planner’s, and the private valuations \(q\), \(p^S\), \(p^X\), \(p^M\), \(p^{\mathcal W}\) equal to the costates. Under (IN) and (SP), \(z+\tau^W=\zeta\), so (UP) gives \[ P^j=1+\zeta\Omega^j\quad\left(j\in\left\{Y,N,D,W,C\right\}\right), \qquad P^I=1+\left(\zeta-p^W\right)\Phi^I , \] with \(P^N,P^D,P^W,P^C>0\) by the wedge conditions and \(1-\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega^Y=1-\zeta\Omega^Y>0\) by hypothesis. The sectors face \[ p^R-z=\Psi-\zeta=F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right), \qquad r^K=\tilde F_K, \qquad \tilde p=\Psi-p^W+d^Wp^P=\mathcal G^K, \qquad p^R-\tau^P\Omega^{N,S}=\Psi-p^W-p^P\Omega^{N,S}, \] the first two positive at every date, as the planner operates and \(\mathcal F_K,\mathcal F_{R^e}>0\).

Step 2: the discovery tax is nonnegative. Since \(\beta^t\lambda_t/\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)=\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}\), the recursion for \(p^X\) in (CR) reads \[ \beta^t\lambda_tp^X_t=\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}p^X_{t+1}-\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}C^D_X\left(1+\zeta\Omega^D\right)\big|_{t+1}\;\le\;\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}p^X_{t+1}, \] as \(C^D_X\ge0\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^D>0\), so \(\beta^t\lambda_tp^X_t\le\beta^s\lambda_sp^X_s\) for every \(s>t\). Cumulative discovery does not fall, \(X_{s+1}=X_s+D_s\ge X_s\). If \(X_{t'}>0\) at some date \(t'\), then \(X_{s+1}\ge X_{t'}>0\) for \(s\ge t'\), and the transversality condition \(\beta^s\lambda_sp^X_sX_{s+1}\to0\) gives \(\beta^s\lambda_sp^X_s\to0\). Hence \(\beta^t\lambda_tp^X_t\le0\), and \(\tau^X_t=-p^X_t\ge0\) at every date. If \(X_t=0\) at every date, nothing is ever discovered, every dividend in the recursion is zero and the transversality condition is silent, so \(p^X_t=\lambda_0p^X_0/\left(\beta^t\lambda_t\right)\) keeps the sign of \(p^X_0\), which the first-order system leaves free; the sign in (IN) is then what the statement supposes of the instrument. The steps that follow use \(\tau^X=-p^X\) and not its sign, so the equilibrium holds whichever sign \(p^X_0\) takes.

Step 3: the markets clear, the stocks follow their transitions, and the government’s and the household’s budgets hold. The allocation is the planner’s, so the goods constraint is the goods market of (MC), \(K^Y=K-K^R\) the capital market and \(R=N+R^R\) the material market. The ledger \(W_t=R_t-\Delta M^K_{t+1}\), the transition \(\Delta M^K_{t+1}=\Phi^I_tG_t-\delta M^K_t\) and the intensity condition give the disposal market, \(W=R-\Phi^IG+\delta M^K=\Omega\mathcal D+\delta M^K\), and the embodied-material market is the intensity condition with \(\Omega^YY=\Omega\omega^YY\) moved to the left. The stocks follow the transitions of Problem 1 because they are the planner’s, the flow \(\Xi=\Xi^h+\mathcal N\) being emitted by the waste sector and by the mining sector as Definition 2’s rule has it. Let the lump sum be the tax revenue, \[ T_t\equiv\tau^P_t\left(\Xi^h_t+\mathcal N_t\right)+\tau^X_tD_t . \] The material-content price is paid by the buyers of the good to the final-goods sector for the matter it sells inside the good, and the embodied-material market makes the tonnes paid for the tonnes sold, so it leaves the government nothing to return, whether or not its payments pass through it. The government budget balances. For (HB), let \(\Pi_t\equiv\Pi^Y_t+\Pi^N_t+\tau^W_tW_t+\Pi^W_t\). Collecting the cash flows with (UP), the emission and discovery taxes cancelling against \(T\), gives at every date \[ \begin{aligned} r^KK+\Pi+T-\left(P^CC+P^IG+\tau^W\delta M^K\right) ={}&\left(Y-C-G-C^N-C^D-C^W\right)+r^K\left(K-K^Y-K^R\right)+p^R\left(N+R^R-R\right) \\ &+z\left(R-\Omega\mathcal D-\Phi^IG\right)+\tau^W\left(W-\Omega\mathcal D-\delta M^K\right), \end{aligned} \] with \(\mathcal D=\omega^YY+\omega^NC^N+\omega^DC^D+\omega^WC^W+\omega^CC\). Each bracket is zero by (MC), the fourth being the embodied-material market. So (HB) holds along the planner’s allocation.

Step 4: the household solves its problem. By Step 1, (C) is \(u'\left(C_t\right)=\lambda_t\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^C_t\right)=\Lambda_tP^C_t\). By (Ω), \(\zeta-p^W+p^M=-\left(1-\sigma\right)\left(p^W-p^M\right)\), so (I) reads \(q=1+\left(\zeta-p^W\right)\Phi^I+\Phi^Ip^M=P^I+\Phi^Ip^M\). The recursions of \(q\) and \(p^M\) in (CR) are those of (H) at \(r^K=\tilde F_K\) and \(\tau^W=p^W\). So (H) holds with \(\Lambda=\lambda\), and the household’s transversality conditions, \(\beta^t\Lambda_tq_tK_{t+1}\to0\) and \(\beta^t\Lambda_tp^M_tM^K_{t+1}\to0\), are the planner’s for \(K\) and \(M^K\). For the date problem, take any \(\left(C,G\right)\) with \(C\ge0\) that meets (HB) at \(t\), with \(K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)K_t+G\) and \(M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t+\Phi^I_tG\). Using \(q_t-\Phi^I_tp^M_t=P^I_t\) and then (HB), \[ u\left(C\right)+\Lambda_t\left[q_tK_{t+1}-p^M_tM^K_{t+1}\right] =u\left(C\right)+\Lambda_tP^I_tG+\left(1-\delta\right)\Lambda_t\left[q_tK_t-p^M_tM^K_t\right] =u\left(C\right)-\Lambda_tP^C_tC+\varkappa_t , \] with \(\varkappa_t\) the same for every such choice. As \(u\) is concave and \(u'\left(C_t\right)=\Lambda_tP^C_t\), \(u\left(C\right)-\Lambda_tP^C_tC\le u\left(C_t\right)-\Lambda_tP^C_tC_t\), and the planner’s \(\left(C_t,G_t\right)\), which meets (HB) by step 3 with \(C_t\ge0\) and \(K_{t+1}\ge0\), attains the date problem.

Step 5: the mining sector solves its problem. Under the common-pool reading the sector carries the reserve alone, \(S_{t+1}=S_t+D-N\). By (IN), (SP) and step 1, its payoff plus the reserve at its valuation is \[ \Pi^N_t+p^S_tS_{t+1}=g^N_t\left(N\right)+g^D_t\left(D\right)+p^S_tS_t , \] \[ g^N_t\left(N\right)\equiv\left(\Psi_t-p^W_t-p^P_t\Omega^{N,S}-p^S_t\right)N-\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^N_t\right)C^N\left(N,S_t,t\right), \qquad g^D_t\left(D\right)\equiv\left(p^S_t+p^X_t-p^P_t\Omega^{D,S}\right)D-\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^D_t\right)C^D\left(D,X_t,t\right), \] both concave on \(\left[0,\infty\right)\), the costs being convex in the flow and their factors positive. The derivative of \(g^N_t\) is zero at \(N_t\) where \(N_t>0\), by (ND), and nonpositive at zero where \(N_t=0\), by its corner; a concave function lies below its tangent, so \(N_t\) maximises \(g^N_t\) over \(N\ge0\), and likewise \(D_t\) maximises \(g^D_t\). The planner’s \(\left(N_t,D_t\right)\) meets the mass restrictions, \(S_{t+1}\ge0\) among them, so it attains the date problem over any set of choices that contains it. The first two conditions of (M) are those of (ND), through \(p^R-\tau^P\Omega^{N,S}=\Psi-p^W-p^P\Omega^{N,S}\) and \(P^N=1+\zeta\Omega^N\), and through \(\tau^X=-p^X\) and \(P^D=1+\zeta\Omega^D\); the reserve’s equation in (M) is that of (CR) at \(P^N=1+\zeta\Omega^N\); and the transversality condition \(\beta^t\lambda_tp^S_tS_{t+1}\to0\) is the planner’s.

Step 6: the waste sector solves its problem. By (SP) and (W), \(\tau^W_t+p^{\mathcal W}_t=p^W_t+p^{\mathcal W}_t=0\), so (GF) holds, and the date problem maximises \[ \left(\tau^W_t+p^{\mathcal W}_t\right)W+\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)+\left(1-\mu\right)p^{\mathcal W}_t\mathcal W_t , \] in which \(W\) no longer appears: the planner’s \(W_t\) attains it, and a pair attains it exactly when it maximises \(\Pi^W_t\). At the supporting prices, by step 1 and with \(\Xi^h\) substituted, \[ \Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)=\mathcal G^K_t\,\mathcal R\left(\varpi H_t,K^R\right)-\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right)c^W\left(\varpi\right)H_t-\tilde F_{K,t}K^R-p^P_t\left[1-\left(1-d^W\right)\varpi\right]H_t . \] Collecting the terms of \(B_t\) that move with the pair, \(Y\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)-C^W\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)+\left(\zeta-p^W+d^Wp^P\right)R^R\) and the untreated stream’s \(-p^P\left[1-\left(1-d^W\right)\varpi\right]H\), and using \(\Psi-\zeta=F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\), \[ \Pi^W_t-B_t=\left(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\right)\left[F_{R,t}\,\mathcal R-F_{K,t}K^R-F\left(K_t-K^R,N_t+\mathcal R,P_t,t\right)\right]+c_t , \] with \(\mathcal R=\mathcal R\left(\varpi H_t,K^R\right)\), \(F_{R,t}\) and \(F_{K,t}\) at the planner’s input, and \(c_t\) the same for every pair.

Capital without a treated flow. Where \(\varpi_tH_t=0\), the planner’s pair has \(K^R_t=0\): capital then recovers nothing, and were \(K^R_t>0\) the derivative of \(B_t\) along \(\left(0,-1\right)\) would be \(\tilde F_{K,t}>0\), against the requirement on the pair.

If \(\mathcal G^K_t\le0\), the pair maximises. For \(K^R>0\), \(\mathcal G^K_t\mathcal R\left(\varpi H_t,K^R\right)\le0=\mathcal G^K_t\mathcal R\left(\varpi H_t,0\right)\) and \(\tilde F_{K,t}K^R>0\), so \(\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)<\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,0\right)\) at every \(\varpi\). The planner’s pair has \(K^R_t=0\): otherwise \(\varpi_tH_t>0\), by capital without a treated flow, so \(\mathcal R\) has the derivative \(a'\left(x_t\right)\) in \(K^R\) there, the derivative of \(B_t\) along \(\left(0,-1\right)\) is \(\tilde F_{K,t}-\mathcal G^K_ta'\left(x_t\right)>0\), against the requirement. Along \(\left(\pm1,0\right)\) from \(\left(\varpi_t,0\right)\), \(\mathcal R=0\) does not move, so \(\Pi^W_t\left(\cdot,0\right)-B_t\left(\cdot,0\right)\) is constant and the requirement makes the one-sided derivatives of the concave \(\Pi^W_t\left(\cdot,0\right)\) at \(\varpi_t\) nonpositive: \(\varpi_t\) maximises it over \(\left[0,1\right]\). So \(\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\le\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,0\right)\le\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi_t,K^R_t\right)\).

If \(\mathcal G^K_t>0\), the pair maximises. Then \(\Pi^W_t\) is concave on \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,\infty\right)\), by Lemma Q, the convexity of \(c^W\) with \(1+\zeta\Omega^W>0\), and linearity of the rest. The pair is not at \(K^R_t=0\) with \(\varpi_tH_t>0\) and \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)=\infty\): along \(\left(0,1\right)\), \(\mathcal R\left(\varpi_tH_t,s\right)/s\to\infty\) as \(s\downarrow0\), and differentiability of \(F\) at the planner’s input gives \(B_t\left(\varpi_t,s\right)-B_t\left(\varpi_t,0\right)=\mathcal G^K_t\mathcal R\left(\varpi_tH_t,s\right)-\tilde F_{K,t}s+o\left(s+\mathcal R\left(\varpi_tH_t,s\right)\right)\), whose ratio to \(s\) tends to \(+\infty\), against the requirement. So \(\mathcal R\) has a finite one-sided derivative \(\mathcal R'\) at the planner’s pair along each direction \(\left(d\varpi,dK^R\right)\) into \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,\infty\right)\): it is differentiable where \(\varpi_tH_t>0\) and \(K^R_t>0\); it has \(\mathcal R'=a'\!\left(0^+\right)dK^R\) where \(K^R_t=0<\varpi_tH_t\) and \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)<\infty\); and where \(\varpi_tH_t=0\), so \(K^R_t=0\), it is \(s\,\mathcal R\left(d\varpi H_t,dK^R\right)\) along the direction, linear in the step \(s\). The chain rule at the planner’s input then gives the bracket of \(\Pi^W_t-B_t\) the one-sided derivative \(F_{R,t}\mathcal R'-F_{K,t}dK^R-\left(-F_{K,t}dK^R+F_{R,t}\mathcal R'\right)=0\), so the one-sided derivatives of \(\Pi^W_t\) equal those of \(B_t\), nonpositive along every direction that keeps the pair in \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,K_t\right]\). As \(K^R_t<K_t\), by production capital is positive, these are all the directions into the sector’s set \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,\infty\right)\). For a concave function the difference quotient along a segment does not rise as the step grows, so \(\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)-\Pi^W_t\left(\varpi_t,K^R_t\right)\) is at most the one-sided derivative along \(\left(\varpi-\varpi_t,K^R-K^R_t\right)\), which is nonpositive.

The valuation. With the sector’s choices the planner’s, (SP) and step 1 give \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}=\alpha\varpi\left(\Psi-p^W\right)-\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)c^W-p^P\left[1-\varpi\left(1-d^W\right)-d^W\alpha\varpi\right]=h\), so (WS) is the recursion of \(p^{\mathcal W}\) in (CR), and the transversality condition \(\beta^t\lambda_tp^{\mathcal W}_t\mathcal W_{t+1}\to0\) is the planner’s.

Step 7: the final-goods sector operates at a maximum of its profit. Every period operates, so Definition 2 asks that the planner’s \(\left(K^Y_t,R_t\right)\) maximise \(\Pi^Y_t\) over \(\left[0,\infty\right)^2\). By step 1, at any \(\left(K,R\right)\), \[ \Pi^Y_t\left(K,R\right)=\left(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\right)\left[F\left(K,R,P_t,t\right)-F_{R,t}R-F_{K,t}K\right], \] with \(F_{R,t}\) and \(F_{K,t}\) at the planner’s input. So (FY) holds there, by the definitions of \(\Psi\) and \(\tilde F_K\), and \(\Pi^Y_t\left(K^Y_t,R_t\right)\) is the left side of (PA), nonnegative by hypothesis. Below the floor \(F=0\), and \(\Pi^Y_t\left(K,R\right)\le0\le\Pi^Y_t\left(K^Y_t,R_t\right)\), as \(F_{R,t},F_{K,t}>0\). On and above the floor, with \(R^e=R-\bar R\), \[ \Pi^Y_t\left(K,R\right)-\Pi^Y_t\left(K^Y_t,R_t\right)=\left(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\right)\left[\mathcal F\left(K,R^e,P_t,t\right)-\mathcal F\left(K^Y_t,R^e_t,P_t,t\right)-F_{K,t}\left(K-K^Y_t\right)-F_{R,t}\left(R^e-R^e_t\right)\right], \] which is nonpositive, as Section 2.1 takes \(\mathcal F\left(\cdot,\cdot,P_t,t\right)\) concave in \(\left(K^Y,R^e\right)\), so that its graph lies below its tangent plane at the planner’s input. The workhorse of Section 3.6 is an example: its output \(A_te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR^e\right)^{\gamma}\), with \(\beta_K,\gamma>0\), as \(\mathcal F_K,\mathcal F_{R^e}>0\), and \(\beta_K+\gamma<1\), is concave in \(\left(K^Y,R^e\right)\) at every \(P\) and \(t\).

Step 8: the workhorse’s threshold. In the workhorse, above the floor \(F=A_te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR^e\right)^{\gamma}\) with \(A_t,B_t>0\) and \(\mu_F\equiv\beta_K+\gamma<1\). At the planner’s input \(K^Y_t>0\), by production capital is positive, and \(R^e_t>0\), as \(F_R=\gamma Y/R^e\) is finite there, so \(Y_t>0\), \(F_RR^e=\gamma Y\) and \(F_KK^Y=\beta_KY\). Then \[ Y-F_RR-F_KK^Y=Y-F_R\left(R^e+\bar R\right)-\beta_KY=Y\left[\left(1-\mu_F\right)-\frac{\gamma\bar R}{R^e}\right], \] and with \(Y_t>0\) and \(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t>0\), (PA) holds at \(t\) exactly when \(R^e_t\ge\gamma\bar R/\left(1-\mu_F\right)\).

Step 9: unified ownership. The sector now owns \(X\) as well, with valuation \(p^X_t\) of a unit of \(X_{t+1}\), and \(\tau^X\equiv0\). Its date problem adds \(p^X_tX_{t+1}=p^X_t\left(X_t+D\right)\) and drops \(\tau^X_tD\), so \(g^D_t\) is the function of step 5 and the planner’s \(\left(N_t,D_t\right)\) still attains it. The exploration condition of (M) at \(\tau^X=0\), with \(p^X\) added to the sector’s own value of a discovered tonne, is \(p^S+p^X=P^DC^D_D+\tau^P\Omega^{D,S}\), the exploration condition of (ND). The valuation follows the asset equation \(\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^X_t=-P^DC^D_X\big|_{t+1}+p^X_{t+1}\), what a unit of \(X_{t+1}\) adds to the next date’s payoff and to the stock it carries, as the reserve’s equation in (M) is for \(S\); at \(P^D=1+\zeta\Omega^D\) it is the recursion of \(p^X\) in (CR), and its transversality condition is the planner’s. The tax leaves the government budget, \(T_t=\tau^P_t\left(\Xi^h_t+\mathcal N_t\right)\), and step 3 is otherwise unchanged. So \(p^X\) arises inside the sector.

Conclusion. Every period operates, with the final-goods sector at a maximum of its profit (step 7); the household, the mining sector and the waste sector solve their problems (steps 4 to 6) at the prices (SP), with the private valuations \(\left(q,p^S,p^M,p^{\mathcal W}\right)\) equal to the costates, the household’s multiplier \(\Lambda=\lambda\) and the planner’s interest rate; the government budget balances and the markets clear (step 3); and the stocks are the planner’s. So the planner’s allocation is a competitive equilibrium of Definition 2, with (IN) and the sign of \(\tau^X\) of step 2.
