O.7 The sign of the gate fee without the emission tax

Proposition 11 of the paper (the gate fee without the emission tax) states that where the owner of the waste stock pays no tax on what the stock emits, the private handling dividend is never negative and the gate fee never positive, and when each is strictly so. It also states that under a nonnegative emission tax a positive fee needs a later tax. This page proves it in full. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

The statement

Proposition 11 (the gate fee without the emission tax). General primitives, with \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)<\infty\), positive user prices of handling and consumption along the path, the sector solving its problem, and the price of the waste stock equal to the forward sum of its dividends. At the owned stockpile without instruments, and at any setting with the stock owned and \(\tau^P=0\) at every date:

1. \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s\ge0\) at every date \(s\), with \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s>0\) if and only if \(s\) is a treatment date;

2. \(\tau^W_t\le0\) at every date \(t\); for \(\mu<1\), \(\tau^W_t<0\) if and only if some later date is a treatment date, so that when treatment never ceases for good the fee is negative at every date, even before treatment begins; for \(\mu=1\), \(\tau^W_t<0\) if and only if \(t+1\) is a treatment date.

With the stock owned and \(\tau^P_s=0\) only at the dates \(s>t\), item 1 holds at every \(s>t\) and item 2 at \(t\). At any setting with the stock owned and a nonnegative emission tax, a positive fee at \(t\) requires a positive tax at some later date, and is guaranteed when nothing is treated after \(t\) and the tax is positive at some later date (\(t+1\) when \(\mu=1\)), the fee then being the discounted sum of the taxes the stock will pay, \[ \tau^W_t = \sum_{s>t}\Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t,s}\left(1-\mu\right)^{s-t-1}\mu\,\tau^P_s , \qquad \Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t,s}\equiv\prod_{j=t+1}^{s}\left(1+r_j\right)^{-1} . \]

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions; it needs no functional form. Item 1 compares the waste sector’s choices at one date, at prices it takes as given, item 2 sums the dividends of a tonne in the stock, and of the other agents only the household’s first-order condition enters, through the discount factors. What the proof uses restates the terms of the statement.

What the proof uses

From the paper’s model (Section 2). The handled flow is \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\), with \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+W_t\) and \(\mu\in\left(0,1\right)\), \(\mu=1\) being the limiting case. Handling costs \(c^W\left(\varpi\right)\equiv c^c\varpi+c^T\left(\varpi\right)\) units of the final good per handled tonne, with \(c^c>0\), \(c^T\left(0\right)=c^{T\prime}\left(0\right)=0\) and \(c^{T\prime\prime}>0\), so \(c^T\) is strictly convex and nonnegative, \(c^W\left(0\right)=0\) and \(c^W\left(\varpi\right)>0\) at \(\varpi>0\). Recovered material is \(R^R=a\left(x\right)T\), with \(T\equiv\varpi H\), \(x\equiv K^R/T\), \(a\left(0\right)=0\), \(a_x>0\), \(a_{xx}<0\) and \(a\le\bar a\le1\); as \(0\le R^R\le\bar aT\), a treated flow of zero recovers nothing. The marginal yield \(\alpha\equiv a-a'x\) of Appendix A.3 lies between zero and \(a\) at \(x>0\), and \(\alpha\left(0\right)=0\) as the slope \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)\) is finite, so \(\alpha\in\left[0,\bar a\right]\) at every intensity. And \(u'>0\).

From the paper’s market economy (Section 4.1, Appendix C). Raw material sells at \(p^R\) per tonne, capital rents at \(r^K\), handing over a tonne of waste costs the gate fee \(\tau^W\), and the matter in the final good is priced at the material-content price \(z\). A unit of the final good put to a current use \(j\in\left\{Y,N,D,W,C\right\}\) costs its buyer the user price \(P^j\equiv1+\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega\omega^j\). The instruments are an emission tax \(\tau^P\) per tonne of \(\Xi\), levied on the agent that emits it, a discovery tax \(\tau^X\), and \(z\). Agents take the paths of prices, instruments, \(P\) and \(\Omega\) as given and discount at the market interest rate \(r\).

The waste sector owns \(\mathcal W\). It takes in the flow \(W\) at the fee, draws \(H=\mu\mathcal W\) from its stock, chooses the treated share \(\varpi\in\left[0,1\right]\) and the recycling capital \(K^R\ge0\) it rents at \(r^K\), sells \(R^R\) at \(p^R\), pays the emission tax on the emission \(\Xi^h\) of Section 2, and buys its handling input at \(P^W\). Its operating profit at \(t\), which Appendix C writes with \(\Xi^h\) substituted, is \[ \Pi^W_t\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\equiv p^R_t\,R^R-P^W_t\,c^W\left(\varpi\right)H_t-r^K_tK^R-\tau^P_t\,\Xi^h , \] \(R^R\) and \(\Xi^h\) being those of the pair at the flow \(H_t\). Its value \(J^W_t\left(\mathcal W_t\right)\) is the fee on what it accepts, plus its operating profit, plus the discounted value of the stock it carries into the next period, at its best choice: the maximum over \(W_t\ge0\), \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\) and \(K^R_t\ge0\) of \(\tau^W_tW_t+\Pi^W_t+J^W_{t+1}\left(\mathcal W_{t+1}\right)/\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)\). Its valuation of a tonne taken in at \(t\) is \(p^{\mathcal W}_t\equiv\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)^{-1}\partial J^W_{t+1}/\partial\mathcal W_{t+1}\), and with the stock owned the gate fee is minus that valuation (Section 4.1), \[ \tau^W_t=-p^{\mathcal W}_t , \tag{GF} \] and the valuation follows the recursion of Section 4.1, with the private handling dividend \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}\) taken at the sector’s treated share and intensity: \[ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^{\mathcal W}_t=\mu\,h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t+1}+\left(1-\mu\right)p^{\mathcal W}_{t+1}, \qquad h^{\mathrm{mkt}}\equiv\alpha\varpi\,p^R-P^Wc^W-\tau^P\left[1-\varpi\left(1-d^W\right)-d^W\alpha\varpi\right]. \tag{WS} \] The owned stockpile has the stock owned and nothing else set (Section 4.2): without instruments, \(\tau^P=z=\tau^X=0\) at every date. A setting with the stock owned has (GF) and any instrument paths. The displaced mass \(\mathcal N\) is emitted by the mining sector (Definition 2), so it enters neither the waste sector’s cash flow nor its stock.

The market interest rate is the household’s: with \(\Lambda_t\) its multiplier on its budget, \(u'\left(C_t\right)=\Lambda_tP^C_t\) and \(1+r_{t+1}\equiv\Lambda_t/\left(\beta\Lambda_{t+1}\right)\) (Appendix C, the household’s conditions). With \(u'>0\) and \(P^C_t>0\), a hypothesis of the statement, \(\Lambda_t>0\) at every date, so \(1+r_{t+1}>0\) and \(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t,s}=\beta^{s-t}\Lambda_s/\Lambda_t>0\) for \(s>t\).

The problem per handled tonne. At a date with \(H_s>0\) the sector’s pair per tonne is \(\left(\varpi_s,k^R_s\right)\), with \(k^R_s\equiv K^R_s/H_s\), and \(x_s=k^R_s/\varpi_s\) at \(\varpi_s>0\). At a date with \(H_s=0\) the operating profit \(-r^K_sK^R_s\) does not depend on the share, and the intensity is undefined. There we read the sector’s choice per handled tonne, as the share and the intensity at which a tonne added to the stock would be handled, which is what the dividend prices. Write \(K^R=k^RH\). At a date \(s\) with \(\tau^P_s=0\), \(\Pi^W_s\left(\varpi,k^RH_s\right)=H_s\,\pi_s\left(\varpi,k^R\right)\), where \[ \pi_s\left(\varpi,k^R\right)\equiv p^R_s\,\varpi\,a\!\left(k^R/\varpi\right)-P^W_s\,c^W\left(\varpi\right)-r^K_sk^R\quad\left(\varpi>0\right), \qquad \pi_s\left(0,k^R\right)\equiv-r^K_sk^R , \] as \(R^R=\varpi H_s\,a\left(k^R/\varpi\right)\) at \(\varpi>0\) and \(R^R=0\) at \(\varpi=0\). Let \(\pi^*_s\equiv\sup\pi_s\) over \(\varpi\in\left[0,1\right]\) and \(k^R\ge0\). At \(H_s>0\) the sector’s pair maximises \(\Pi^W_s\) exactly when its pair per tonne maximises \(\pi_s\). At \(H_s=0\) the reading takes a maximiser \(\left(\varpi_s,k^R_s\right)\) of \(\pi_s\), with \(x_s\equiv k^R_s/\varpi_s\) at \(\varpi_s>0\), since \(\pi_s\) is what a tonne added to the stock would earn; that the sector solves its problem includes, at such a date, that \(\pi_s\) has a maximiser.

The terms of the statement. A treatment date is a date \(s\) at which the sector treats, \(\varpi_s>0\), with the share per tonne at \(H_s=0\). The sector solves its problem when at every date its choice attains the maximum defining \(J^W_t\). As the pair \(\left(\varpi_t,K^R_t\right)\) enters that maximum only through \(\Pi^W_t\), the next stock not depending on it, the pair then maximises \(\Pi^W_t\), as Appendix C states, and at a date \(s\) with \(\tau^P_s=0\) the pair per tonne maximises \(\pi_s\). The forward sum of its dividends is \(\sum_{s>t}w_{t,s}h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s\), with the weights of the statement’s display, \[ w_{t,s}\equiv\Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t,s}\left(1-\mu\right)^{s-t-1}\mu\qquad\left(s>t\right), \] the probability that a tonne taken in at \(t\) is handled at \(s\), times the discount factor to \(t\). With \(0^0\equiv1\), at \(\mu=1\) the only weight that is not zero is \(w_{t,t+1}=\Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t,t+1}\). It is the forward solution of (WS): dividing (WS) by \(1+r_{t+1}\) and substituting it into itself from \(t\) to \(n\) gives the partial sum to \(n\) plus \(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t,n}\left(1-\mu\right)^{n-t}p^{\mathcal W}_n\).

The dividend is the optimised profit per handled tonne

Lemma H. At a date \(s\) with \(\tau^P_s=0\) and \(P^W_s>0\), at which the sector solves its problem, \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s=\pi^*_s\ge0\), and \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s>0\) if and only if \(s\) is a treatment date.

Proof. Dumping is free. The pair \(\left(0,0\right)\) is feasible with \(\pi_s\left(0,0\right)=0\), so \(\pi^*_s\ge0\). The pair per tonne \(\left(\varpi_s,k^R_s\right)\) attains \(\pi^*_s\), which needs \(r^K_s\ge0\), since at \(r^K_s<0\) the profit \(\pi_s\left(0,k^R\right)=-r^K_sk^R\) grows without bound. At \(\tau^P_s=0\) the dividend is \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s=\alpha_s\varpi_sp^R_s-P^W_sc^W\left(\varpi_s\right)\).

Without treatment. Let \(\varpi_s=0\). Then \(\pi^*_s=-r^K_sk^R_s\le0\), so \(\pi^*_s=0\). Both terms of \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s\) are zero: the first because \(\varpi_s=0\) and \(\alpha\in\left[0,\bar a\right]\), whatever value the undefined intensity gives it, the second as \(c^W\left(0\right)=0\). So \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s=0=\pi^*_s\).

Treatment leaves a rent. Let \(\varpi_s>0\) and \(\ell\in\left(0,1\right)\). Scaling the pair by \(\ell\) keeps its intensity, so revenue, collection cost and rent scale by \(\ell\), and \[ \pi_s\left(\ell\varpi_s,\ell k^R_s\right)-\ell\,\pi^*_s=P^W_s\left[\ell\,c^T\left(\varpi_s\right)-c^T\left(\ell\varpi_s\right)\right]>0 , \] by \(P^W_s>0\) and the strict convexity of \(c^T\) with \(c^T\left(0\right)=0\). Were \(\pi^*_s=0\), the feasible pair at \(\ell=\tfrac12\) would earn more than \(\pi^*_s\). So \(\pi^*_s>0\).

The rent is the dividend. Still with \(\varpi_s>0\): \(\pi_s\left(\varpi_s,0\right)=-P^W_sc^W\left(\varpi_s\right)<0\), since \(a\left(0\right)=0\), \(P^W_s>0\) and \(c^W\left(\varpi_s\right)>0\), so \(k^R_s>0\) and \(x_s\in\left(0,\infty\right)\). As \(k^R_s\) is an interior maximiser of \(k^R\mapsto\pi_s\left(\varpi_s,k^R\right)\), whose derivative is \(p^R_sa'\left(k^R/\varpi_s\right)-r^K_s\), we have \(p^R_sa'\left(x_s\right)=r^K_s\), the capital margin of Section 4.1 at \(\tau^P=0\). With \(a=\alpha+a'x\), \(\varpi_sa\left(x_s\right)=\alpha_s\varpi_s+a'\left(x_s\right)k^R_s\), so \[ \pi^*_s=\alpha_s\varpi_sp^R_s-P^W_sc^W\left(\varpi_s\right)+k^R_s\left[p^R_sa'\left(x_s\right)-r^K_s\right]=h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s . \] In both cases \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s=\pi^*_s\), positive exactly when \(\varpi_s>0\). The cases apply to every maximiser of \(\pi_s\), so either every maximiser treats or none does: whether \(s\) is a treatment date does not depend on the one the sector picks.

Proof of Proposition 11

Proof. Step 1: item 2 at a date \(t\) with \(\tau^P_s=0\) at every \(s>t\). Lemma H applies at every \(s>t\), as \(P^W_s>0\) and the sector solves its problem, so \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s\ge0\), positive exactly when \(s\) is a treatment date. By the hypothesis on the price of the waste stock, \(p^{\mathcal W}_t=\sum_{s>t}w_{t,s}h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s\), a sum of nonnegative terms, which is nonnegative and positive exactly when one of its terms is. When \(\mu<1\) every weight is positive, as \(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t,s}>0\) and \(\mu>0\), so \(p^{\mathcal W}_t>0\) exactly when some \(s>t\) is a treatment date; at \(\mu=1\) the only positive weight is \(w_{t,t+1}\), so \(p^{\mathcal W}_t>0\) exactly when \(t+1\) is one. By (GF), \(\tau^W_t=-p^{\mathcal W}_t\le0\), negative on the same conditions. The argument uses \(\tau^P\) at the dates after \(t\) only, which with Lemma H at each \(s>t\) is the sentence of the statement on \(\tau^P_s=0\) only at the dates \(s>t\).

Step 2: items 1 and 2. At the owned stockpile without instruments, and at any setting with the stock owned and \(\tau^P=0\) at every date, Lemma H holds at every date, which is item 1, and step 1 at every date, which is item 2. For \(\mu<1\), if treatment never ceases for good, every date has a later treatment date, so \(\tau^W_t<0\) at every \(t\), the dates before the first treatment date included. The instruments \(z\) and \(\tau^X\) enter only through \(p^R\), \(r^K\), \(P^W\) and \(P^C\), which the proof takes as given.

Step 3: a positive fee requires a later tax. Let the stock be owned, \(\tau^P_s\ge0\) at every date, and \(\tau^W_t>0\). Were \(\tau^P_s=0\) at every \(s>t\), step 1 would give \(\tau^W_t\le0\). So \(\tau^P_s\ne0\), hence \(\tau^P_s>0\), at some \(s>t\).

Step 4: with nothing treated after \(t\), the fee is the discounted tax. At a date \(s\) that is not a treatment date, \(\varpi_s=0\), the first term of \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s\) is zero as in the proof of Lemma H, and \(c^W\left(0\right)=0\), so \(h^{\mathrm{mkt}}_s=-\tau^P_s\left[1-0-0\right]=-\tau^P_s\). If no date after \(t\) is a treatment date, the hypothesis on the price gives \(p^{\mathcal W}_t=-\sum_{s>t}w_{t,s}\tau^P_s\), and (GF) gives \(\tau^W_t=\sum_{s>t}w_{t,s}\tau^P_s\), the display of the statement. If moreover \(\tau^P_s\ge0\) at every \(s>t\) and \(\tau^P_{s'}>0\) at a date \(s'>t\) of positive weight, any later date when \(\mu<1\) and \(s'=t+1\) when \(\mu=1\), then \(\tau^W_t\ge w_{t,s'}\tau^P_{s'}>0\).

The weak sign does not need the forward sum

The weak sign of item 2 holds without the hypothesis on the price of the waste stock, once \(J^W_{t+1}\left(\mathcal W_{t+1}\right)\) is read as the sector’s value, the supremum of the discounted cash flows of its plans from \(t+1\) on. Let \(\tau^P_s=0\) at every \(s>t\) and \(\mathcal W'_{t+1}>\mathcal W_{t+1}\). From the larger stock a plan can keep its acceptances \(W_s\), so every later handled flow is larger, \(H'_s\ge H_s\), and its treated flows \(T_s\) and recycling capital \(K^R_s\), at the share \(T_s/H'_s\) where \(H'_s>0\). It then recovers the same material, earns the same fees, pays the same rent and dumps the extra tonnes, free at \(\tau^P_s=0\). Its handling bill \(P^W_s\left[c^cT_s+H'_sc^T\left(T_s/H'_s\right)\right]\) is no larger, as \(P^W_s>0\) and \(H\,c^T\left(T/H\right)\) does not rise with \(H\) at fixed \(T\), \(c^T\left(\varpi\right)/\varpi\) being nondecreasing for a convex \(c^T\) with \(c^T\left(0\right)=0\). With \(\Lambda^{\mathrm{mkt}}>0\) its discounted cash flow exceeds the original’s by a sum of nonnegative terms, so \(J^W_{t+1}\) is nondecreasing, its derivative is nonnegative, and \(p^{\mathcal W}_t\ge0\), \(\tau^W_t\le0\). The strict signs and the two clauses on the tax use the forward sum.
