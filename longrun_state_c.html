O.3 When state C is available: the tail condition and the workhorse’s path

Section 3.5 of the paper (the closure criterion) states when state C is available at all, and this page proves it in full. That is a question about feasible paths rather than candidates. Under the soft ceiling no feasible path is in state C when the yield tail is too slow, and in the paper’s workhorse, with no new material stored in capital, state C is available exactly when \(\mu\mathcal B>\bar R\), from every initial capital stock above a threshold. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol a proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used. The definitions the proofs take from the paper, the collected ledger (L) and Lemma A are on the page of lemmas shared by the long-run proofs.

The statement

The availability of state C.

(a) Let the ceiling be soft and Assumption 1(i) hold, and let the limit yield function \(a^\infty\) have a slow tail, \(\int_1^\infty\left[1-a^\infty\left(x\right)\right]dx/x=\infty\). Then no feasible path is in state C, in either soft-ceiling cell and from any initial stocks. A logarithmic tail, \(1-a^\infty\left(x\right)\sim c\left(\ln\xi x\right)^{-\iota}\), is slow when \(\iota\le1\).

(b) Take the workhorse under the soft ceiling, with \(\phi^I=0\), \(\omega^Y,\omega^C>0\), and extraction and exploration costs that are finite at a positive reserve and below the exhaustion point of discovery. State C is available from some initial capital stock if and only if \(\mu\mathcal B>\bar R\), and then from every initial capital stock above a finite threshold that depends on the primitives and the other initial stocks. On the path constructed, consumption, capital and output grow at the circular rate \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}\), \(\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\Gamma\), and its value is finite for \(\eta\ge1\), and for \(\eta<1\) when \(\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\eta}<1+\rho\).

We prove part (a) of the availability of state C for general primitives, and part (b) by a construction in the workhorse at \(\phi^I=0\), where the material embodied in capital only runs off; the workhorse’s exponential yield makes every intensity schedule explicit. The workhorse is restated at the head of Proof of the availability of state C.

Proof of the availability of state C

The workhorse (the paper’s long-run section): output \(A_te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR^e\right)^{\gamma}\) for \(R\ge\bar R\), and zero below, with \(A_0,B_0>0\) and the trends of Assumption 1(i), \(\kappa\ge0\), \(\beta_K,\gamma>0\) and \(\beta_K+\gamma<1\); the exponential yield \(a\left(x\right)=\bar a\left(1-e^{-\xi x}\right)\), \(\xi>0\), which under the soft ceiling is \(1-e^{-\xi x}\); CRRA utility, \(u\left(C\right)=C^{1-\eta}/\left(1-\eta\right)\) with \(\eta>0\), \(\ln C\) at \(\eta=1\); and \(\Gamma\equiv\left(1+g_A\right)\left(1+g_B\right)^{\gamma}>1\).

(a) A slow tail

No first-order condition is used.

Proof. Suppose a feasible path is in state C. Then \(R_t\to R_\infty>\bar R\ge0\), so by Lemma A(c) there is a date \(T_2\) with \(H_t\ge\underline H\equiv R_\infty/2\) for \(t\ge T_2\), and the loop closes. As \(1-\varpi_t\le1-a_t\varpi_t\to0\), there is \(T_3\ge T_2\) with \(\varpi_t\ge\tfrac12\) for \(t\ge T_3\).

Capital grows at most geometrically. Output grows at the rate \(g>0\) of Definition 1, so for any \(\bar g>g\) there is a date after which \(Y_{t+1}\le\left(1+\bar g\right)Y_t\), and hence \(Y_t\le\bar Y\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\) at every date for some constant \(\bar Y\). The goods constraint, with consumption and the three cost flows nonnegative, gives \(K_{t+1}\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t+Y_t\), and by induction \(K_t\le\bar K\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\) with \(\bar K\equiv K_0+\bar Y/\left(\bar g+\delta\right)\): if it holds at \(t\), then \(K_{t+1}\le\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\left[\left(1-\delta\right)\bar K+\bar Y\right]\le\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\left[\left(1-\delta\right)\bar K+\left(\bar g+\delta\right)\bar K\right]=\bar K\left(1+\bar g\right)^{t+1}\).

The leakage rate is bounded below. For \(t\ge T_3\), \(K^R_t\le K_t\) gives \(x_t=K^R_t/\left(\varpi_tH_t\right)\le2K_t/\underline H\le c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\) with \(c_K\equiv2\bar K/\underline H\). Write \(L^\infty\equiv1-a^\infty\), which is nonincreasing. Since \(a\left(x,t\right)\le a^\infty\left(x\right)\), \[ 1-a_t\varpi_t\;\ge\;1-a_t\;\ge\;L^\infty\left(x_t\right)\;\ge\;L^\infty\!\left(c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\right) \qquad\left(t\ge T_3\right). \]

The slow tail. On \(\left[c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^t,\,c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^{t+1}\right]\), \(L^\infty\le L^\infty\!\left(c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\right)\) and \(\int dx/x=\ln\left(1+\bar g\right)\), so \[ \int_{c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^{T_3}}^{\infty}L^\infty\left(x\right)\frac{dx}{x}\;\le\;\ln\left(1+\bar g\right)\sum_{t\ge T_3}L^\infty\!\left(c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^t\right). \] The left side is infinite: as \(0\le L^\infty\le1\), it differs from \(\int_1^\infty L^\infty dx/x=\infty\) by at most \(\left|\ln\left[c_K\left(1+\bar g\right)^{T_3}\right]\right|\). So \(\sum_{t\ge T_3}\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)=\infty\), and the loop does not close: a contradiction.

The logarithmic tail. If \(1-a^\infty\left(x\right)\sim c\left(\ln\xi x\right)^{-\iota}\) with \(c>0\), there is \(\check x\) with \(\xi\check x>1\) and \(1-a^\infty\left(x\right)\ge\tfrac c2\left(\ln\xi x\right)^{-\iota}\) for \(x\ge\check x\), and with \(z=\ln\xi x\), \(\int_{\check x}^\infty\left(\ln\xi x\right)^{-\iota}dx/x=\int_{\ln\xi\check x}^\infty z^{-\iota}dz=\infty\) for \(\iota\le1\).

(b) The workhorse

The proof constructs the path. Throughout, the workhorse is under the soft ceiling, \(a\left(x\right)=1-e^{-\xi x}\), and \(\phi^I=0\), so \(\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I=0\) and the material embodied in capital runs off, \(M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t\), whatever the investment. Write \(c^{\mathrm{full}}_t\equiv c^c\left(t\right)+c^T\left(1,t\right)\) for the handling cost per handled tonne at full treatment, nonincreasing in \(t\).

Proof. Step 1: only if. On any feasible path in state C, \(R^R_t=a_t\varpi_t\mu\mathcal W_t\) with \(a_t\varpi_t\to1\) and \(\mathcal W_t\to\mathcal W_\infty\), so \(R_\infty=\lim_tR^R_t=\mu\mathcal W_\infty\le\mu\mathcal B\) by Lemma A(b). With \(R_\infty>\bar R\), \(\mu\mathcal B>\bar R\). This holds from any initial capital stock and for general primitives.

Step 2: closing the loop from a date \(T_1\). Let the stocks at a date \(T_1\), the initial ones if \(T_1=0\) and otherwise those of a path feasible through \(T_1-1\), satisfy \(\mathfrak m\equiv\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}-\bar R>0\). We show that there is \(\underline K_{T_1}<\infty\), depending on the primitives, on \(\left(\mathcal W_{T_1},M^K_{T_1},P_{T_1}\right)\) and on \(A_{T_1}\) and \(B_{T_1}\), such that from every \(K_{T_1}\ge\underline K_{T_1}\) a feasible continuation is in state C, with consumption at least a chosen \(\underline c>0\) at every date.

The intensity schedule. Choose \(\hat x>0\) with \(3e^{-\xi\hat x}\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}\le\mathfrak m/2\), and let \(\hat x_j\equiv\hat x+j/\xi\) for \(j\ge0\), so that \(1-a\left(\hat x_j\right)=e^{-\xi\hat x}e^{-j}\) and \[ \mu\sum_{j\ge0}\left[1-a\left(\hat x_j\right)\right]=\frac{e}{e-1}\,\mu e^{-\xi\hat x}\;\le\;\bar b\equiv2\mu e^{-\xi\hat x} . \] Since \(\mathfrak m\le\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}\), the choice gives \(\bar b\le\mathfrak m/\left(3\mathcal W_{T_1}\right)<1\). Since \(\mu<1\), \(a\left(\hat x\right)\left(1-\bar b\right)\ge1-e^{-\xi\hat x}-\bar b\ge1-3e^{-\xi\hat x}\), so \(a\left(\hat x\right)\left(1-\bar b\right)\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}\ge\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}-\mathfrak m/2=\bar R+\mathfrak m/2\).

The material block. For \(t=T_1+j\) set \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=1\) and \(K^R_t=\hat x_j\mu\mathcal W_t\), so that \(x_t=\hat x_j\) while \(\mathcal W_t>0\). Then \(\mathcal N_t=0\), \(R_t=R^R_t=a\left(\hat x_j\right)\mu\mathcal W_t\), the ledger gives \(W_t=R_t+\delta M^K_t\), and the transitions give \[ \mathcal W_{t+1}=\left[1-\mu e^{-\xi\hat x_j}\right]\mathcal W_t+\delta M^K_t, \qquad \mathcal M_{t+1}=\mathcal M_t-\mu e^{-\xi\hat x_j}\mathcal W_t, \qquad \Xi_t=d^W\mu e^{-\xi\hat x_j}\mathcal W_t . \] None of this involves capital. Five facts follow, for every \(t\ge T_1\).

  • (M1) \(\mathcal W_t\ge\left(1-\bar b\right)\mathcal W_{T_1}>0\): the factors \(1-b_s\), \(b_s\equiv\mu e^{-\xi\hat x_s}\in\left[0,1\right]\), give \(\mathcal W_t\ge\mathcal W_{T_1}\prod_{s<j}\left(1-b_s\right)\ge\mathcal W_{T_1}\left(1-\sum_sb_s\right)\), the product bound following by induction from \(\left(1-\Sigma\right)\left(1-b\right)\ge1-\Sigma-b\) for \(\Sigma,b\ge0\).
  • (M2) \(R_t\ge a\left(\hat x\right)\left(1-\bar b\right)\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}\ge\bar R+\mathfrak m/2\), since \(\hat x_j\ge\hat x\): production runs at every date.
  • (M3) \(\mathcal M_t\) is nonincreasing, and \(\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal M_t\le\mathcal M_{T_1}\).
  • (M4) \(0\le P_t\le\bar P\equiv P_{T_1}+\mathcal M_{T_1}\): since \(d^W<1\), \(\Xi_t\le\mathcal M_t-\mathcal M_{t+1}\), and \(P_{t+1}=\left[1-\theta\left(P_t\right)\right]P_t+\Xi_t\) lies in \(\left[0,P_t+\Xi_t\right]\), so \(P_t\le P_{T_1}+\mathcal M_{T_1}-\mathcal M_t\).
  • (M5) \(M^K_t\to0\); \(\mathcal M_t\) decreases to a limit \(\mathcal M_\infty\ge\left(1-\bar b\right)\mathcal W_{T_1}>0\), and \(\mathcal W_t=\mathcal M_t-M^K_t\to\mathcal M_\infty\); \(x_t=\hat x_j\to\infty\) and \(a_t\to1\), with \(\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)<\infty\) over the continuation; \(R_t\to R_\infty=\mu\mathcal M_\infty\ge\bar R+\mathfrak m/2\); and \(P_t\to0\). For the last, \(\theta\) is decreasing, so \(\theta\left(P_t\right)\ge\theta\left(\bar P\right)>0\) and \(P_{t+1}\le\left[1-\theta\left(\bar P\right)\right]P_t+\Xi_t\), with \(\Xi_t\le\mu e^{-\xi\hat x_j}\mathcal M_{T_1}\to0\). For any \(\varepsilon>0\), once \(\Xi_t\le\varepsilon\theta\left(\bar P\right)\), \(P_{t+n}\le\left[1-\theta\left(\bar P\right)\right]^nP_t+\varepsilon\).

The constants. Let \(\underline y\equiv A_{T_1}B_{T_1}^{\gamma}e^{-\kappa\bar P}\left(\mathfrak m/2\right)^{\gamma}>0\). Choose a production core \(k\in\left(0,\left[\underline y/\left(g^{\mathrm{circ}}+\delta\right)\right]^{1/\left(1-\beta_K\right)}\right)\), so that the core surplus \(\mathfrak s\equiv\underline y\,k^{\beta_K}-\left(g^{\mathrm{circ}}+\delta\right)k\) is positive, and any \(\underline c>0\). Since \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}>0\), the left side of \[ \mathfrak s\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\;\ge\;\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}\left(\hat x_{j+1}+c^{\mathrm{full}}_{T_1}\right)+\underline c \tag{S} \] grows geometrically in \(j\) and the right side linearly; let \(j^*\) be the least integer such that (S) holds for every \(j\ge j^*\). Let \(\bar G\equiv\left(g^{\mathrm{circ}}+\delta\right)k\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{j^*}+\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}\hat x_{j^*}\), \(\vartheta\equiv\min\left\{1,\Gamma\left(1-\delta\right)^{\beta_K}\right\}\), \(\underline E\equiv0\) if \(j^*=0\) and otherwise \[ \underline E\;\equiv\;\left[\frac{\bar G+c^{\mathrm{full}}_{T_1}\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}+\underline c}{\underline y\,\vartheta^{j^*}}\right]^{1/\beta_K}, \qquad \underline K_{T_1}\;\equiv\;k+\hat x\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}+\underline E . \]

The goods block. Let \(K_{T_1}\ge\underline K_{T_1}\) and \(E\equiv K_{T_1}-k-\hat x\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}\ge\underline E\), a buffer of production capital that is never replaced. For \(t=T_1+j\) let \[ K^Y_t=k\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j+\left(1-\delta\right)^jE, \qquad K_{t+1}=K^Y_{t+1}+K^R_{t+1}, \qquad I_t=K_{t+1}-K_t, \] \(C_t\) from the goods constraint, \(\Omega_t\equiv R_t/\mathcal D_t\) and the states from the transitions. At \(j=0\), \(K^Y_{T_1}+K^R_{T_1}=K_{T_1}\). Within a period the material block fixes \(\mathcal W_{t+1}\) before capital is chosen, so \(K^R_{t+1}\) and \(K_{t+1}\) are defined, and the construction is recursive. Since \(A_tB_t^{\gamma}=A_{T_1}B_{T_1}^{\gamma}\Gamma^j\), (M2) and (M4) give \(Y_t\ge\underline y\,\Gamma^j\left(K^Y_t\right)^{\beta_K}\), and with \(\Gamma=\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\beta_K}\), \[ Y_t\;\ge\;\underline y\,k^{\beta_K}\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j \qquad\text{and}\qquad Y_t\;\ge\;\underline y\,E^{\beta_K}\left[\Gamma\left(1-\delta\right)^{\beta_K}\right]^j\;\ge\;\underline y\,E^{\beta_K}\vartheta^j . \] Gross formation is \(G_t=K_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)K_t=\left(g^{\mathrm{circ}}+\delta\right)k\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j+G^R_t\), the buffer contributing nothing, with \(G^R_t\equiv K^R_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)K^R_t\le\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}\hat x_{j+1}\) by (M3). The cost flows are \(C^N_t=C^D_t=0\) and \(C^W_t=c^{\mathrm{full}}_t\mu\mathcal W_t\le c^{\mathrm{full}}_{T_1}\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}\). For \(j\ge j^*\), \(C_t=Y_t-G_t-C^W_t\ge\mathfrak s\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j-\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}\left(\hat x_{j+1}+c^{\mathrm{full}}_{T_1}\right)\ge\underline c\) by (S). For \(j<j^*\), where \(G_t\le\bar G\) and \(\vartheta^j\ge\vartheta^{j^*}\), \(C_t\ge\underline y\,\underline E^{\beta_K}\vartheta^{j^*}-\bar G-c^{\mathrm{full}}_{T_1}\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}=\underline c\).

Feasibility. The goods constraint holds by the definition of \(C_t\), with \(C_t\ge\underline c>0\). The ledger holds with \(W_t=R_t+\delta M^K_t\ge0\). The intensity condition holds with \(\Omega_t=R_t/\mathcal D_t\ge0\) finite, because \(\phi^IG_t=0\) and \(\mathcal D_t\ge\omega^CC_t>0\). \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=1\), \(K^R_t\ge0\), \(K^Y_t>0\) so \(K^R_t\le K_t\), and \(K_{t+1}>0\). \(S_t=S_{T_1}\ge0\), \(X_t=X_{T_1}\le\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t\ge0\).

State C. Extraction and discovery are zero from \(T_1\) on, so \(N,D\to0\) with \(\sum_tN_t<\infty\), the dates before \(T_1\) being finitely many. By (M2) and (M5), \(R_t\to R_\infty>\bar R\) with \(R^R_t=R_t\), \(a_t\varpi_t=a_t\to1\) with \(x_t\to\infty\) and the loop closed, and both stocks converge. For the goods block divide by \(\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\): \(K^Y_t/\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\to k\) because \(1-\delta<1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\), and \(K^R_t/\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\le\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}\hat x_j/\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\to0\), so \(K_t/\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\to k\); with \(P_t\to0\) and \(R_t\to R_\infty\), \[ \frac{Y_t}{\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j}=A_{T_1}B_{T_1}^{\gamma}e^{-\kappa P_t}\left[\frac{K^Y_t}{\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j}\right]^{\beta_K}\left(R_t-\bar R\right)^{\gamma}\;\longrightarrow\;y_\infty\equiv A_{T_1}B_{T_1}^{\gamma}k^{\beta_K}\left(R_\infty-\bar R\right)^{\gamma}\;\ge\;\underline y\,k^{\beta_K}; \] and since \(\left|G^R_t\right|\le\mu\mathcal M_{T_1}\left(\hat x_{j+1}+\hat x_j\right)\) and \(C^W_t\) is bounded, \(C_t/\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\to y_\infty-\left(g^{\mathrm{circ}}+\delta\right)k\ge\mathfrak s>0\). A positive sequence whose ratio to \(\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\) has a positive limit grows at \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}\), so consumption, capital and output grow at the common rate \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}>0\). Every clause of Definition 1(C) holds.

Step 3: filling the waste stock from the initial stocks. We show that, when \(\mu\mathcal B>\bar R\), from every \(K_0\ge\underline K\), the threshold defined below, the following path is feasible and, by step 2, in state C. Let \(\mathfrak m_{\mathcal B}\equiv\mu\mathcal B-\bar R>0\). Choose an integer \(T_1\ge2\) with \(\mu\left(1-\delta\right)^{T_1}\mathcal B\le\mathfrak m_{\mathcal B}/4\), a share \(\upsilon\in\left(0,1\right)\) with \(2\mu\upsilon\mathcal B\le\mathfrak m_{\mathcal B}/4\), an intensity \(x^E>0\) with \(T_1\mu^2e^{-\xi x^E}\mathcal B\le\mathfrak m_{\mathcal B}/4\), and \(\underline c>0\). For \(t<T_1\) the path

  • discovers \(D_0=\left(1-\upsilon\right)\left(\bar X_{\max}-X_0\right)\), and \(D_t=0\) for \(t\ge1\);
  • extracts \(N_0=\left(1-\upsilon\right)S_0\) and \(N_1=\left(1-\upsilon\right)S_1\), and \(N_t=0\) for \(t\ge2\);
  • treats all handled waste, \(\varpi_t=1\), with recycling capital \(K^R_t=x^E\mu\mathcal W_t\);
  • consumes \(C_t=\underline c\);
  • accumulates \(K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)K_t+Y_t-\underline c-C^N_t-C^D_t-C^W_t\), with \(Y_t=F\left(K_t-K^R_t,R_t,P_t,t\right)\), zero at a date with \(R_t<\bar R\);

and takes \(W_t\) from the ledger, \(\Omega_t\equiv R_t/\mathcal D_t\) and the states from the transitions. From \(T_1\) on it is the continuation of step 2.

The material block before \(T_1\) does not involve capital. At \(t<T_1\), \(R^R_t=a\left(x^E\right)\mu\mathcal W_t\), \(R_t=N_t+R^R_t\), the ledger gives \(W_t=R_t+\delta M^K_t\), the displaced mass \(\mathcal N_t=\Omega^{N,S}N_t+\Omega^{D,S}D_t\) of periods 0 and 1 goes to the environment, \(\Xi_t=\Xi^h_t+\mathcal N_t\), and the transitions of \(S\), \(X\), \(P\), \(M^K\) and \(\mathcal W\) use only these. So the stocks at \(T_1\) and the cost flows \(\mathfrak c_t\equiv C^N_t+C^D_t+C^W_t\), \(t<T_1\), are fixed by the initial material stocks and the choices of step 3, whatever \(K_0\). They are finite. \(C^N_0\), \(C^N_1\) and \(C^D_0\) are costs at a positive reserve and below the exhaustion point of discovery, or zero where the reserve is empty or the exhaustion point reached; the other extraction and exploration costs are zero; and \(C^W_t=c^{\mathrm{full}}_t\mu\mathcal W_t\).

The waste stock at \(T_1\) carries the floor. By the reserve transition \(N_0+N_1=S_0+D_0-S_2\), and \(D_0=\left(1-\upsilon\right)\left(\bar X_{\max}-X_0\right)\), so \(\mathcal M_0+\sum_{t<T_1}N_t=\mathcal M_0+\bar N-\mathfrak u=\mathcal B-\mathfrak u\), with the mass left unused \[ \mathfrak u\equiv\upsilon\left(\bar X_{\max}-X_0\right)+S_2\;\le\;2\upsilon\mathcal B , \] because \(\bar X_{\max}-X_0\le\bar N\), \(S_2=\upsilon S_1=\upsilon\left(\upsilon S_0+D_0\right)\le\upsilon\bar N\) and \(\bar N\le\mathcal B\). The leakage flow before \(T_1\) is \(\left(1-a\left(x^E\right)\right)\mu\mathcal W_t=e^{-\xi x^E}\mu\mathcal W_t\le e^{-\xi x^E}\mu\mathcal B\) per period, by Lemma A(b), and \(M^K_{T_1}=\left(1-\delta\right)^{T_1}M^K_0\le\left(1-\delta\right)^{T_1}\mathcal B\). Summing (L) over \(t<T_1\), \[ \mu\mathcal W_{T_1}=\mu\left(\mathcal M_{T_1}-M^K_{T_1}\right)\;\ge\;\mu\mathcal B-2\mu\upsilon\mathcal B-T_1\mu^2e^{-\xi x^E}\mathcal B-\mu\left(1-\delta\right)^{T_1}\mathcal B\;\ge\;\bar R+\tfrac14\mathfrak m_{\mathcal B} , \] so step 2 applies at \(T_1\) and supplies \(\underline K_{T_1}\), which depends on stocks and technology levels at \(T_1\) that \(K_0\) does not move.

Capital. Let \[ \underline K\;\equiv\;\left(1-\delta\right)^{-T_1}\Big[\max\left\{\underline K_{T_1},\,x^E\mu\mathcal B\right\}+\sum_{t<T_1}\left(\underline c+\mathfrak c_t\right)\Big] \] and \(K_0\ge\underline K\). Since \(Y_t\ge0\), \(K_{t+1}\ge\left(1-\delta\right)K_t-\left(\underline c+\mathfrak c_t\right)\), and by induction \(K_t\ge\left(1-\delta\right)^{T_1}K_0-\sum_{s<T_1}\left(\underline c+\mathfrak c_s\right)\ge\max\left\{\underline K_{T_1},x^E\mu\mathcal B\right\}\) for \(0\le t\le T_1\).

Feasibility before \(T_1\). The goods constraint holds by the definition of \(K_{t+1}\), with \(C_t=\underline c>0\). \(\mathcal D_t\ge\omega^C\underline c>0\) whether production runs or not, so \(\Omega_t\ge0\) is finite and the intensity condition holds. \(W_t\ge0\); \(N_t,D_t\ge0\); \(\varpi_t=1\); \(K^R_t\le x^E\mu\mathcal B\le K_t\); \(K_{t+1}\ge0\); \(S_1=\upsilon S_0+D_0\ge0\) and \(S_t=S_2=\upsilon S_1\ge0\) for \(t\ge2\); \(X_t\le X_0+D_0\le\bar X_{\max}\); \(M^K_t=\left(1-\delta\right)^tM^K_0\ge0\). Production may be idle at some of these dates; no constraint of Problem 1 requires it to run.

So from every \(K_0\ge\underline K\) the path is feasible, and in state C by step 2, all clauses of Definition 1(C) being asymptotic and \(\sum_tN_t=N_0+N_1\). The threshold depends on the primitives and on \(\left(S_0,X_0,P_0,M^K_0,\mathcal W_0\right)\). With step 1, this proves the equivalence.

Step 4: the value. On the continuation, \(\underline c\le C_t\le\bar c_C\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\) for some \(\bar c_C\), since \(C_t/\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^j\) converges, and \(P_t\in\left[0,\bar P\right]\), on which the continuous \(v\) is bounded. For \(\eta>1\), \(u\left(\underline c\right)\le u\left(C_t\right)<0\). For \(\eta=1\), \(\left|\ln C_t\right|\le c_1+c_2j\) with \(c_1\equiv\left|\ln\underline c\right|+\left|\ln\bar c_C\right|\) and \(c_2\equiv\ln\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)\), and \(\sum_j\beta^j\left(c_1+c_2j\right)<\infty\). For \(\eta<1\), \(0<u\left(C_t\right)\le\bar c_C^{1-\eta}\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{\left(1-\eta\right)j}/\left(1-\eta\right)\), whose discounted sum is finite when \(\beta\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\eta}<1\), that is \(\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\eta}<1+\rho\). The dates before \(T_1\) add finitely many finite terms. So the value is finite on the stated terms.
