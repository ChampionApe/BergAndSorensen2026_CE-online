O.11, continued: the planner’s side, a path with a finite value and an optimal path

The proof of Theorem 1 of the paper (a laissez-faire collapse where the planner survives) needs two facts about the planner’s problem in the economy it constructs: a feasible path with a finite value, and an optimal path. This page proves both, as Lemmas W and E, for a class of economies that contains that one, together with Lemma Z on what every feasible path of such an economy does. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page. We take the simplest route: the path of Lemma W closes the loop from the first period along an explicit schedule of recycling capital, and Lemma E finds an optimal path by compactness, with no first-order condition.

What the lemmas use

From Section 2 of the paper. A feasible path meets every constraint of Problem 1: the goods constraint \(Y=C+I+\delta K+C^N+C^D+C^W\), the ledger \(W_t=R_t-\Delta M^K_{t+1}\), the intensity condition \(R=\Omega\left(\mathcal D+\phi^IG\right)\) with \(\mathcal D\equiv\omega^YY+\omega^NC^N+\omega^DC^D+\omega^WC^W+\omega^CC\) and \(G\equiv I+\delta K\), and the transitions, with the flows as Section 2 defines them; the domains \(C,N,D,K^R,W,\Omega\ge0\), \(K_{t+1}\ge0\) and \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\); and the mass restrictions \(S_t\ge0\), \(X_t\le\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t\ge0\). Capital is split as \(K=K^Y+K^R\), so \(0\le K^R_t\le K_t\). The handled flow is \(H_t\equiv\mu\mathcal W_t\) and the treated flow \(T_t\equiv\varpi_tH_t\); recovered material is \(R^R_t=\mathcal R\left(T_t,K^R_t\right)\), where \(\mathcal R\left(T,K\right)=a\left(K/T\right)T\) for \(T>0\) and nothing is recovered at \(T=0\), \(\mathcal R\left(0,K\right)=0\); and \(R=N+R^R\). The handled stream emits \(\Xi^h\equiv\left[1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\left(1-a\right)\right]H\), which reads \(\Xi^h=H-R^R-\left(1-d^W\right)\left(T-R^R\right)\), the displaced mass is \(\mathcal N\equiv\Omega^{N,S}N+\Omega^{D,S}D\), and pollution follows \(P_{t+1}=\left[1-\theta\left(P_t\right)\right]P_t+\Xi^h_t+\mathcal N_t\). Welfare is \(U_0=\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^t\left[u\left(C_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right]\), with \(\beta\equiv1/\left(1+\rho\right)\). The value of a feasible path is the limit, as \(n\to\infty\), of the partial sums of \(U_0\) up to date \(n\), where that limit exists in \(\left[-\infty,\infty\right]\), and is finite when the limit is a real number; an optimal path is a feasible path whose value exists and is at least the value of every feasible path whose value exists.

The economies (E). An economy (E) is Problem 1 with these primitives and initial stocks, every other primitive being any that Section 2 allows: a floor \(\bar R>0\); for \(R\ge\bar R\), output \(\mathcal F=A_0\Gamma^te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(R^e\right)^{\gamma}\) with \(A_0>0\), \(\Gamma>1\), \(\kappa\ge0\), \(\beta_K,\gamma>0\) and \(\beta_K+\gamma<1\), and \(F=0\) below the floor; the yield \(a\left(x\right)=1-e^{-\xi x}\) with \(\xi>0\); \(\phi^I=0\) and \(\omega^C>0\); a collection charge \(c^c>0\) and a treatment cost \(c^T\), both constant in time; a decay rate \(\theta\) continuous with values in \(\left(0,1\right)\); CRRA utility \(u\left(C\right)=C^{1-\eta}/\left(1-\eta\right)\) with \(\eta>1\), so that \(u<0\) on \(\left(0,\infty\right)\) and \(u\left(0\right)=-\infty\); a disutility \(v\) real-valued, continuous and nondecreasing on \(P\ge0\); and the initial stocks \(S_0=0\), \(X_0=\bar X_{\max}\), \(M^K_0=0\), \(\mathcal W_0>0\), \(P_0\ge0\) and \(K_0>0\). Write \(\bar P\equiv P_0+\mathcal W_0\). As \(\gamma>0\), \(\mathcal F\) vanishes at \(R^e=0\), so \(F\) is continuous in \(\left(K^Y,R,P\right)\) on \(\left[0,\infty\right)^3\) at every date; and \(c^T\), being differentiable, is continuous.

Lemma Z. Take an economy (E) and a path that meets the transitions and the ledger of Problem 1, the domains \(N,D,K^R,W\ge0\) and \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\), and the mass restrictions; every feasible path does. At every date:

(a) \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(S_t=0\), \(X_t=\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t=0\), so \(C^N_t=C^D_t=0\), \(\mathcal N_t=0\) and \(W_t=R_t=R^R_t\);

(b) \(0\le R^R_t\le T_t\le H_t\) and \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\mathcal W_t-\left(H_t-R^R_t\right)\), so \(\mathcal W_t\) is nonnegative and nonincreasing;

(c) \(0\le\Xi^h_t\le\mathcal W_t-\mathcal W_{t+1}\) and \(0\le P_t\le\bar P\).

Proof.

  1. As \(X_{t+1}=X_t+D_t\le\bar X_{\max}\) with \(D_t\ge0\) and \(X_0=\bar X_{\max}\), induction gives \(D_t=0\) and \(X_t=\bar X_{\max}\). Then \(S_{t+1}=S_t-N_t\ge0\) with \(N_t\ge0\) and \(S_0=0\) gives \(N_t=0\) and \(S_t=0\). Section 2 sets \(C^N\left(0,S,t\right)=C^D\left(0,X,t\right)=0\), and \(\mathcal N=\Omega^{N,S}N+\Omega^{D,S}D=0\). With \(\phi^I=0\) the transition of embodied material reads \(M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t\), so \(M^K_t=0\), and the ledger gives \(W_t=R_t\), which is \(R^R_t\) as \(N_t=0\).

  2. With \(W_t=R^R_t\), the waste-stock transition reads \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+R^R_t=\mathcal W_t-\left(H_t-R^R_t\right)\). Let \(\mathcal W_t\ge0\), as at \(t=0\). Then \(H_t\ge0\) and \(T_t=\varpi_tH_t\in\left[0,H_t\right]\), and \(R^R_t\in\left[0,T_t\right]\), as \(\mathcal R\left(T,K\right)=\left(1-e^{-\xi K/T}\right)T\le T\) for \(T>0\) and \(\mathcal R\left(0,K\right)=0\). So \(\mathcal W_{t+1}\ge\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t\ge0\) and \(\mathcal W_{t+1}\le\mathcal W_t\), and induction completes (b).

  3. By (b), \(\Xi^h_t=H_t-T_t+d^W\left(T_t-R^R_t\right)\ge0\), and \(\Xi^h_t=H_t-R^R_t-\left(1-d^W\right)\left(T_t-R^R_t\right)\le H_t-R^R_t=\mathcal W_t-\mathcal W_{t+1}\), as \(d^W<1\). As \(\theta\in\left(0,1\right)\) and \(\mathcal N_t=0\), \(P_{t+1}=\left[1-\theta\left(P_t\right)\right]P_t+\Xi^h_t\) lies between \(0\) and \(P_t+\Xi^h_t\) when \(P_t\ge0\). By induction \(0\le P_t\le P_0+\sum_{s<t}\left(\mathcal W_s-\mathcal W_{s+1}\right)=P_0+\mathcal W_0-\mathcal W_t\le\bar P\).

A feasible path with a finite value

Lemma W. Let an economy (E) have constants \(x_0\ge1/\xi\) and \(k>0\) with \(K_0=k+x_0\mu\mathcal W_0\), \[ b\equiv\frac{\mu e^{-\xi x_0}}{1-\Gamma^{-1}}<1, \qquad \underline R\equiv\left(1-e^{-\xi x_0}\right)\left(1-b\right)\mu\mathcal W_0>\bar R , \] and \[ \underline C\equiv A_0e^{-\kappa\bar P}\left(\underline R-\bar R\right)^{\gamma}k^{\beta_K}-\delta k-\mu\mathcal W_0\left(\delta x_0+\frac{\ln\Gamma}{\xi}\right)-\left[c^c+c^T\left(1\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W_0\;>\;0 . \] Then Problem 1 has a feasible path with \(R_t\ge\underline R\) and \(C_t\ge\underline C\) at every date, so that every date operates, and its value is finite.

Proof. The path. Let \(x_t\equiv x_0+t\ln\Gamma/\xi\), so that \(e^{-\xi x_t}=e^{-\xi x_0}\Gamma^{-t}\). At every date set \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=1\), \(K^R_t=x_t\mu\mathcal W_t\) and \(K_{t+1}=k+x_{t+1}\mu\mathcal W_{t+1}\), with \(I_t\equiv K_{t+1}-K_t\), \(C_t\) from the goods constraint, \(W_t=R_t\), \(\Omega_t\equiv R_t/\mathcal D_t\), and the states from the transitions. With \(\varpi_t=1\) the treated flow is \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\) and capital per treated tonne is \(x_t\), so \(R_t=R^R_t=\left(1-e^{-\xi x_t}\right)\mu\mathcal W_t\) and \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu e^{-\xi x_t}\right)\mathcal W_t\), which is positive as \(\mu e^{-\xi x_t}<1\). So \(\mathcal W_{t+1}\), and with it \(K_{t+1}\), is fixed at date \(t\), and the construction is recursive. As \(K_0=k+x_0\mu\mathcal W_0\), \(K_t=k+K^R_t\) and \(K^Y_t=k>0\) at every date. The path meets the transitions, the ledger, the domains of \(N\), \(D\), \(K^R\), \(W\) and \(\varpi\), and the mass restrictions, \(S\), \(X\) and \(M^K\) staying at their initial values; so Lemma Z applies to it.

The waste stock and material input. With \(b_s\equiv\mu e^{-\xi x_s}\in\left(0,1\right)\), \(\sum_{s\ge0}b_s=\mu e^{-\xi x_0}\sum_{s\ge0}\Gamma^{-s}=b\). As \(\prod_{s<t}\left(1-b_s\right)\ge1-\sum_{s<t}b_s\), by induction from \(\left(1-B\right)\left(1-b_t\right)\ge1-B-b_t\) for \(B\ge0\), \(\left(1-b\right)\mathcal W_0\le\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal W_0\). As \(x_t\ge x_0\), \(R_t\ge\left(1-e^{-\xi x_0}\right)\left(1-b\right)\mu\mathcal W_0=\underline R>\bar R\): every date operates, with \(R^e_t\ge\underline R-\bar R>0\).

Output, handling and capital formation. By Lemma Z(c), \(P_t\le\bar P\), so \(Y_t=A_0\Gamma^te^{-\kappa P_t}k^{\beta_K}\left(R^e_t\right)^{\gamma}\ge\underline Y\Gamma^t\), with \(\underline Y\equiv A_0e^{-\kappa\bar P}\left(\underline R-\bar R\right)^{\gamma}k^{\beta_K}\). By Lemma Z(a), \(C^N_t=C^D_t=0\), and the handling cost is \(C^W_t=\left[c^c+c^T\left(1\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W_t\le\left[c^c+c^T\left(1\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W_0\). Gross capital formation is \(G_t=K_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)K_t=\delta k+\mu\left[x_{t+1}\mathcal W_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)x_t\mathcal W_t\right]\). As \(\mathcal W_{t+1}\le\mathcal W_t\) and \(x_{t+1}=x_t+\ln\Gamma/\xi\), \[ G_t\;\le\;\delta k+\mu\mathcal W_t\left(\delta x_t+\frac{\ln\Gamma}{\xi}\right)\;\le\;\delta k+\mu\mathcal W_0\left(\delta x_0+\frac{\ln\Gamma}{\xi}\right)+t\,\delta\mu\mathcal W_0\frac{\ln\Gamma}{\xi} . \]

Consumption is at least \(\underline C\). The goods constraint gives \(C_t=Y_t-G_t-C^W_t\). With \(\Gamma^t\ge1+t\ln\Gamma\), \[ C_t\;\ge\;\underline C+t\ln\Gamma\left(\underline Y-\frac{\delta\mu\mathcal W_0}{\xi}\right). \] The hypothesis \(\underline C>0\) gives \(\underline Y>\delta\mu\mathcal W_0x_0\ge\delta\mu\mathcal W_0/\xi\), as \(x_0\ge1/\xi\), so the second term is nonnegative and \(C_t\ge\underline C>0\).

The path is feasible. The goods constraint holds by the definition of \(C_t\), and the ledger by \(W_t=R_t\ge0\) with \(M^K=0\). The intensity condition is \(R_t=\Omega_t\mathcal D_t\), as \(\phi^I=0\); every term of \(\mathcal D_t\) is nonnegative, so \(\mathcal D_t\ge\omega^CC_t>0\), and \(\Omega_t=R_t/\mathcal D_t\ge0\) meets it. The domains hold, with \(C_t>0\), \(0\le K^R_t<K_t\) and \(K_{t+1}>0\); the transitions and the mass restrictions hold by construction.

The value is finite. As \(u\) is increasing and negative, \(u\left(\underline C\right)\le u\left(C_t\right)<0\), and \(v\left(0\right)\le v\left(P_t\right)\le v\left(\bar P\right)\). So both \(\sum_t\beta^tu\left(C_t\right)\) and \(\sum_t\beta^tv\left(P_t\right)\) converge absolutely, and the value is a real number.

An optimal path

Lemma E. If some feasible path of an economy (E) has a finite value, Problem 1 has an optimal path.

Proof. Every feasible path has a value. By Lemma Z(c), \(0\le P_t\le\bar P\), so with \(\bar v\equiv\max_{\left[0,\bar P\right]}\left|v\right|\), finite as \(v\) is continuous, \(\sum_t\beta^tv\left(P_t\right)\) converges absolutely. As \(u<0\) on \(\left(0,\infty\right)\) and \(u\left(0\right)=-\infty\), the partial sums of \(\sum_t\beta^tu\left(C_t\right)\) are nonincreasing and converge in \(\left[-\infty,0\right]\). So the value exists, and it is at most \(-v\left(0\right)/\left(1-\beta\right)\), as \(v\) is nondecreasing.

A relaxed problem. A programme is a sequence of reals \(\pi=\left(C_t,K_{t+1},\varpi_t,K^R_t\right)_{t\ge0}\). Given \(\pi\) and the initial stocks, \(K_0\) among them and \(K_t\) for \(t\ge1\) a coordinate of \(\pi\), define date by date \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\), \(T_t=\varpi_tH_t\), \(R_t=\mathcal R\left(T_t,K^R_t\right)\), \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\mathcal W_t-\left(H_t-R_t\right)\), \(\Xi^h_t=H_t-R_t-\left(1-d^W\right)\left(T_t-R_t\right)\), \(P_{t+1}=\left[1-\theta\left(P_t\right)\right]P_t+\Xi^h_t\), \(Y_t=F\left(K_t-K^R_t,R_t,P_t,t\right)\) and \(C^W_t=\left[c^c\varpi_t+c^T\left(\varpi_t\right)\right]H_t\). Let \(\mathbb S\) be the set of programmes that satisfy, at every date, \[ C_t\ge0,\quad K_{t+1}\ge0,\quad\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right],\quad0\le K^R_t\le K_t,\quad C_t\le g_t\left(\pi\right)\equiv Y_t+\left(1-\delta\right)K_t-K_{t+1}-C^W_t . \] With \(N=D=0\), \(W=R\) and \(S\), \(X\) and \(M^K\) at their initial values, a programme in \(\mathbb S\) defines a path that meets the hypotheses of Lemma Z, so its conclusions (b) and (c) hold on it. A feasible path has \(N=D=0\), \(C^N=C^D=0\), \(W=R\) and these flows, by Lemma Z(a), so its programme is in \(\mathbb S\), with \(C_t=g_t\) the goods constraint, and its value is \[ U\left(\pi\right)\;\equiv\;\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^tu\left(C_t\right)-\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^tv\left(P_t\right). \]

Every coordinate lies in a compact interval. On \(\mathbb S\), \(R_t\le H_t\le\mu\mathcal W_0\) by Lemma Z(b), and output is zero below the floor, so \(Y_t\le A_0\Gamma^tK_t^{\beta_K}\left(\mu\mathcal W_0\right)^{\gamma}\), as \(K^Y_t\le K_t\), \(R^e_t\le R_t\) and \(e^{-\kappa P_t}\le1\). As \(C_t\ge0\) and \(C^W_t\ge0\), \(K_{t+1}\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t+Y_t\). Let \(\bar K_0\equiv K_0\), \(\bar Y_t\equiv A_0\Gamma^t\bar K_t^{\beta_K}\left(\mu\mathcal W_0\right)^{\gamma}\) and \(\bar K_{t+1}\equiv\left(1-\delta\right)\bar K_t+\bar Y_t\); the map \(K\mapsto\left(1-\delta\right)K+A_0\Gamma^tK^{\beta_K}\left(\mu\mathcal W_0\right)^{\gamma}\) is nondecreasing, so by induction \(K_t\le\bar K_t\). Hence at every date \(0\le C_t\le g_t\le\bar Y_t+\bar K_t\), \(0\le K_{t+1}\le\bar K_{t+1}\), \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\) and \(0\le K^R_t\le\bar K_t\): each coordinate of a programme in \(\mathbb S\) lies in a compact interval that depends on its date alone.

Limits stay in \(\mathbb S\). Every sequence of programmes in \(\mathbb S\) has a subsequence that converges coordinate by coordinate: extract one along which the first coordinate converges, from it one along which the second does, and so on, and take the diagonal. Every flow and state of date \(t\) is a continuous function of finitely many coordinates, by induction on \(t\): \(\mathcal R\) is continuous on \(\left[0,\infty\right)^2\), since \(\mathcal R\left(T,K\right)=\left(1-e^{-\xi K/T}\right)T\) for \(T>0\) and \(0\le\mathcal R\left(T,K\right)\le T\); and \(\theta\), \(c^T\) and \(F\) are continuous. So each inequality that defines \(\mathbb S\) is preserved in the limit, and a coordinatewise limit of programmes in \(\mathbb S\) is in \(\mathbb S\).

The value is upper semicontinuous. Let programmes \(\pi^n\) in \(\mathbb S\) converge coordinate by coordinate to \(\pi\). Each \(P^n_t\to P_t\), and \(\left|\beta^tv\left(P^n_t\right)\right|\le\beta^t\bar v\), so \(\sum_t\beta^tv\left(P^n_t\right)\to\sum_t\beta^tv\left(P_t\right)\), the tails being uniformly small. For every \(m\), \(\sum_{t\le m}\beta^tu\left(C^n_t\right)\to\sum_{t\le m}\beta^tu\left(C_t\right)\) in \(\left[-\infty,0\right)\), as \(u\) is continuous from \(\left[0,\infty\right)\) to \(\left[-\infty,0\right)\), with \(u\left(C\right)\to-\infty\) as \(C\to0\). As \(u<0\), \(\sum_t\beta^tu\left(C^n_t\right)\le\sum_{t\le m}\beta^tu\left(C^n_t\right)\), so \(\limsup_n\sum_t\beta^tu\left(C^n_t\right)\le\sum_{t\le m}\beta^tu\left(C_t\right)\) for every \(m\). Letting \(m\to\infty\), \(\limsup_nU\left(\pi^n\right)\le U\left(\pi\right)\).

A maximiser. Let \(\bar U\equiv\sup_{\pi\in\mathbb S}U\left(\pi\right)\). The feasible path with a finite value gives \(\bar U>-\infty\), and \(\bar U\le-v\left(0\right)/\left(1-\beta\right)\). Take \(\pi^n\) in \(\mathbb S\) with \(U\left(\pi^n\right)\to\bar U\), and a subsequence converging to some \(\pi^*\), which is in \(\mathbb S\). Then \(U\left(\pi^*\right)\ge\limsup_nU\left(\pi^n\right)=\bar U\), so \(U\left(\pi^*\right)=\bar U\). As \(\bar U>-\infty\), \(C^*_t>0\) at every date. And \(C^*_t=g_t\left(\pi^*\right)\) at every date: otherwise, raising \(C^*_t\) to \(g_t\left(\pi^*\right)\) gives a programme in \(\mathbb S\), since \(C_t\) enters no flow and no inequality of \(\mathbb S\) but its own two, with a strictly larger \(U\), \(u\) being increasing.

The maximiser is an optimal path. Complete \(\pi^*\) with \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(I_t\equiv K^*_{t+1}-K^*_t\), \(W_t\equiv R_t\) and \(\Omega_t\equiv R_t/\mathcal D_t\), where \(\mathcal D_t\ge\omega^CC^*_t>0\). The goods constraint is \(C^*_t=g_t\left(\pi^*\right)\) with \(C^N=C^D=0\); the ledger holds with \(M^K=0\); the intensity condition holds with \(\Omega_t\ge0\), as \(\phi^I=0\); the transitions and the domains hold; and \(S\), \(X\) and \(M^K\) stay at their initial values, which meet the mass restrictions. So \(\pi^*\), completed, is a feasible path, with value \(\bar U\). Every feasible path has a value, \(U\) of its programme, which is at most \(\bar U\). So the completed \(\pi^*\) is an optimal path.
