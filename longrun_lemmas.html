Shared lemmas: the first-order conditions, the pollution price and the material budget

This page holds what several proofs of this appendix share: the facts they take from the paper’s model and its long-run section, the first-order conditions of Problem 1 they use, and two lemmas, Lemma P on the sign of the pollution price and Lemma A on what the material budget bounds. The proofs of O.1 to O.6 and O.9 use it. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol a proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

What the proofs use

From the paper’s model. A feasible path meets every constraint of Problem 1: the goods constraint, the ledger, the intensity condition and the transitions, with the flows defined as in Section 2, the domains \(C,N,D,K^R,W,\Omega\ge0\), \(K_{t+1}\ge0\) and \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\), and the mass restrictions \(S_t\ge0\), \(X_t\le\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t\ge0\). Capital is split between production and recycling, \(K=K^Y+K^R\), so \(K^R_t\le K_t\) and \(K^Y_t=K_t-K^R_t\) is the capital input of \(F\). We impose that the initial stocks are nonnegative. The paper takes \(\theta\) continuously differentiable, so \(\theta'\) is bounded on every bounded interval; only Lemma P uses this. Gross capital formation \(G\) is free in sign, Problem 1 restricting only \(K_{t+1}\ge0\).

Write \(\mathcal M_t\equiv M^K_t+\mathcal W_t\) and \(a_t\equiv a\left(x_t,t\right)\). Since \(R^R_t=a_t\varpi_tH_t\), the collected ledger of Section 2 reads \[ \mathcal M_{t+1}\;=\;\mathcal M_t+N_t-\left(H_t-R^R_t\right), \qquad H_t-R^R_t=\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t . \tag{L} \] The displaced mass \(\mathcal N\equiv\Omega^{N,S}N+\Omega^{D,S}D\), with \(\Omega^{N,S},\Omega^{D,S}\ge0\), is in neither stock: Section 2 sends it to the environment in the period it is displaced, and the flow reaching the environment is \(\Xi=\Xi^h+\mathcal N\), with the handled stream’s emission \(\Xi^h\equiv\left[1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\left(1-a\right)\right]H\). The waste-stock transition with \(W_t\ge0\) keeps \(\mathcal W_t\ge0\), so \(\mathcal M_t\ge0\). At a date with no treated flow, \(\varpi_tH_t=0\), nothing is recovered, \(R^R_t=0\), and we set \(a_t\varpi_t\equiv0\). Every cost flow is nonnegative: \(C^N\left(0,S,t\right)=0\) with \(C^N_N>0\), likewise \(C^D\), and \(c^c>0\), \(c^T\ge0\).

From the paper’s long-run section. The material budget is \[ \mathcal B\;\equiv\;\bar N+M^K_0+\mathcal W_0 , \qquad \bar N\;\equiv\;S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0 , \] with \(\bar N\) the virgin budget, and the loop closes on a path when \(\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)<\infty\). A variable \(Z\) grows at rate \(g\) when \(Z_{t+1}/Z_t\to1+g\). The operating set is \(\mathcal O\equiv\left\{t:R_t\ge\bar R\right\}\).

  • State A (Definition 1): “collapse if \(\mathcal O\) is finite: production ceases after a finite total number of operating periods, output is zero thereafter, and consumption, financed from the capital stock alone, converges to zero”. After the last operating date output is zero, so the goods constraint gives \(K_{t+1}\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\) and \(C_t\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\), and capital and consumption tend to zero: a path with a finite operating set meets the whole clause.
  • State C (Definition 1): “if, asymptotically, extraction and discovery die, \(N,D\to0\) with \(\sum_tN_t<\infty\); material input converges to a positive level met entirely by recycling, \(R\to R_\infty>\bar R\) with \(R^R\to R_\infty\); the loop closes, \(a\varpi\to1\) with \(x\to\infty\); the two anthropogenic stocks converge; and the goods block grows at a common rate \(g>0\).” The goods block is output, capital and consumption, as the notation glosses \(g\). Part (a) uses the growth of output only; the construction of part (b) delivers all three.
  • Available, candidate: “A state is available in a cell when the cell’s primitives and the initial stocks admit a feasible path of Problem 1 in it […]. An available path that also satisfies the first-order conditions is a candidate.” The conditions used are listed in The first-order conditions used.
  • Assumption 1: “(i) Trending technology. For \(R\ge\bar R\), \(F\left(K^Y,R,P,t\right)=A_t\,\tilde{\mathcal F}\left(K^Y,B_tR^e,P\right)\) with \(A_{t+1}/A_t=1+g_A\), \(B_{t+1}/B_t=1+g_B\), \(g_A,g_B\ge0\) and \(g_A+g_B>0\). Every other primitive converges, uniformly on compact sets, to a stationary limit with the properties of Section 2, and \(\mu\) is constant. (ii) Convergence. Along the paths we classify, every variable converges after detrending: goods-block variables grow at constant asymptotic rates, and material flows and stocks decay at constant rates or converge.” Its part (iii) is not used. Part (i) gives the yield function a limit \(a^\infty\) that is increasing with \(a^\infty\left(x\right)<1\) at every finite \(x\), and since \(a\) is nondecreasing in \(t\), \(a\left(x,t\right)\le a^\infty\left(x\right)\) for all \(x\) and \(t\).

The first-order conditions used. Appendix A of the paper states the first-order conditions of Problem 1 with three provisions. The wedge provision: “Every sign asserted in this appendix holds under the wedge conditions \(1-\zeta\Omega^Y>0\) and \(1+\zeta\Omega^j>0\) for every use \(j\)”, inequalities on endogenous objects “which we maintain wherever a sign depends on them”. The Inada provision: “Consumption is interior by the Inada condition on \(u\)”. The pricing provision: “under the transversality condition \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta^t\lambda_tm_tZ_{t+1}=0\) for a stock \(Z\) with costate \(m\) each price is the discounted stream of the dividends the stock pays while it survives, with \(\Lambda_{t,s}\equiv\prod_{j=t+1}^{s}\left(1+r_j\right)^{-1}\)”, where \(1+r_{t+1}\equiv\lambda_t/\left(\beta\lambda_{t+1}\right)\). A candidate is read with the wedge and Inada provisions and with the transversality condition. The proofs use one consequence of the discounted stream, the sign of the pollution price, and Lemma P derives it from the transversality condition. Pollution “survives at the marginal retention factor \(1-\theta-\theta'P\)”, which we write \(\chi_t\equiv1-\theta\left(P_t\right)-\theta'\left(P_t\right)P_t\).

The Lagrangian of Problem 1 adjoins, in period \(t\) and with weight \(\beta^t\), the goods constraint at \(\lambda_t\), the ledger written with \(W\) on the left at \(\lambda_tp^W_t\), the intensity condition written with \(R\) on the left at \(\lambda_t\zeta_t\), and the pollution transition at \(-\lambda_tp^P_t\) (Appendix A.2). Consumption enters the period-\(t\) terms through \(u\left(C_t\right)\), the goods constraint, and \(\mathcal D_t\) with the weight \(\omega^C\), so its interior first-order condition is \[ u'\left(C_t\right)=\lambda_t\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^C_t\right), \tag{C} \] and \(u'>0\) with the wedge condition \(1+\zeta_t\Omega^C_t>0\) gives \(\lambda_t>0\) at every date; hence \(1+r_t>0\) and \(\Lambda_{t,s}>0\). The two recycling controls \(K^R_t\) and \(\varpi_t\) enter the period-\(t\) terms only through \[ \lambda_t\Big\{Y_t-C^W_t-p^W_tR_t+\zeta_t\left[R_t-\Omega_t\left(\mathcal D_t+\phi^IG_t\right)\right]-p^P_t\Xi_t\Big\}, \] with \(Y=F\left(K-K^R,R,P,t\right)\), \(R=N+a\left(x,t\right)\varpi H\) and \(x=K^R/\left(\varpi H\right)\), \(C^W=\left[c^c\varpi+c^T\left(\varpi\right)\right]H\), \(\Xi=\Xi^h+\mathcal N\), of which only \(\Xi^h=\left[1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\left(1-a\right)\right]H\) moves with the two controls, and \(Y\) and \(C^W\) entering \(\mathcal D\) with the weights \(\omega^Y\) and \(\omega^W\). Differentiating, with \(\alpha=a-a'x\), \[ \frac{\partial R}{\partial K^R}=a',\quad \frac{\partial R}{\partial\varpi}=\alpha H,\quad \frac{\partial\Xi}{\partial K^R}=-d^Wa',\quad \frac{\partial\Xi}{\partial\varpi}=-\left[\left(1-d^W\right)+d^W\alpha\right]H,\quad \frac{\partial C^W}{\partial\varpi}=\left[c^c+c^{T\prime}\left(\varpi\right)\right]H, \] with \(\partial Y/\partial K^R=F_Ra'-F_K\) and \(\partial Y/\partial\varpi=F_R\alpha H\). Collecting terms with \(\Omega^j\equiv\Omega\omega^j\) and the gains of Appendix A, \[ \Psi\equiv F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)+\zeta, \qquad \mathcal G^K\equiv\Psi-p^W+d^Wp^P, \qquad \mathcal G^\varpi\equiv\alpha\left(\Psi-p^W\right)+p^P\left[\left(1-d^W\right)+d^W\alpha\right]=\alpha\,\mathcal G^K+\left(1-d^W\right)p^P , \tag{G} \] the derivative of the braces is \(a'\mathcal G^K-\tilde F_K\) in \(K^R\), with \(\tilde F_K\equiv F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\) the material-adjusted marginal product of capital, and \(H\left\{\mathcal G^\varpi-\left[c^c+c^{T\prime}\left(\varpi\right)\right]\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)\right\}\) in \(\varpi\). Dividing by \(\beta^t\lambda_t>0\), a candidate satisfies the recycling-capital and treatment margins \[ a'\left(x_t,t\right)\mathcal G^K_t=\tilde F_{K,t} \quad\text{at a date at which }K^R_t\text{ is interior, with }\varpi_tH_t>0, \tag{KR} \] \[ \mathcal G^\varpi_t=\left[c^c\left(t\right)+c^{T\prime}\left(\varpi_t,t\right)\right]\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right) \quad\text{at a date with }0<\varpi_t<1\text{ and }H_t>0 . \tag{T} \] The pollution stock \(P_{t+1}\) enters the Lagrangian through \(-\beta^{t+1}v\left(P_{t+1}\right)\), through \(Y_{t+1}\) in the goods constraint and in \(\mathcal D_{t+1}\) of period \(t+1\), and through the pollution transitions of periods \(t\) and \(t+1\), adjoined at \(-\lambda_tp^P_t\) and \(-\lambda_{t+1}p^P_{t+1}\). Its derivative is \[ -\beta^{t+1}v'\left(P_{t+1}\right)+\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}F_{P,t+1}\left(1-\zeta_{t+1}\Omega^Y_{t+1}\right)+\beta^t\lambda_tp^P_t-\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}\chi_{t+1}p^P_{t+1}=0 , \] and dividing by \(\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}>0\) gives the pollution costate recursion \[ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^P_t=\mathcal V_{t+1}+\chi_{t+1}p^P_{t+1}, \qquad \mathcal V\equiv\frac{v'\left(P\right)}{\lambda}-F_P\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right). \tag{PP} \] Iterated \(n\) times, (PP) reads \[ p^P_t=\sum_{s=t+1}^{t+n}\Lambda_{t,s}\Big[\prod_{t<i<s}\chi_i\Big]\mathcal V_s+\Lambda_{t,t+n}\Big[\prod_{t<i\le t+n}\chi_i\Big]p^P_{t+n}, \] and \(p^P_t\) is the discounted stream of the dividends \(\mathcal V\) the stock pays while it survives at the factors \(\chi\) exactly when the last term tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\).

Lemma P. On a candidate along which pollution is positive at some date, \(p^P_t>0\) at every date; in particular \[ p^P_t\;\ge\;0 . \tag{P} \]

Proof. The terms are positive. \(\Lambda_{t,s}>0\) by (C). \(\mathcal V_s>0\): \(v'>0\) and \(\lambda_s>0\) make its first term positive, and its second is nonnegative because \(F_P\le0\), with \(\mathcal F_P<0\) at an operating date and \(F=0\) below the floor, and \(1-\zeta_s\Omega^Y_s>0\) by the wedge condition. \(\chi_i>0\), because \(\theta<1\), \(\theta'<0\) and \(P_i\ge0\); the pollution stock stays nonnegative from \(P_0\ge0\), since \(\Xi\ge0\) and \(\theta<1\). So the partial sums of the iterated (PP) increase with \(n\).

A nonpositive price forces \(P_{s+1}/\chi_{t,s}\to0\). Suppose \(p^P_t\le0\) at some date \(t\). The last term of the iterated (PP) is \(p^P_t\) less the partial sum, so it is at most \(-\Lambda_{t,t+1}\mathcal V_{t+1}\) for every \(n\ge1\). Write \(\chi_{t,s}\equiv\prod_{t<i\le s}\chi_i\), with \(\chi_{t,t}\equiv1\). Since \(\Lambda_{t,s}=\beta^{s-t}\lambda_s/\lambda_t\) and \(\beta^t\lambda_t\Lambda_{t,t+1}=\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}\), this reads \[ \beta^s\lambda_s\left(-p^P_s\right)\chi_{t,s}\;\ge\;\beta^{t+1}\lambda_{t+1}\mathcal V_{t+1}\;>\;0 \qquad\left(s>t\right). \] The costate of pollution is \(-\lambda p^P\), so its transversality condition reads \(\beta^s\lambda_sp^P_sP_{s+1}\to0\). Multiplying the display by \(P_{s+1}/\chi_{t,s}\), it forces \(P_{s+1}/\chi_{t,s}\to0\).

But \(P_{s+1}/\chi_{t,s}\) is bounded away from zero. The stock loses at most its average rate, \(P_{i+1}\ge\left[1-\theta\left(P_i\right)\right]P_i\), while the price survives at the marginal factor \(\chi_i=1-\theta\left(P_i\right)+\left|\theta'\left(P_i\right)\right|P_i\). Take a date \(u>t\) with \(P_u>0\), which the hypothesis provides, since a positive stock stays positive. For every \(s\ge u\), using \(1+y\le e^y\) and \(1-\theta\left(P_i\right)\ge1-\theta\left(0\right)>0\), \(\theta\) being decreasing, \[ \frac{P_{s+1}}{\chi_{t,s}}\;\ge\;\frac{P_u}{\chi_{t,u-1}}\prod_{i=u}^{s}\frac{1-\theta\left(P_i\right)}{\chi_i}\;\ge\;\frac{P_u}{\chi_{t,u-1}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{1-\theta\left(0\right)}\sum_{i\ge u}\left|\theta'\left(P_i\right)\right|P_i\Big). \] The sum is finite. By Lemma A(a) and (b), which use no first-order condition, and \(\Xi^h_i\le\left(1-a_i\varpi_i\right)H_i\), a consequence of \(d^W<1\), cumulated emissions are bounded, \(\sum_i\Xi_i\le\bar\Xi\equiv\mathcal B+\Omega^{N,S}\bar N+\Omega^{D,S}\left(\bar X_{\max}-X_0\right)\). So \(P_i\le P_0+\bar\Xi\), \(\theta\left(P_i\right)\ge\underline\theta\equiv\theta\left(P_0+\bar\Xi\right)>0\) and \(P_{i+1}\le\left(1-\underline\theta\right)P_i+\Xi_i\) at every date, and summing over \(i\le n\) gives \(\underline\theta\sum_{i\le n}P_i\le P_0+\bar\Xi\). Hence \(P_i\to0\) with \(\sum_iP_i<\infty\); as \(\theta'\) is bounded near zero, \(\left|\theta'\left(P_i\right)\right|\) is bounded from some date on, and \(\sum_i\left|\theta'\left(P_i\right)\right|P_i<\infty\). So \(P_{s+1}/\chi_{t,s}\) is bounded away from zero for \(s\ge u\), against the transversality condition, and \(p^P_t>0\).

The hypothesis of Lemma P holds on every path with \(P_0>0\) or with a positive handled flow at some date: a handled tonne emits, \(\Xi^h_s>0\) when \(H_s>0\), since \(d^W>0\) and \(a<1\) at every finite intensity, and then \(P_{s+1}>0\).

What the material budget bounds

Lemma A. On every feasible path:

(a) \(\sum_{t\ge0}N_t\le S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0\) and \(\sum_{t\ge0}D_t\le\bar X_{\max}-X_0\); so \(N_t\to0\).

(b) \(\mathcal M_{n+1}+\sum_{t=0}^{n}\left(H_t-R^R_t\right)\le\mathcal B\) for every \(n\ge0\); so \(\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal M_t\le\mathcal B\) at every date, \(\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t\le\mathcal B\), and \(R_t\le N_t+\mu\mathcal B\).

(c) If \(R_t\to R_\infty>0\), then \(H_t\ge R_\infty/2\) from some date \(T_2\) on and \(\sum_{t\ge T_2}\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)\le2\mathcal B/R_\infty\): the loop closes.

Proof.

  1. Summing \(X_{t+1}=X_t+D_t\) and \(S_{t+1}=S_t+D_t-N_t\) over \(t<n\) gives \(\sum_{t<n}D_t=X_n-X_0\le\bar X_{\max}-X_0\) and \(\sum_{t<n}N_t=S_0-S_n+X_n-X_0\le S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0\), by \(S_n\ge0\) and \(X_n\le\bar X_{\max}\). A series of nonnegative terms with bounded partial sums converges, and its terms tend to zero.

  2. Summing (L) over \(t\le n\), \[ \mathcal M_{n+1}+\sum_{t\le n}\left(H_t-R^R_t\right)=\mathcal M_0+\sum_{t\le n}N_t\;\le\;\mathcal M_0+\bar N=\mathcal B \] by (a). Both terms on the left are nonnegative, since \(\mathcal M_{n+1}\ge0\) and \(R^R_t=a_t\varpi_tH_t\le H_t\). The last claim is \(R_t=N_t+R^R_t\) with the throughput cap of Section 2, \(R^R_t\le\bar a\mu\mathcal W_t\le\mu\mathcal B\).

  3. By (a), \(R^R_t=R_t-N_t\to R_\infty\), so \(H_t\ge R^R_t\ge R_\infty/2\) for all \(t\) from some \(T_2\) on, and by (b) \(\tfrac12R_\infty\sum_{t\ge T_2}\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)\le\sum_{t\ge T_2}\left(H_t-R^R_t\right)\le\mathcal B\).
