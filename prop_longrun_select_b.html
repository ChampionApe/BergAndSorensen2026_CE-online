O.5 Selection against balanced dematerialization

Proposition 8 of the paper (selection against balanced dematerialization) states that in the floorless Cobb-Douglas workhorse a path in state B is beaten by a late switch to full treatment, so that no optimal path with a finite value ends in state B. This page proves it in full and adds a corollary of its own, the growth gap, which compares the growth of a state-B path of the workhorse, at the rates of the paper’s Appendix B.6, with that of the closed loop. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

The statements

Proposition 8 (selection against balanced dematerialization). Take the Cobb-Douglas workhorse without a floor, under the soft ceiling with a stationary yield function, CRRA utility and \(\phi^I=0\). Every state-B path with \(\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}<1\) has a finite value and is strictly improved on by a path that copies it until a late date and then treats all handled waste, with enough added recycling capital that less of the waste stock is lost each period, provided either (a) \(\left(1-\nu\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta-1}<1\), for any yield with the soft ceiling, or (b) \(g>0\) and the yield tail is at least as thin as exponential. With the exponential yield, (a) or (b) holds for every state-B path with \(\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}<1\), so no optimal path with a finite value is in state B, except at the knife-edge \(\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}=1\).

The growth gap (a corollary of the workhorse’s rates, Appendix B.6). Every state-B path of the workhorse grows more slowly than the closed loop and faster than its matter decays, \(1-\nu<1+g<1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\). For a candidate with converging recycling margins the production function and the extraction margin fix the rates jointly (Appendix B.6), and its growth rate \(g^{\mathrm{dem}}\) satisfies \[ 1+g^{\mathrm{dem}}=\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{\frac{\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)}{\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)+\gamma}}\;<\;1+g^{\mathrm{circ}} \qquad\text{for }\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)+\gamma>0 . \]

In the growth gap, \(\mu_N\) is the elasticity with which the workhorse’s marginal extraction cost rises as the reserve falls (Section 3).

Three cases are not covered by the proposition: tails slower than exponential where \(\eta>1\), \(g>0\) and (a) fails, some power tails among them; material input decaying more slowly than any geometric rate, or \(R\) decaying at a rate other than that of \(\mathcal W\); and \(\phi^I>0\), where the switch changes the storage share.

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions. The proposition compares feasible paths, and we prove it by building the switch and bounding the difference in value, with no first-order condition. The Cobb-Douglas form enters the comparison of output across the two paths and the growth gap; the thin tail enters case (b) only; and one property of pollution decay, that it does not tip near zero, enters case (a) only.

What the proof uses

From the shared page. The shared lemmas for the long-run proofs restate the paper’s model as these proofs use it. We use the definition of a feasible path given there: the constraints and domains of Problem 1, the three restrictions on masses \(S_t\ge0\), \(X_t\le\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t\ge0\), nonnegative initial stocks, \(K^R_t\le K_t\), and gross capital formation free in sign. We use the nonnegativity of every cost flow, the material budget \(\mathcal B\equiv\bar N+M^K_0+\mathcal W_0\) with \(\bar N\equiv S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0\), and Lemma A: (a) \(\sum_tN_t\le\bar N\) and \(\sum_tD_t\le\bar X_{\max}-X_0\), so \(N_t\to0\), and \(D_t\to0\) likewise; (b) \(\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal B\) at every date and \(\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t\le\mathcal B\). The proof of Lemma A uses only the material constraints: the ledger, \(R=N+a\varpi H\), the transitions of \(S\), \(X\), \(M^K\) and \(\mathcal W\), the domains \(N,D,K^R,W\ge0\) and \(\varpi\in\left[0,1\right]\), the restrictions on masses and nonnegative initial stocks. We call a path that meets these and the pollution transition materially feasible; Lemma A holds on it.

From the paper’s model (Section 2). The flow reaching the environment is \(\Xi=\Xi^h+\mathcal N\), with \(\Xi^h\equiv\left[1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\left(1-a\right)\right]H\) the emission of the handled stream, \(0<d^W<1\), and \(\mathcal N=\Omega^{N,S}N+\Omega^{D,S}D\) the displaced mass, \(\Omega^{N,S},\Omega^{D,S}\ge0\). Both handling charges are non-increasing in time, so the handling cost per handled tonne at full treatment satisfies \(c^c\left(t\right)+c^T\left(1,t\right)\le c^{\mathrm{full}}\equiv c^c\left(0\right)+c^T\left(1,0\right)\), finite because \(c^T\) is convex with \(c^T\left(0\right)=0\) and \(c^{T\prime}\left(1\right)<\infty\). We read the relative intensities as nonnegative, \(\omega^j\ge0\), each use releasing waste. We write \(\mathcal P\left(P\right)\equiv\left[1-\theta\left(P\right)\right]P\), so the pollution transition is \(P_{t+1}=\mathcal P\left(P_t\right)+\Xi_t\).

From the paper’s long-run section (Section 3).

  • Rates: “A variable \(Z\) grows at rate \(g\) when \(Z_{t+1}/Z_t\to1+g\) and decays at rate \(\nu\in\left(0,1\right)\) when \(Z_{t+1}/Z_t\to1-\nu\)”; the gross factor is positive, \(1+g>0\) (the notation page, rule 8).
  • State B (Definition 1): “balanced dematerialization if \(C_t>0\) at every date and, asymptotically, \(C\), \(Y\) and \(K^Y\) grow at a common rate \(g\) with \(Y/C\) bounded above and away from zero, while \(R\) and \(\mathcal W\) decay at a common rate \(\nu\in\left(0,1\right)\)”. We call \(\left(g,\nu\right)\) the path’s rates. The ratios of consecutive terms being defined, \(Y\), \(K^Y\), \(R\) and \(\mathcal W\) are positive from some date on, and there are constants \(0<\underline o\le\bar o\) with \(\underline o\le Y_t/C_t\le\bar o\) from some date on.
  • The workhorse: output “\(A_te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR^e\right)^{\gamma}\) with \(\beta_K+\gamma<1\)”, where \(\beta_K,\gamma>0\) and \(\kappa\ge0\), and without a floor \(R^e=R\); the trends of Assumption 1(i), “\(A_{t+1}/A_t=1+g_A\), \(B_{t+1}/B_t=1+g_B\), \(g_A,g_B\ge0\) and \(g_A+g_B>0\)”, with “\(\Gamma\equiv\left(1+g_A\right)\left(1+g_B\right)^{\gamma}>1\)”; the circular rate \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}\) of “\(\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\Gamma\)”; “the exponential yield \(a\left(x\right)=\bar a\left(1-e^{-\xi x}\right)\)”, under the soft ceiling \(1-e^{-\xi x}\); and “CRRA utility”, \(u\left(C\right)=C^{1-\eta}/\left(1-\eta\right)\) with \(\eta>0\), and \(\ln C\) at \(\eta=1\). The workhorse’s extraction cost enters only through \(\mu_N\), in the growth gap.
  • A stationary yield function under the soft ceiling, which the proposition puts in place of the exponential yield: a function \(a\left(x\right)\) of the intensity alone with the properties of Section 2 at \(\bar a=1\), \(a\left(0\right)=0\), \(a'>0\), \(a''<0\) and \(a\left(x\right)\to1\). It is continuous and strictly increasing with \(a<1\), so its inverse \(a^{-1}:\left[0,1\right)\to\left[0,\infty\right)\) is defined.
  • Assumption 1(iii), from the paper’s statement of what it assumes about the long run: “Regeneration. \(\theta\left(P\right)\ge\theta_{\min}>0\) for all \(P\), and \(\rho+\theta\left(P\right)+\theta'\left(P\right)P>0\) on the relevant range.” The paper’s Appendix A says of the pollution price that where \(r+\theta+\theta'P\le0\) “the forward sum need not converge, this model’s tipping point, ruled out by a restriction on \(\theta\) named in Section 3”; that restriction is this second clause. It is the second route to the property case (a) uses near zero, and nothing else on this page uses it.

Readings of the statement.

  • The loss rate of the waste stock is \(\ell_t\equiv\mu\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)\in\left[0,\mu\right]\), so that \(\ell_t\mathcal W_t=\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t\) is the leakage flow and \(\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\mathcal W_t=a_t\varpi_tH_t\) the recovered flow; at a date with no treated flow \(a_t\varpi_t\equiv0\), as on the shared page. “Less of the waste stock is lost each period” is a lower loss rate.
  • The yield tail is at least as thin as exponential, a thin tail for short, when there are \(\xi>0\) and \(c_\xi\ge1\) with \(1-a\left(x\right)\le c_\xi e^{-\xi x}\) for all \(x\ge0\); the exponential yield has a thin tail with \(c_\xi=1\).
  • The value of a path is \(U_0=\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\beta^t\left[u\left(C_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right]\) where the limit exists in \(\left[-\infty,+\infty\right]\), and an optimal path is a feasible path whose value is the supremum of values over feasible paths.
  • Pollution does not tip near zero when there is \(P_+>0\) with \(\rho+\theta\left(P\right)+\theta'\left(P\right)P>0\) for all \(P\in\left[0,P_+\right]\). The paper’s model has this property, since it takes \(\theta\) continuously differentiable (Section 2, as the shared page restates): \(\theta'\) is then bounded near zero, so \(\theta'\left(P\right)P\to0\) as \(P\to0\), while \(\rho+\theta\left(0\right)>0\). Assumption 1(iii) gives it a second way, since pollution tends to zero on every state-B path (Lemma Z, in A path in state B), so the levels near zero are in the relevant range.

Pollution on a materially feasible path

Lemma E. On every materially feasible path:

(a) \(0\le P_t\le\bar P\equiv P_0+\mathcal B+\Omega^{N,S}\bar N+\Omega^{D,S}\left(\bar X_{\max}-X_0\right)\) at every date;

(b) \(\mathcal P\) is strictly increasing on \(\left[0,\infty\right)\);

(c) for all dates \(t\ge s\), \(P_t\le\left[1-\theta\left(\bar P\right)\right]^{t-s}P_s+\bar\Xi_s/\theta\left(\bar P\right)\), with \(\bar\Xi_s\equiv\sup_{r\ge s}\Xi_r\); so \(P_t\to0\) when \(\Xi_t\to0\).

Proof.

  1. \(\mathcal P'\left(P\right)=1-\theta\left(P\right)-\theta'\left(P\right)P>0\), since \(\theta<1\), \(\theta'<0\) and \(P\ge0\).

  2. Every term of \(\Xi\) is nonnegative: the bracket of \(\Xi^h\) because \(\varpi\in\left[0,1\right]\) and \(a\le1\), and \(\mathcal N\) because \(N,D\ge0\). From \(P_0\ge0\) and \(\theta\in\left(0,1\right)\), \(P_{t+1}=\mathcal P\left(P_t\right)+\Xi_t\) lies in \(\left[0,P_t+\Xi_t\right]\), so \(P_t\ge0\) and \(P_t\le P_0+\sum_{s<t}\Xi_s\). Since \(d^W<1\), \(\Xi^h_s\le\left[\left(1-\varpi_s\right)+\varpi_s\left(1-a_s\right)\right]H_s=\left(1-a_s\varpi_s\right)H_s\), whose sum is at most \(\mathcal B\) by Lemma A(b), and \(\sum_s\mathcal N_s\le\Omega^{N,S}\bar N+\Omega^{D,S}\left(\bar X_{\max}-X_0\right)\) by Lemma A(a).

  3. By (a) and \(\theta'<0\), \(\theta\left(P_t\right)\ge\theta\left(\bar P\right)>0\), so \(P_{t+1}\le\left[1-\theta\left(\bar P\right)\right]P_t+\Xi_t\). The bound holds at \(t=s\), and if it holds at \(t\), then \(P_{t+1}\le\left[1-\theta\left(\bar P\right)\right]^{t+1-s}P_s+\left[1-\theta\left(\bar P\right)\right]\bar\Xi_s/\theta\left(\bar P\right)+\bar\Xi_s\), which is the bound at \(t+1\). If \(\Xi_t\to0\), choose \(s\) with \(\bar\Xi_s\) small and then \(t\) large.

A path in state B

Lemma Z. Let \(z\) be a feasible path in state B with rates \(\left(g,\nu\right)\), in the Cobb-Douglas workhorse without a floor and with CRRA utility, under any yield function and any \(\phi^I\ge0\).

(a) \(P_t\to0\), and \(\left(1+g\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\Gamma\left(1-\nu\right)^{\gamma}\); hence \(1-\nu<1+g<1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\).

(b) \(\mathcal W_t/C_t\to0\); and if \(\left(1-\nu\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta-1}<1\), then \(\left(1+t\right)\mathcal W_tC_t^{\eta-1}\to0\).

(c) If \(\Pi\equiv\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}<1\), the value of \(z\) is finite, and for every \(\upsilon\in\left(0,\min\left\{\Pi,1-\Pi\right\}\right)\) there is a date \(T_\upsilon\) such that \(\pi_t\equiv\beta^tC_t^{1-\eta}\) satisfies \[ \pi_s\left(\Pi-\upsilon\right)^{t-s}\;\le\;\pi_t\;\le\;\pi_s\left(\Pi+\upsilon\right)^{t-s}\qquad\text{for }t\ge s\ge T_\upsilon . \]

(d) If \(\phi^I=0\), then at every date, with the inflow \(n_t\equiv N_t+\delta M^K_t\ge0\), \[ \mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\ell_t\right)\mathcal W_t+n_t, \qquad R_t=N_t+\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\mathcal W_t\;\le\;\mathcal W_{t+1}; \tag{W} \] and for every \(\upsilon\in\left(0,\min\left\{\nu,1-\nu\right\}\right)\) there is a date \(T'_\upsilon\) such that, for \(t\ge s\ge T'_\upsilon\), \[ \left(1-\nu-\upsilon\right)^{t-s}\mathcal W_s\le\mathcal W_t\le\left(1-\nu+\upsilon\right)^{t-s}\mathcal W_s, \qquad \ell_t\ge\nu-\upsilon, \qquad n_t\le\ell_t\mathcal W_t\le\mu\mathcal W_t, \qquad \sum_{r\ge s}\mathcal W_r\le\frac{\mathcal W_s}{\nu-\upsilon} . \] In particular \(\nu\le\mu\).

Proof.

  1. \(\Xi^h_t\le H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\to0\), the bracket being at most one, and \(\mathcal N_t\to0\) by Lemma A(a); so \(\Xi_t\to0\), and Lemma E(c) gives \(P_t\to0\). Output is positive from some date on, and \(A_t\), \(B_t\) follow the trends, so \[ \frac{Y_{t+1}}{Y_t}=\left(1+g_A\right)\left(1+g_B\right)^{\gamma}e^{-\kappa\left(P_{t+1}-P_t\right)}\left(\frac{K^Y_{t+1}}{K^Y_t}\right)^{\beta_K}\left(\frac{R_{t+1}}{R_t}\right)^{\gamma}\;\longrightarrow\;\Gamma\left(1+g\right)^{\beta_K}\left(1-\nu\right)^{\gamma}, \] while the left side tends to \(1+g>0\); dividing by \(\left(1+g\right)^{\beta_K}\) gives the identity. Since \(\left(1-\nu\right)^{\gamma}<1\), \(\left(1+g\right)^{1-\beta_K}<\Gamma=\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\beta_K}\). Since \(\Gamma>1\), \(0<1-\nu<1\) and \(\gamma<1-\beta_K\), \(\left(1+g\right)^{1-\beta_K}>\left(1-\nu\right)^{\gamma}>\left(1-\nu\right)^{1-\beta_K}\).

  2. The ratio of consecutive terms of \(\mathcal W_t/C_t\) tends to \(\left(1-\nu\right)/\left(1+g\right)\), below one by (a), and that of \(\left(1+t\right)\mathcal W_tC_t^{\eta-1}\) to \(\left(1-\nu\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta-1}\). A positive sequence whose ratio tends to a limit below one tends to zero.

  3. \(\pi_{t+1}/\pi_t=\beta\left(C_{t+1}/C_t\right)^{1-\eta}\to\Pi\), so the ratio lies in \(\left[\Pi-\upsilon,\Pi+\upsilon\right]\) from some date \(T_\upsilon\) on, and iterating gives the bounds. For \(\eta\ne1\), \(\sum_t\beta^t\left|u\left(C_t\right)\right|=\sum_t\pi_t/\left|1-\eta\right|<\infty\) by the upper bound, as \(\Pi+\upsilon<1\). For \(\eta=1\), \(\left|\ln C_t\right|\le\left|\ln C_0\right|+bt\) with \(b\equiv\sup_s\left|\ln\left(C_{s+1}/C_s\right)\right|\), finite because the sequence converges and \(C_s>0\) at every date, so \(\sum_t\beta^t\left|\ln C_t\right|<\infty\). By Lemma E(a) pollution stays in \(\left[0,\bar P\right]\), where the continuous \(v\) is bounded, so \(\sum_t\beta^tv\left(P_t\right)\) converges absolutely. The value is finite.

  4. With \(\phi^I=0\), \(\Phi^I=0\), so \(M^K_t=\left(1-\delta\right)^tM^K_0\ge0\) and the ledger reads \(W_t=R_t+\delta M^K_t\). With \(R_t=N_t+a_t\varpi_t\mu\mathcal W_t=N_t+\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\mathcal W_t\), the waste-stock transition gives (W), and \(R_t\le W_t\le\mathcal W_{t+1}\). Let \(T'_\upsilon\) be a date from which \(\mathcal W_t>0\) and \(\mathcal W_{t+1}/\mathcal W_t\) lies within \(\upsilon\) of \(1-\nu\); \(\mathcal W\) decreases from then on, as \(1-\nu+\upsilon<1\). Iterating the bounds on \(\mathcal W_{t+1}/\mathcal W_t\) gives the first display and the geometric sum. By (W), \(\ell_t\mathcal W_t=\mathcal W_t-\mathcal W_{t+1}+n_t\ge\left(\nu-\upsilon\right)\mathcal W_t\) and \(n_t=\ell_t\mathcal W_t-\left(\mathcal W_t-\mathcal W_{t+1}\right)\le\ell_t\mathcal W_t\), while \(\ell_t\le\mu\) since \(a_t\varpi_t\ge0\). Finally \(\nu-\upsilon\le\mu\) for every small \(\upsilon\) gives \(\nu\le\mu\).

Proof of Proposition 8

Let \(z\) be a state-B path with rates \(\left(g,\nu\right)\), \(\phi^I=0\) and \(\Pi\equiv\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}<1\). Its value is finite by Lemma Z(c). We build the switch \(z'\), with primes for its variables, and show that its value exceeds that of \(z\) for every late enough switch date.

Proof. Step 1: the constants and the common part of the switch. Fix \[ 0<\upsilon<\tfrac14\min\left\{\nu,\;\nu+g,\;1-\nu,\;\Pi,\;1-\Pi\right\}, \qquad\text{and in case (b) also }\upsilon<g/2 , \] where \(\nu+g>0\) by Lemma Z(a). Let \(T_0\) be a date from which the bounds of Lemma Z(c) and (d) hold with this \(\upsilon\), \(Y_t\), \(K^Y_t\) and \(R_t\) are positive, \(\underline o\le Y_t/C_t\le\bar o\), and \(\left(1+g-\upsilon\right)C_t\le C_{t+1}\le\left(1+g+\upsilon\right)C_t\). The switch date is any \(T_1\ge T_0+1\); further lower bounds on \(T_1\) are collected as they arise, and no constant below depends on \(T_1\).

The path \(z'\) has the controls of \(z\) through \(T_1-2\), and at \(T_1-1\) those of \(z\) except investment, consumption and the intensity \(\Omega\). From \(T_1\) on it extracts and explores as \(z\) does, \(N'_t=N_t\) and \(D'_t=D_t\). It treats all handled waste, \(\varpi'_t=1\), at an intensity \(x'_t\) set by the rule of step 2 or step 3, with recycling capital \(K^{R\prime}_t\equiv x'_t\mu\mathcal W'_t\). It finances that capital with the added capital \(Q_t\equiv\max\left\{0,K^{R\prime}_t-K^R_t\right\}\) held on top of the capital of \(z\), \(K'_t\equiv K_t+Q_t\), with \(Q_t\equiv0\) for \(t<T_1\), so that it invests \(I'_t=I_t+Q_{t+1}-Q_t\) from \(T_1-1\) on. Consumption \(C'_t\) is the goods constraint solved for it, \(W'_t\) is given by the ledger, \(\Omega'_t\equiv R'_t/\mathcal D'_t\), and the states follow the transitions. Four consequences follow.

  • Production capital never falls below that of \(z\): \(K^{Y\prime}_t=K'_t-K^{R\prime}_t=K^Y_t+\max\left\{0,K^R_t-K^{R\prime}_t\right\}\ge K^Y_t\) for \(t\ge T_1\).
  • With \(\phi^I=0\), neither the ledger nor any transition of a material stock or of pollution involves \(C\), \(I\) or \(\Omega\), so period \(T_1-1\) has the material block of \(z\): \(\mathcal W'_{T_1}=\mathcal W_{T_1}\) and \(P'_{T_1}=P_{T_1}\). \(S'\), \(X'\) and \(M^{K\prime}\) equal \(S\), \(X\) and \(M^K\) at every date, and so does the inflow \(n_t\) of (W).
  • For \(t\ge T_1\), with the loss rate \(\ell'_t=\mu\left[1-a\left(x'_t\right)\right]\), the argument of (W) gives \(R'_t=N_t+\left(\mu-\ell'_t\right)\mathcal W'_t\) and \(\mathcal W'_{t+1}=\left(1-\ell'_t\right)\mathcal W'_t+n_t\), and at \(\varpi'=1\) the flow reaching the environment is \(\Xi'_t=d^W\ell'_t\mathcal W'_t+\mathcal N_t\), with the displaced mass of \(z\). On \(z\), since \(d^W<1\), \(\Xi_t\ge d^W\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t+\mathcal N_t=d^W\ell_t\mathcal W_t+\mathcal N_t\).
  • The stock gap \(w_t\equiv\mathcal W'_t-\mathcal W_t\) has \(w_{T_1}=0\) and, for \(t\ge T_1\), \[ w_{t+1}=w_t-\ell'_t\mathcal W'_t+\ell_t\mathcal W_t, \qquad R'_t-R_t=\mu w_t-\ell'_t\mathcal W'_t+\ell_t\mathcal W_t . \tag{W'} \]

Once a rule fixes \(x'_t\), the switch is materially feasible: the ledger and the transitions hold by construction, \(N'_t,D'_t\ge0\), \(\varpi'_t=1\), \(K^{R\prime}_t\ge0\), \(W'_t=R'_t+\delta M^K_t\ge0\), and \(S'\), \(X'\) and \(M^{K\prime}\) are those of \(z\). So Lemmas A and E apply to it before its consumption is known to be positive.

Step 2: case (b), the half-leakage rule. Let \(g>0\) and the yield have a thin tail. The rule is \[ 1-a\left(x'_t\right)=\frac{\ell_t\mathcal W_t}{2\mu\mathcal W'_t}, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad \ell'_t\mathcal W'_t=\tfrac12\ell_t\mathcal W_t : \] the switch loses half the tonnes \(z\) loses. For \(t\ge T_1\):

  • (b1) The rule is well defined and the stock gap grows. If \(w_t\ge0\), true at \(T_1\), the right side of the rule lies in \(\left(0,\frac12\right]\), since \(0<\nu-\upsilon\le\ell_t\le\mu\) and \(0<\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal W'_t\); so \(x'_t=a^{-1}\left(1-\ell_t\mathcal W_t/\left(2\mu\mathcal W'_t\right)\right)\) exists, \(a\left(x'_t\right)\ge\frac12\), and (W’) gives \(w_{t+1}=w_t+\frac12\ell_t\mathcal W_t\ge w_t\). By induction \(w_t=\frac12\sum_{r=T_1}^{t-1}\ell_r\mathcal W_r\ge0\), and the loss rate is lower, \(\ell'_t=\frac12\ell_t\mathcal W_t/\mathcal W'_t\le\frac12\ell_t\).
  • (b2) The stock is bounded. By Lemma Z(d), \(w_t\le\frac\mu2\sum_{r\ge T_1}\mathcal W_r\le\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}/\left[2\left(\nu-\upsilon\right)\right]\) and \(\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal W_{T_1}\), so \(\mathcal W'_t\le\bar w_b\mathcal W_{T_1}\) with \(\bar w_b\equiv1+\mu/\left[2\left(\nu-\upsilon\right)\right]\).
  • (b3) The intensity grows at most linearly. By Lemma Z(d) and (b2), \(1-a\left(x'_t\right)\ge\left(\nu-\upsilon\right)\mathcal W_t/\left(2\mu\bar w_b\mathcal W_{T_1}\right)\ge\frac{\nu-\upsilon}{2\mu\bar w_b}\left(1-\nu-\upsilon\right)^{t-T_1}\), and the thin tail gives \(x\le\xi^{-1}\ln\left[c_\xi/\left(1-a\left(x\right)\right)\right]\) at every \(x\), so \[ x'_t\;\le\;\frac{b_0+b_1\left(t-T_1\right)}{\xi}, \qquad b_0\equiv\ln\frac{2\mu\bar w_bc_\xi}{\nu-\upsilon}, \qquad b_1\equiv\ln\frac1{1-\nu-\upsilon} . \]
  • (b4) More material at every date, and eventually much more. By (W’) and the rule, \(R'_t-R_t=\mu w_t+\frac12\ell_t\mathcal W_t\ge0\). For \(t\ge T_1+1\), (b1) gives \(R'_t\ge a\left(x'_t\right)\mu\mathcal W'_t\ge\frac\mu2w_t\ge\frac\mu4\ell_{T_1}\mathcal W_{T_1}\ge\frac\mu4\left(\nu-\upsilon\right)\mathcal W_{T_1}\), while \(R_t\le\mathcal W_{t+1}\le\left(1-\nu+\upsilon\right)^{t+1-T_1}\mathcal W_{T_1}\) by (W) and Lemma Z(d); so \[ \frac{R'_t}{R_t}\;\ge\;c_R\,\vartheta^{\,t-T_1}\quad\left(t\ge T_1+1\right), \qquad c_R\equiv\frac{\mu\left(\nu-\upsilon\right)}{4\left(1-\nu+\upsilon\right)}, \qquad \vartheta\equiv\frac1{1-\nu+\upsilon}>1 . \]
  • (b5) Less pollution at every date. By step 1 and the rule, \(\Xi'_t=\frac12d^W\ell_t\mathcal W_t+\mathcal N_t\le\Xi_t\). From \(P'_{T_1}=P_{T_1}\), Lemma E(b) gives by induction \(P'_{t+1}=\mathcal P\left(P'_t\right)+\Xi'_t\le\mathcal P\left(P_t\right)+\Xi_t=P_{t+1}\).

Step 3: case (a), the fixed-intensity rule. Let \(\left(1-\nu\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta-1}<1\), the yield be any stationary yield under the soft ceiling, and pollution not tip near zero, on \(\left[0,P_+\right]\). Put \(\nu'\equiv\frac12\left[\max\left\{0,-g\right\}+\nu\right]\). Since \(\nu-\max\left\{0,-g\right\}=\min\left\{\nu,\nu+g\right\}>4\upsilon\), \[ \max\left\{0,-g\right\}+2\upsilon\;<\;\nu'\;<\;\nu-2\upsilon, \qquad\text{so}\qquad 0<\nu'<\nu\le\mu \quad\text{and}\quad 1-\nu'<1+g-2\upsilon . \] The rule is \(x'_t\equiv x^\circ\equiv a^{-1}\left(1-\nu'/\mu\right)\), defined because \(\nu'/\mu\in\left(0,1\right)\), so the loss rate is lower at every date, \(\ell'_t=\nu'<\nu-\upsilon\le\ell_t\). Write \(\vartheta\equiv\left(1-\nu'\right)/\left(1-\nu+\upsilon\right)>1\). For \(t\ge T_1\):

  • (a1) The stock gap is positive and decays no faster than at \(\nu'\). (W’) reads \(w_{t+1}=\left(1-\nu'\right)w_t+\left(\ell_t-\nu'\right)\mathcal W_t\), with \(\ell_t-\nu'>\upsilon\); so \(w_t\ge0\), \(w_{T_1+1}\ge\left(\nu-\upsilon-\nu'\right)\mathcal W_{T_1}\), and \(w_t\ge\left(1-\nu'\right)^{t-T_1-1}w_{T_1+1}\) for \(t\ge T_1+1\).
  • (a2) The stock decays at the rate \(\nu'\). Solving the recursion of \(\mathcal W'\), and bounding \(n_r\le\mu\mathcal W_r\le\mu\left(1-\nu+\upsilon\right)^{r-T_1}\mathcal W_{T_1}\) by Lemma Z(d), so that the sum is at most \(\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}\left(1-\nu'\right)^{t-1-T_1}\sum_{j\ge0}\vartheta^{-j}\), \[ \mathcal W'_t=\left(1-\nu'\right)^{t-T_1}\mathcal W_{T_1}+\sum_{r=T_1}^{t-1}\left(1-\nu'\right)^{t-1-r}n_r\;\le\;\bar w_a\left(1-\nu'\right)^{t-T_1}\mathcal W_{T_1}, \qquad \bar w_a\equiv1+\frac{\mu}{\left(1-\nu'\right)\left(1-\vartheta^{-1}\right)} . \]
  • (a3) More material at every date, and eventually much more. By (W’), \(R'_t-R_t=\left(\mu-\nu'\right)w_t+\left(\ell_t-\nu'\right)\mathcal W_t\ge0\). For \(t\ge T_1+1\), \(R'_t\ge\left(\mu-\nu'\right)w_t\) and \(R_t\le\left(1-\nu+\upsilon\right)^{t+1-T_1}\mathcal W_{T_1}\), so with (a1) \[ \frac{R'_t}{R_t}\;\ge\;c_R\,\vartheta^{\,t-T_1}\quad\left(t\ge T_1+1\right), \qquad c_R\equiv\frac{\left(\mu-\nu'\right)\left(\nu-\upsilon-\nu'\right)}{\left(1-\nu'\right)\left(1-\nu+\upsilon\right)} . \]
  • (a4) Pollution stays close. Emissions are not ordered here: the switch holds a larger stock and eventually emits more. The displaced mass cancels from the difference, and both handled streams lie in \(\left[0,\mu\mathcal W'_t\right]\), so by (a2) \(\left|\Xi'_t-\Xi_t\right|\le\mu\bar w_a\mathcal W_{T_1}\). A uniform bound comes first. The switch is materially feasible by step 1, so Lemma E(c) from \(s=T_1\), applied to both paths with \(\Xi_r\le\mu\mathcal W_r+\mathcal N_r\) and \(\Xi'_r\le\mu\mathcal W'_r+\mathcal N_r\), gives \[ \bar P_{T_1}\equiv\sup_{t\ge T_1}\max\left\{P_t,P'_t\right\}\;\le\;P_{T_1}+\frac{\mu\bar w_a\mathcal W_{T_1}+\sup_{r\ge T_1}\mathcal N_r}{\theta\left(\bar P\right)}, \] which tends to zero as \(T_1\to\infty\), by Lemma Z(a), Lemma Z(d) and Lemma A(a). Take \(T_1\) large enough that \(\bar P_{T_1}\le P_+\) and \(\kappa\bar P_{T_1}\le\ln2\). A discounted bound comes second. Let \(\mathfrak d_t\equiv P'_t-P_t\), so \(\mathfrak d_{T_1}=0\). By the mean value theorem, \(\mathcal P\left(P'_t\right)-\mathcal P\left(P_t\right)=\mathcal P'\left(\check P_t\right)\mathfrak d_t\) at a point \(\check P_t\) between \(P_t\) and \(P'_t\), hence in \(\left[0,P_+\right]\), where \(0<\mathcal P'=1-\theta-\theta'P<1+\rho\) because pollution does not tip there. So \(\left|\mathfrak d_{t+1}\right|\le\left(1+\rho\right)\left|\mathfrak d_t\right|+\mu\bar w_a\mathcal W_{T_1}\), and since \(\beta\left(1+\rho\right)=1\), induction gives \[ \beta^{t-T_1}\left|\mathfrak d_t\right|\;\le\;\mu\bar w_a\mathcal W_{T_1}\sum_{r\ge1}\beta^r\;=\;\bar d_{T_1}\equiv\frac{\mu\bar w_a\mathcal W_{T_1}}{\rho} . \] As \(\left|\mathfrak d_t\right|\le P_+\) too, \(\beta^{t-T_1}\left|\mathfrak d_t\right|\le\min\left\{\bar d_{T_1},\beta^{t-T_1}P_+\right\}\). Let \(J\) be the least integer \(j\ge0\) with \(\beta^jP_+\le\bar d_{T_1}\), so \(J\le1+\ln^+\left(P_+/\bar d_{T_1}\right)/\ln\left(1+\rho\right)\) with \(\ln^+\equiv\max\left\{0,\ln\right\}\). The first \(J\) terms are at most \(\bar d_{T_1}\) each and the rest form a geometric tail, so \[ \sum_{t\ge T_1}\beta^{t-T_1}\left|\mathfrak d_t\right|\;\le\;\bar d_{T_1}\left[J+\frac{1+\rho}{\rho}\right] . \] Since \(v'\) is increasing, the mean value theorem on \(\left[0,P_+\right]\) gives \(\left|v\left(P'_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right|\le v'\left(P_+\right)\left|\mathfrak d_t\right|\) for \(t\ge T_1\).

Step 4: consumption. At \(t\ge T_1\) the goods constraints of \(z'\) and \(z\) differ in output, in gross formation, \(G'_t-G_t=Q_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)Q_t\), and in the handling cost; the extraction and exploration costs agree. With \(C^W_t\ge0\), \(C^{W\prime}_t=\left[c^c\left(t\right)+c^T\left(1,t\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W'_t\le c^{\mathrm{full}}\mu\mathcal W'_t\), \(Q_t\ge0\) and \(Q_{t+1}\le K^{R\prime}_{t+1}=x'_{t+1}\mu\mathcal W'_{t+1}\), \[ C'_t\;\ge\;C_t+\left(Y'_t-Y_t\right)-\mathfrak s_tC_t, \qquad \mathfrak s_t\equiv\frac{x'_{t+1}\mu\mathcal W'_{t+1}+c^{\mathrm{full}}\mu\mathcal W'_t}{C_t}\quad\left(t\ge T_1\right), \] and at \(T_1-1\), where only investment and consumption change, \(C'_{T_1-1}=C_{T_1-1}-Q_{T_1}\ge\left(1-\mathfrak s_{T_1-1}\right)C_{T_1-1}\) with \(\mathfrak s_{T_1-1}\equiv x'_{T_1}\mu\mathcal W_{T_1}/C_{T_1-1}\). For output, \(A_t\) and \(B_t\) are common to the two paths and \(K^{Y\prime}_t\ge K^Y_t\), so \[ \frac{Y'_t}{Y_t}=e^{-\kappa\left(P'_t-P_t\right)}\left(\frac{K^{Y\prime}_t}{K^Y_t}\right)^{\beta_K}\left(\frac{R'_t}{R_t}\right)^{\gamma}\;\ge\;e^{-\kappa\bar\delta}\left(\frac{R'_t}{R_t}\right)^{\gamma}, \] with \(\bar\delta\equiv0\) in case (b), by (b5), and \(\bar\delta\equiv\bar P_{T_1}\) in case (a), by (a4). Hence the consumption ratio \(\mathfrak c_t\equiv C'_t/C_t\) satisfies, for \(t\ge T_1\), \[ \mathfrak c_t\;\ge\;1+\frac{Y_t}{C_t}\left[e^{-\kappa\bar\delta}\left(\frac{R'_t}{R_t}\right)^{\gamma}-1\right]-\mathfrak s_t . \]

The cost share vanishes. Let \(\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\equiv\sup_{t\ge T_1-1}\mathfrak s_t+\bar o\kappa\bar\delta\). In case (b), by (b2), (b3) and \(C_t\ge\left(1+g-\upsilon\right)^{t-T_1}C_{T_1}\ge\left(1+g/2\right)^{t-T_1}C_{T_1}\), \[ \mathfrak s_t\;\le\;\mu\bar w_b\frac{\mathcal W_{T_1}}{C_{T_1}}\cdot\frac{\xi^{-1}\left[b_0+b_1\left(t+1-T_1\right)\right]+c^{\mathrm{full}}}{\left(1+g/2\right)^{t-T_1}}\;\le\;\mu\bar w_b\,\mathfrak m_b\frac{\mathcal W_{T_1}}{C_{T_1}}, \qquad \mathfrak m_b\equiv\sup_{j\ge0}\frac{\xi^{-1}\left[b_0+b_1\left(j+1\right)\right]+c^{\mathrm{full}}}{\left(1+g/2\right)^{j}}, \] finite because \(g>0\) and the numerator is affine in \(j\); and \(\mathfrak s_{T_1-1}\le\mu\xi^{-1}b_0\left(1+g+\upsilon\right)\mathcal W_{T_1}/C_{T_1}\). In case (a), by (a2) and \(1-\nu'<1+g-\upsilon\), \(\mathfrak s_t\le\left(x^\circ+c^{\mathrm{full}}\right)\mu\bar w_a\mathcal W_{T_1}/C_{T_1}\) and \(\mathfrak s_{T_1-1}\le x^\circ\mu\left(1+g+\upsilon\right)\mathcal W_{T_1}/C_{T_1}\), while \(\bar P_{T_1}\to0\) by (a4). Every constant here is independent of \(T_1\), and \(\mathcal W_{T_1}/C_{T_1}\to0\) by Lemma Z(b), so \(\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\to0\).

The lag. Let \(m\ge1\) be the least integer with \(\left(c_R\vartheta^m\right)^{\gamma}\ge2\left(1+1/\underline o\right)\); it exists because \(\vartheta>1\), and it does not depend on \(T_1\). Take \(T_1\) large enough that \(\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\le\frac12\). Then:

  • at \(t=T_1-1\), \(\mathfrak c_t\ge1-\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\);
  • at \(t\ge T_1\), \(R'_t\ge R_t\) by (b4) or (a3), so the bracket is at least \(e^{-\kappa\bar\delta}-1\ge-\kappa\bar\delta\); if it is negative, \(Y_t/C_t\le\bar o\) gives \(\mathfrak c_t\ge1-\bar o\kappa\bar\delta-\mathfrak s_t\ge1-\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\), and if it is nonnegative, \(\mathfrak c_t\ge1-\mathfrak s_t\ge1-\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\);
  • at \(t\ge T_1+m\), \(e^{-\kappa\bar\delta}\ge\frac12\) and \(R'_t/R_t\ge c_R\vartheta^m\) make the bracket at least \(1/\underline o\), so \(\mathfrak c_t\ge1+\underline o/\underline o-\mathfrak s_t\ge\frac32\).

In particular \(C'_t\ge\frac12C_t>0\) at every \(t\ge T_1-1\).

Step 5: feasibility. Through \(T_1-2\) the controls are those of \(z\), so the states agree through \(T_1-1\). At \(T_1-1\) investment and consumption change by \(+Q_{T_1}\) and \(-Q_{T_1}\), which keeps the goods constraint, and \(C'_{T_1-1}>0\) by step 4. From \(T_1\) on, the goods constraint holds by the definition of \(C'_t\), with \(C'_t>0\) by step 4, and the material constraints hold by step 1; \(K'_t-K^{R\prime}_t=K^{Y\prime}_t\ge K^Y_t\ge0\); and gross formation carries no sign restriction. From \(T_1-1\) on, \(K'_{t+1}=K_{t+1}+Q_{t+1}\ge0\). With \(\phi^I=0\) the intensity condition reads \(R=\Omega\mathcal D\), and it holds with \(\Omega'_t=R'_t/\mathcal D'_t\), nonnegative and finite, once \(\mathcal D'_t>0\) for \(t\ge T_1-1\). On \(z\), \(R_t=\Omega_t\mathcal D_t>0\) with \(\Omega_t\ge0\), so \(\mathcal D_t>0\) for \(t\ge T_0\). Every term of \(\mathcal D\) is nonnegative, and term by term \(\mathcal D'_t\ge\frac12\mathcal D_t\): consumption is at least half that of \(z\) by step 4; output is at least half, since \(Y'_t\ge e^{-\kappa\bar\delta}Y_t\ge\frac12Y_t\) for \(t\ge T_1\) and \(Y'_{T_1-1}=Y_{T_1-1}\); the extraction and exploration costs are those of \(z\); and the handling cost is at least that of \(z\), \(C^{W\prime}_t=\left[c^c\left(t\right)+c^T\left(1,t\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W'_t\ge\left[c^c\left(t\right)\varpi_t+c^T\left(\varpi_t,t\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W_t=C^W_t\), because \(c^T\) increases in \(\varpi\) and \(\mathcal W'_t\ge\mathcal W_t\) by (b1) or (a1), with equality at \(T_1-1\). So \(z'\) is feasible.

Step 6: the value. For every \(\eta>0\) let \(\mathfrak u\left(y\right)\equiv\left(y^{1-\eta}-1\right)/\left(1-\eta\right)\) for \(\eta\ne1\) and \(\mathfrak u\left(y\right)\equiv\ln y\) at \(\eta=1\), so that \(u\left(yC\right)-u\left(C\right)=C^{1-\eta}\,\mathfrak u\left(y\right)\). \(\mathfrak u\) is increasing and concave with \(\mathfrak u\left(1\right)=0\) and \(\mathfrak u'\left(y\right)=y^{-\eta}\), so on \(\left[\frac12,1\right]\) concavity gives \(\mathfrak u\left(y\right)\ge-\mathfrak u'\left(y\right)\left(1-y\right)\ge-2^{\eta}\left(1-y\right)\), and \(\mathfrak u\ge0\) on \(\left[1,\infty\right)\). By step 4, \(\mathfrak u\left(\mathfrak c_t\right)\ge-2^{\eta}\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\) for \(t\ge T_1-1\) and \(\mathfrak u\left(\mathfrak c_t\right)\ge\mathfrak u\left(\frac32\right)>0\) for \(t\ge T_1+m\). The two paths agree in consumption before \(T_1-1\) and in pollution through \(T_1\), so their partial sums of value differ by \[ \sum_{t=T_1-1}^{T}\pi_t\,\mathfrak u\left(\mathfrak c_t\right)-\sum_{t=T_1}^{T}\beta^t\left[v\left(P'_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right] . \] The first sum has terms bounded below by \(-2^{\eta}\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\pi_t\), summable by Lemma Z(c), so it converges in \(\left(-\infty,+\infty\right]\); the second converges absolutely, both paths’ pollution lying in \(\left[0,\bar P\right]\), where \(v\) is bounded; and the value of \(z\) is finite. So the value of \(z'\) exists in \(\left(-\infty,+\infty\right]\), and \(U_0\left(z'\right)-U_0\left(z\right)\) is the limit of the display as \(T\to\infty\). Bound the first sum below by \(-2^{\eta}\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\pi_t\) on \(T_1-1\le t<T_1+m\) and by \(\mathfrak u\left(\frac32\right)\pi_t\) on \(t\ge T_1+m\), and use Lemma Z(c) from \(s=T_1-1\ge T_0\): \(\pi_{T_1-1}\le\pi_{T_1}/\left(\Pi-\upsilon\right)\), \(\sum_{t\ge T_1}\pi_t\le\pi_{T_1}/\left(1-\Pi-\upsilon\right)\) and \(\sum_{t\ge T_1+m}\pi_t\ge\pi_{T_1}\left(\Pi-\upsilon\right)^m/\left(1-\Pi+\upsilon\right)\). The pollution sum is nonpositive in case (b), by (b5) and \(v'>0\), and in case (a) at most \(\beta^{T_1}v'\left(P_+\right)\bar d_{T_1}\left[J+\left(1+\rho\right)/\rho\right]\), by (a4). Dividing by \(\pi_{T_1}=\beta^{T_1}C_{T_1}^{1-\eta}\), \[ \frac{U_0\left(z'\right)-U_0\left(z\right)}{\beta^{T_1}C_{T_1}^{1-\eta}} \;\ge\; \underbrace{\mathfrak u\left(\tfrac32\right)\frac{\left(\Pi-\upsilon\right)^m}{1-\Pi+\upsilon}}_{\text{gain}} -\underbrace{2^{\eta}\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\left[\frac1{\Pi-\upsilon}+\frac1{1-\Pi-\upsilon}\right]}_{\text{cost}} -\underbrace{\frac{v'\left(P_+\right)\mu\bar w_a}{\rho}\,\mathcal W_{T_1}C_{T_1}^{\eta-1}\left[J+\frac{1+\rho}{\rho}\right]}_{\text{pollution, case (a)}} . \] The gain is positive and independent of \(T_1\), and the cost tends to zero with \(\bar{\mathfrak s}_{T_1}\). In case (a), \(\ln^+\left(P_+/\bar d_{T_1}\right)\le\ln^+\left[\rho P_+/\left(\mu\bar w_a\right)\right]+\ln^+\left(1/\mathcal W_{T_1}\right)\), and Lemma Z(d) gives \(\mathcal W_{T_1}\ge\left(1-\nu-\upsilon\right)^{T_1-T_0}\mathcal W_{T_0}\), so \(J\) grows at most linearly in \(T_1\); the pollution term is then at most a constant times \(\left(1+T_1\right)\mathcal W_{T_1}C_{T_1}^{\eta-1}\), which tends to zero by Lemma Z(b). This is where the condition of (a) enters. So there is a date beyond which every switch date \(T_1\) gives \(U_0\left(z'\right)>U_0\left(z\right)\), the switch copying \(z\) until \(T_1-2\) and then treating all handled waste at a lower loss rate.

The exponential yield

Proof. The exponential yield has \(1-a\left(x\right)=e^{-\xi x}\), a thin tail with \(c_\xi=1\). If \(\eta>1\) and \(g>0\), (b) holds. Otherwise (a) holds: at \(\eta=1\), \(\left(1-\nu\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta-1}=1-\nu<1\); for \(\eta>1\) with \(g\le0\), and for \(\eta<1\) with \(g\ge0\), \(\left(1+g\right)^{\eta-1}\le1\); and for \(\eta<1\) with \(g<0\), \(0<1+g<1\) and \(0<1-\eta<1\) give \(\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}>1+g>1-\nu\) by Lemma Z(a). So, by the proof of Proposition 8, step 2 in case (b) and step 3 in case (a), a state-B path with \(\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}<1\) is strictly improved on by a feasible path, and is not optimal. A state-B path with \(\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}>1\) has \(\eta\ne1\), since \(\beta<1\), and \(\pi_{t+1}/\pi_t\to\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}>1\), so \(\pi_t\to\infty\); its utility sum \(\sum_t\pi_t/\left(1-\eta\right)\) is \(+\infty\) for \(\eta<1\) and \(-\infty\) for \(\eta>1\), while its pollution sum converges by Lemma E(a), so its value is not finite. An optimal path with a finite value in state B therefore has \(\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}=1\).
