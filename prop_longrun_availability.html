O.1 Which long-run states each cell makes available

Proposition 4 of the paper (availability) states which of the three long-run states each cell of the taxonomy makes available, and what a candidate in state B or C must look like. This page proves it in full. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol a proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used. Lemma A, the collected ledger (L) and the first-order conditions (C), (G), (KR), (T) and (PP) are proved on the page of lemmas shared by the long-run proofs, and the workhorse clause of item (iii) rests on Lemmas W1 and W2 of the page on the workhorse’s balanced paths.

The statement

Proposition 4 (availability). Under Assumption 1, along paths that converge in the sense of its part (ii):

(i) With a floor. State A is available, and state B is not. Under the hard ceiling every feasible path is in state A, whatever \(g_A\) and \(g_B\). Under the soft ceiling a path that operates infinitely often closes the loop and has \(\mathcal M_\infty\ge\bar R\,\mathcal T\), and it operates from some date on if \(R_\infty>\bar R\). Hence state C is available only if some feasible path closes the loop with \(\mathcal M_\infty>\bar R\,\mathcal T\), which requires \(\mu\mathcal B>\bar R\); a path with \(\mathcal M_\infty<\bar R\,\mathcal T\) collapses, and so does every path if \(\mu\mathcal B<\bar R\).

(ii) Without a floor. State A is not available, and under the hard ceiling state C is not available. Where state B is available its margins obey the bound of Lemma B.1.

(iii) Candidates. In either soft-ceiling cell a candidate state-C path exists only if the closure criterion of Proposition 7 holds, and with a floor a candidate whose yield fails it collapses. In the Cobb-Douglas workhorse of Section 3.6 the circular balanced growth path is a candidate configuration under these conditions, subject to two inequalities on its wedges (Appendix B.6), and a candidate state-B path with converging recycling margins has the rates of Appendix B.6, which exist exactly when \(\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)+\gamma>0\).

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions. Items (i) and (ii) and the first sentence of item (iii) hold for general primitives and follow from the constraints of Problem 1 and the accounting; a first-order condition enters only through the definition of a candidate. The second sentence of item (iii) is a statement about the workhorse, proved from the first-order conditions of Appendix A with the two inequalities on the wedges taken as given; we prove nothing about whether they hold.

What the proofs use

The shared page restates a feasible path with the mass restrictions, the displaced mass \(\mathcal N\) and the emission flow \(\Xi=\Xi^h+\mathcal N\), the material budget \(\mathcal B=\bar N+M^K_0+\mathcal W_0\) with the virgin budget \(\bar N=S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0\), the loop closing, the operating set \(\mathcal O\), states A and C of Definition 1, availability and candidates, and Assumption 1(i) and (ii). We use in addition:

  • State B (Definition 1): “balanced dematerialization if \(C_t>0\) at every date and, asymptotically, \(C\), \(Y\) and \(K^Y\) grow at a common rate \(g\) with \(Y/C\) bounded above and away from zero, while \(R\) and \(\mathcal W\) decay at a common rate \(\nu\in\left(0,1\right)\)”.
  • Assumption 1(iii): “\(\theta\left(P\right)\ge\theta_{\min}>0\) for all \(P\), and \(\rho+\theta\left(P\right)+\theta'\left(P\right)P>0\) on the relevant range”. We use its first half.
  • The storage share and the residence time. Appendix A writes \(\sigma\equiv\Phi^IG/R\) for the storage share, and Proposition 3 writes \(\mathcal T\equiv1/\mu+\sigma_\infty/\delta\) for the mean residence time of a tonne per pass, with \(\sigma_\infty\) the limit of \(\sigma_t\) along the path; the retained material endowment is \(\mathcal M_\infty\equiv\lim_t\left(M^K_t+\mathcal W_t\right)\).
  • The closure criterion (Proposition 7). At a date with \(\varpi_tH_t>0\), \(x_t=K^R_t/\left(\varpi_tH_t\right)\) and the leakage rate of a treated tonne is \(L_t\equiv1-a_t\). Along a candidate whose recycling-capital margin (KR) is interior with \(\mathcal G^K_t\ne0\) from a date \(T_0\) on, the closure criterion is \(\sum_{t\ge T_0}L_t<\infty\); the candidate’s yield fails it when such a \(T_0\) exists and \(\sum_{t\ge T_0}L_t=\infty\).
  • The loss rate of the waste stock, \(\ell_t\equiv\mu\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)\), the share of the stock at the start of period \(t\) that is handled and not recovered. With \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\), the collected ledger of Section 2, \(\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t=N_t-\Delta M^K_{t+1}-\Delta\mathcal W_{t+1}\), reads \(\mathcal W_{t+1}-\mathcal W_t=N_t-\left(M^K_{t+1}-M^K_t\right)-\ell_t\mathcal W_t\).

Two short lemmas complete the list.

Lemma L (the flow identity of Little’s law). On a feasible path along which \(N_t\to0\), \(a_t\varpi_t\to1\), and \(R_t\), \(M^K_t\) and \(\mathcal W_t\) converge, with \(R_\infty\equiv\lim_tR_t>0\): \(\sigma_t\) converges, to \(\sigma_\infty=\delta M^K_\infty/R_\infty\ge0\), and \(\mathcal M_\infty=R_\infty\mathcal T\) with \(\mathcal T\ge1/\mu\).

Proof. Since \(R_t=N_t+a_t\varpi_t\mu\mathcal W_t\), \(R_\infty=\mu\mathcal W_\infty\). The transition of embodied material gives \(\Phi^I_tG_t=M^K_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t\to\delta M^K_\infty\), so \(\sigma_t=\Phi^I_tG_t/R_t\to\delta M^K_\infty/R_\infty\), which is nonnegative because \(M^K_t\ge0\). Adding the two stocks, \(\mathcal M_\infty=R_\infty/\mu+\sigma_\infty R_\infty/\delta=R_\infty\mathcal T\), and \(\sigma_\infty\ge0\) gives \(\mathcal T\ge1/\mu\).

Lemma B (the bound of the paper’s Lemma B.1). Along a feasible path in state B with decay rate \(\nu\) on which \(M^K\) is eventually nonincreasing, \(\liminf_t\ell_t\ge\nu\), that is \(\limsup_ta_t\varpi_t\le1-\nu/\mu\); so \(\nu\le\mu\), with equality only if \(a\varpi\to0\), and under the hard ceiling a path with \(a_t\to\bar a\) and \(\varpi_t\to1\) has \(\nu\le\mu\left(1-\bar a\right)\).

Proof. Since \(\mathcal W\) decays at rate \(\nu\), \(\mathcal W_t>0\) from some date on, and there the ledger divided by \(\mathcal W_t\) gives \[ \ell_t=\frac{N_t-\left(M^K_{t+1}-M^K_t\right)}{\mathcal W_t}+1-\frac{\mathcal W_{t+1}}{\mathcal W_t} . \] Once \(M^K\) is nonincreasing the first term is nonnegative, as \(N_t\ge0\), and the second tends to \(\nu\); so \(\liminf_t\ell_t\ge\nu\), which is \(\limsup_ta_t\varpi_t\le1-\nu/\mu\). Since \(a_t\varpi_t\ge0\), \(\ell_t\le\mu\) at every date, so \(\nu\le\mu\), and \(\nu=\mu\) forces \(\limsup_ta_t\varpi_t\le0\), that is \(a\varpi\to0\). If \(a_t\to\bar a\) and \(\varpi_t\to1\), then \(a_t\varpi_t\to\bar a\le1-\nu/\mu\).

Proof of item (i)

Let \(\bar R>0\).

Proof. State A is available. Fix \(\varkappa\in\left(0,1-\delta\right)\) and set, at every date, \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=0\), \(K^R_t=0\), \(\Omega_t=0\), \(K_{t+1}=\varkappa K_t\), \(C_t=\left(1-\delta-\varkappa\right)K_t\) and \(W_t=\delta M^K_t\), the states following the transitions of Problem 1. Nothing is treated, so \(R^R_t=0\) and \(R_t=0<\bar R\): no date operates, and \(Y_t=0\). The cost flows are zero, by \(C^N\left(0,S,t\right)=C^D\left(0,X,t\right)=0\) and \(C^W_t=\left[c^c\cdot0+c^T\left(0\right)\right]H_t=0\), and \(C_t+I_t+\delta K_t=\left[\left(1-\delta-\varkappa\right)+\left(\varkappa-1\right)+\delta\right]K_t=0=Y_t\): the goods constraint holds. The intensity condition holds as \(0=0\). With \(\Phi^I_t=\Omega_t\phi^I=0\), \(M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t\), and the ledger \(W_t=R_t-\left(M^K_{t+1}-M^K_t\right)=\delta M^K_t\) holds. The domains hold: \(C_t,K_{t+1}\ge0\) from \(K_0\ge0\), \(W_t\ge0\), and the mass restrictions \(S_t=S_0\ge0\), \(X_t=X_0\le\bar X_{\max}\), \(M^K_t=\left(1-\delta\right)^tM^K_0\ge0\). So the path is feasible, \(\mathcal O\) is empty and finite, and the path is in state A. It converges in the sense of Assumption 1(ii): \(K\) and \(C\) have the constant ratio \(\varkappa\), or are zero; \(Y\) and the flows \(N\), \(D\), \(R\) are zero; \(S\) and \(X\) are constant; \(M^K_t\to0\), and \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+\delta M^K_t\to0\). Pollution converges too: \(\mathcal N_t=0\), so \(\Xi_t=\Xi^h_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\to0\), and \(P_{t+1}\le\left(1-\theta_{\min}\right)P_t+\Xi_t\) by Assumption 1(iii); for any \(b>0\), once \(\Xi_s\le b\,\theta_{\min}\) for all \(s\ge t\), \(P_{t+n}\le\left(1-\theta_{\min}\right)^nP_t+b\), so \(P_t\to0\).

State B is not available. On a path in state B, \(R\) decays at a rate \(\nu\in\left(0,1\right)\), so \(R_t\to0\), and \(R_t<\bar R\) from some date on; there \(Y_t=0\) while \(C_t>0\), so \(Y_t/C_t=0\) is not bounded away from zero. No feasible path is in state B.

The hard ceiling. At a date with a treated flow, \(a_t\varpi_t\le a\left(x_t,t\right)<\bar a\), the yield staying below its ceiling at every finite intensity; at a date without one, \(a_t\varpi_t=0\). So \(\left(1-\bar a\right)H_t\le\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t\), and Lemma A(b) gives \(\sum_tH_t\le\mathcal B/\left(1-\bar a\right)\). With \(R_t=N_t+a_t\varpi_tH_t\le N_t+\bar aH_t\) and Lemma A(a), \[ \sum_{t\ge0}R_t\;\le\;\bar N+\frac{\bar a\,\mathcal B}{1-\bar a}\;\equiv\;\bar{\mathcal R}. \] Every operating date has \(R_t\ge\bar R\), so \(\bar R\cdot\#\mathcal O\le\bar{\mathcal R}\): \(\mathcal O\) is finite, and the path is in state A by the gloss of Definition 1(A) on the shared page. No property of \(F\) was used, so this holds whatever \(g_A\) and \(g_B\), and on every feasible path.

The soft ceiling: a path that operates infinitely often. Let a path converge in the sense of Assumption 1(ii), with \(\mathcal O\) infinite. \(R\) is a material flow, so it decays at a constant rate or converges, and it is bounded, \(R_t\le N_t+\mu\mathcal B\le\bar N+\mu\mathcal B\) by Lemma A; either way \(R_t\to R_\infty\) for a finite \(R_\infty\ge0\). Since \(R_t\ge\bar R\) at infinitely many dates, \(R_\infty\ge\bar R>0\). Lemma A(c) closes the loop, so \(a_t\varpi_t\to1\); \(N_t\to0\) by Lemma A(a); and \(M^K\) and \(\mathcal W\), material stocks, decay at constant rates or converge, and converge either way. Lemma L gives \(\mathcal M_\infty=R_\infty\mathcal T\ge\bar R\,\mathcal T\). If \(R_\infty>\bar R\), then \(R_t>\bar R\) from some date on, and the path operates from that date on.

State C is available only if some feasible path closes the loop with \(\mathcal M_\infty>\bar R\,\mathcal T\). A feasible path in state C has \(R_t\to R_\infty>\bar R>0\), \(N_t\to0\), \(a_t\varpi_t\to1\) and both stocks convergent, by Definition 1(C). Lemma A(c) closes its loop, and Lemma L gives \(\mathcal M_\infty=R_\infty\mathcal T>\bar R\,\mathcal T\). As \(\mathcal M_\infty\le\mathcal B\) by Lemma A(b) and \(\mathcal T\ge1/\mu\), \[ \frac{\bar R}{\mu}\;\le\;\bar R\,\mathcal T\;<\;\mathcal M_\infty\;\le\;\mathcal B, \] so \(\mu\mathcal B>\bar R\).

A path with \(\mathcal M_\infty<\bar R\,\mathcal T\) collapses. Let a path that converges in the sense of Assumption 1(ii) have \(\mathcal M_\infty<\bar R\,\mathcal T\), with \(\mathcal T\) formed from the limit \(\sigma_\infty\) of its storage shares. If \(\mathcal O\) were infinite, the argument for a path that operates infinitely often would give \(\mathcal M_\infty=R_\infty\mathcal T\ge\bar R\,\mathcal T\) with the same \(\sigma_\infty\). So \(\mathcal O\) is finite, and the path is in state A.

Every path collapses if \(\mu\mathcal B<\bar R\). On every feasible path \(R_t\le N_t+\mu\mathcal B\) by Lemma A(b), and \(N_t\to0\) by Lemma A(a). If \(\mu\mathcal B<\bar R\), then \(R_t<\bar R\) from some date on, \(\mathcal O\) is finite, and the path is in state A, under either ceiling and whether or not it converges.

Proof of item (ii)

Let \(\bar R=0\).

Proof. State A is not available. \(R_t=N_t+R^R_t\ge0=\bar R\) at every date, so on every feasible path \(\mathcal O\) is the set of all dates, which is infinite.

Under the hard ceiling state C is not available. State C requires \(a_t\varpi_t\to1\), while under the hard ceiling \(a_t\varpi_t<\bar a<1\) at every date, as in the proof of item (i).

The margins of state B. Wherever a state-B path exists, Lemma B bounds its margins where \(M^K\) is eventually nonincreasing: this is the bound of the paper’s Lemma B.1.

Proof of item (iii), first sentence

Proof. A candidate in state C meets the criterion. Let a candidate be in state C, in either soft-ceiling cell. Then \(R_t\to R_\infty>\bar R\ge0\), so the loop closes by Lemma A(c), as Definition 1(C) also states. Let \(T_0\) be a date from which (KR) is interior with \(\mathcal G^K_t\ne0\); then \(K^R_t>0\) and \(\varpi_tH_t>0\), so \(x_t\) and \(L_t\) are defined, for \(t\ge T_0\). Since \(0\le a_t\le1\) and \(\varpi_t\le1\), \(L_t=1-a_t\le1-a_t\varpi_t\), and \[ \sum_{t\ge T_0}L_t\;\le\;\sum_{t\ge T_0}\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)\;<\;\infty : \] the closure criterion holds.

With a floor, a candidate whose yield fails the criterion collapses. Let \(\bar R>0\), and let a candidate that converges in the sense of Assumption 1(ii) fail the criterion: (KR) is interior with \(\mathcal G^K_t\ne0\) from some \(T_0\) on, and \(\sum_{t\ge T_0}L_t=\infty\). As in the proof of item (i), \(R_t\to R_\infty\) for a finite \(R_\infty\ge0\). If \(\mathcal O\) were infinite, \(R_\infty\ge\bar R>0\), Lemma A(c) would close the loop, and the inequality \(L_t\le1-a_t\varpi_t\) would make \(\sum_{t\ge T_0}L_t\) finite. So \(\mathcal O\) is finite: the candidate collapses.

Proof of item (iii), second sentence

Lemmas W1 and W2 of the workhorse page prove it. In the workhorse under the soft ceiling, Lemma W1 shows that along the circular balanced growth path the deflated prices of Appendix A are the closed-form block (B); that, if the extraction and discovery costs vanish against output, \(\left(C^N_t+C^D_t\right)/Y_t\to0\), the limit system has a solution at a capital-output ratio in the admissible range, and is admissible exactly under the two inequalities (WI) on its wedges; and that along the path the recycling-capital margin has a root at every late date with a summable leakage rate, and, if the marginal handling charge at full treatment, \(c^c\left(t\right)+c^{T\prime}\left(1,t\right)\), is bounded over time, treatment is full. So the closure criterion, the condition the first sentence places on a candidate in state C, holds along the margin: the path is a candidate configuration under these conditions, subject to the two inequalities. Lemma W2 derives the rates of the state-B candidates that Appendix B.6 describes, and shows that they exist exactly when \(\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)+\gamma>0\).
