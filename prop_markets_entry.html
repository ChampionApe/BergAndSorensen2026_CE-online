O.6 The entry condition into treatment

Proposition 9 of the paper (the entry condition into treatment) states when the waste sector treats the flow it handles, at any setting of the instruments and on a candidate planner path, and what open access, the owned stockpile and a rising material price make of that test. This page proves it in full. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

The statement

Proposition 9 (the entry condition into treatment). General primitives. At any date with a positive handled flow, a positive rental and a positive handling wedge \(P^W>0\), at any setting of the instruments and under either ownership regime:

  1. the waste sector’s treated share is unique, and \[ \varpi=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad E\le0 ; \] when \(E>0\) it treats at the intensity \(x^*\), with \(K^R=x^*\varpi H\), and treats fully if and only if \(E\ge P^Wc^{T\prime}\!\left(1\right)\). A candidate planner path, one meeting the necessary conditions of Appendix A, has no treatment at a date if and only if \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}\le0\), where \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}\) is \(E\) at the planner’s prices, \(\tilde p=\mathcal G^K\), \(r^K=F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\), \(\tau^P=p^P\) and \(P^W=1+\zeta\Omega^W\);
  2. under open access, \[ E^{\mathrm{OA}} = \alpha\!\left(x^*\right)F_R-c^c, \qquad a'\!\left(x^*\right)F_R = F_K\ \text{if }a'\!\left(0^+\right)F_R>F_K,\quad x^*=0\ \text{otherwise}, \] so the open-access economy treats only if \(\bar aF_R>c^c\); where it extracts, \(F_R=C^N_N+p^S\), the effort and the rent of the virgin tonne that a recovered tonne replaces;
  3. at the owned stockpile, at a common \(\left(F_R,F_K,\Omega\right)\) and with the fee nonpositive, the intensity is open access’s and \[ E^{\mathrm{OS}}-E^{\mathrm{OA}} = -\tau^W\left[\alpha\!\left(x^*\right)F_R\,\Omega^Y+c^c\,\Omega^W\right]\;\ge\;0 , \] strictly whenever \(\tau^W<0\) and either \(\Omega^W>0\) or \(\Omega^Y>0\) with \(x^*>0\);
  4. along an open-access path on which \(F_R\to\infty\) and \(F_K/F_R\to0\), with the yield nondecreasing and the collection charge nonincreasing in time, the entry margin diverges: treatment and recycling capital enter at a finite date, and if the marginal treatment cost at full treatment is bounded across dates, treatment is full from a finite date.

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions. Every item holds for general primitives, with no functional form. Items 1 to 3 are statements about one date, and only the planner’s clause of item 1 uses first-order conditions. What the statement and the proof take from Sections 2 and 4 and from Appendix A of the paper is restated in What the proof uses.

What the proof uses

From Section 2 of the paper. Within a date we suppress the time argument and write \(a'\) for \(a_x\). We read the yield as continuous on \(\left[0,\infty\right)\) and twice differentiable on \(\left(0,\infty\right)\), as its derivatives presume. Then \(a'\) is positive and strictly decreasing, and it tends to zero at infinity, since a limit \(\ell>0\) would make \(a\left(x\right)\ge a\left(1\right)+\ell\left(x-1\right)\) unbounded; and \(a\) rises strictly to \(\bar a\), so \(a\left(x\right)<\bar a\). By the mean value theorem \(a\left(x\right)=a'\left(\xi\right)x\) for some \(\xi\in\left(0,x\right)\). So \(a\left(x\right)>a'\left(x\right)x\), and the slope of the yield at the origin, which Section 4.2 writes \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)\equiv\lim_{x\downarrow0}a\left(x\right)/x\), is also \(\lim_{x\downarrow0}a'\left(x\right)\), a limit in \(\left(0,\infty\right]\) as \(a'\) is positive and decreasing. The marginal recycling yield \(\alpha\left(x\right)\equiv a\left(x\right)-a'\left(x\right)x\) therefore has \(0<\alpha\left(x\right)\le a\left(x\right)\) for \(x>0\), and we set \(\alpha\left(0\right)\equiv0\). For a treated flow \(T>0\), \(\mathcal R\left(T,xT\right)=a\left(x\right)T\), and with none nothing is recovered, \(\mathcal R\left(0,K^R\right)=0\), the only value consistent with \(0\le\mathcal R\le\bar aT\). The collection charge \(c^c\) is positive; \(c^T\left(0\right)=c^{T\prime}\left(0\right)=0\), \(c^{T\prime}>0\) on \(\left(0,1\right]\) and \(c^{T\prime\prime}>0\), so \(c^{T\prime}\) increases strictly on \(\left[0,1\right]\) and \(c^T\left(\varpi\right)>0\) for \(\varpi>0\); both charges are nonincreasing in time. \(\Omega^j=\Omega\omega^j\ge0\), since \(\omega^j\ge0\) and \(\Omega\ge0\) in Problem 1. Output vanishes below the floor, so \(F_K=F_R=0\) where \(R<\bar R\), while where \(R\ge\bar R\), \(F_K=\mathcal F_K>0\) and \(F_R=\mathcal F_{R^e}>0\). Section 2.1 maintains, at the output function, “the Inada condition in capital, \(\mathcal F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\) at any \(R^e>0\)”.

From Section 4 of the paper. The gate fee \(\tau^W\) is what a generator pays to hand over a tonne of waste, the emission tax \(\tau^P\) is levied per tonne of \(\Xi\) on its emitter, \(z\) is the material-content price, and a unit of the final good put to the current use \(j\) costs the user price \(P^j\equiv1+\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega^j\) (Section 4.1). With \(p^R\) the price of a tonne of raw material and \(r^K\) the rental of capital, the final-goods sector’s first-order conditions are \(F_R\left[1-\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega^Y\right]=p^R-z\) and \(F_K\left[1-\left(z+\tau^W\right)\Omega^Y\right]=r^K\), and where \(N>0\) the mining sector’s extraction condition is \(p^R=P^NC^N_N+\tau^P\Omega^{N,S}+p^S\), with \(p^S\) the mine’s value of a tonne left in the ground. An idle date has \(Y=R=0\) and a zero rental (Definition 2); at every other date the final-goods sector operates and its two conditions hold.

The waste sector (Section 4.1) takes in the flow \(W_t\) at the fee, draws \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\) from the waste stock, chooses the treated share \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\) and the recycling capital \(K^R_t\ge0\), rented at \(r^K_t\), sells \(R^R_t=\mathcal R\left(\varpi_tH_t,K^R_t\right)\) at \(p^R_t\), buys the \(C^W_t=\left[c^c\varpi_t+c^T\left(\varpi_t\right)\right]H_t\) units of the final good its handling uses at \(P^W_t\), and pays the emission tax on \(\Xi^h_t\); \(\tilde p\equiv p^R+d^W\tau^P\) is its value of a recovered tonne. Where the stock is owned, the sector’s value \(J^W_t\) “is the fee on what it accepts, plus its operating profit, plus the discounted value of the stock it carries into the next period” (Section 4.1): the largest value, over \(W_t\ge0\) and the pair \(\left(\varpi_t,K^R_t\right)\), of its period cash flow plus \(J^W_{t+1}\left(\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+W_t\right)/\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)\), with \(r\) the market interest rate; \(J^W\) is real-valued, the paper differentiating it to price the stock. Where nobody owns the stock, “since nobody values the stock, the waste sector chooses its treated share and recycling capital against its operating profit alone” (Section 4.2), a reason that holds whatever the instruments.

Under open access (Section 4.2) nobody owns the stock and \(\tau^W=\tau^P=z=0\), so every user price is one and, where the final-goods sector operates, \(p^R=F_R\) and \(r^K=F_K\). At the owned stockpile the stock is owned and \(\tau^P=z=0\), so \(P^W=1+\tau^W\Omega^W\) and, where the final-goods sector operates, \(p^R=F_R\left(1-\tau^W\Omega^Y\right)\) and \(r^K=F_K\left(1-\tau^W\Omega^Y\right)\). For \(r^K>0\) the recovery value of a treated tonne and the entry margin are (Section 4.2) \[ \mathcal G^{x}\!\left(\tilde p,r^K\right)\equiv\max_{x\ge0}\left[a\left(x\right)\tilde p-r^Kx\right], \qquad E\equiv\mathcal G^{x}\!\left(\tilde p,r^K\right)+\tau^P\left(1-d^W\right)-P^Wc^c , \] and \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}\) and \(E^{\mathrm{OS}}\) are \(E\) under open access and at the owned stockpile; “at a common \(\left(F_R,F_K,\Omega\right)\)” evaluates both at the same values of the three.

From Appendix A, through the shared page. The shared page of the long-run proofs restates the prices of Appendix A and the Lagrangian of Problem 1. We take from it that \(\lambda_t>0\) on a candidate, by (C); that the pair \(\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\) enters the period-\(t\) terms of the Lagrangian only as \(\beta^t\lambda_t\) times the braces \(\left\{Y-C^W-p^WR+\zeta\left[R-\Omega\left(\mathcal D+\phi^IG\right)\right]-p^P\Xi\right\}\), whose derivatives where \(\varpi H>0\) are \(a'\mathcal G^K-\tilde F_K\) in \(K^R\) and \(H\left\{\mathcal G^\varpi-\left[c^c+c^{T\prime}\left(\varpi\right)\right]\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)\right\}\) in \(\varpi\); and the prices of (G), \(\Psi\equiv F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)+\zeta\), \(\mathcal G^K\equiv\Psi-p^W+d^Wp^P\) and \(\mathcal G^\varpi=\alpha\mathcal G^K+\left(1-d^W\right)p^P\), with \(\tilde F_K\equiv F_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)\).

Appendix A states the conditions on the pair, (KR) and (T) where it is interior, as branch systems in which “each corner holds when its inequality goes the other way”; “\(F_R\) is \(\mathcal F_{R^e}\) at the prevailing \(R\ge\bar R\)” and “the conditions are evaluated on operating periods”. It notes that “The upper bound \(K^R\le K\) never binds, since \(F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\)”, the reason being Section 2.1’s Inada condition in capital, \(\mathcal F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\) at any \(R^e>0\), and adds: “At \(\varpi=0\) the ratio \(x=K^R/\left(\varpi H\right)\) is undefined, so entry into treatment and into recycling capital is one joint decision, tested over the best available pair \(\left(\varpi,x\right)\).” We read these first-order conditions as one requirement on the pair: at the candidate’s pair, with every other control and every multiplier held at the candidate’s values, the one-sided derivative of the braces is nonpositive in every direction \(\left(d\varpi,dK^R\right)\) along which the pair stays in \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,K_t\right]\) for small steps, \(\mathcal F\) being differentiable at the date’s input, as the shared page’s derivatives presume. Where the pair is interior this is (KR) and (T), at a bound the inequality of its branch, and at \(\varpi=0\) the joint test.

The recovery value

Lemma R. Let \(r>0\) and \(\tilde p\) be real. The maximum of \(f\left(x\right)\equiv a\left(x\right)\tilde p-rx\) over \(x\ge0\) is attained at exactly one point \(x^*\left(\tilde p,r\right)\): \(x^*>0\) with \(a'\left(x^*\right)\tilde p=r\) if \(\tilde p>0\) and \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)\tilde p>r\), and \(x^*=0\) otherwise. Its value is \(\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p,r\right)=\alpha\left(x^*\right)\tilde p\), and:

(a) \(\mathcal G^x\ge0\), with \(\mathcal G^x>0\) if and only if \(x^*>0\);

(b) \(\mathcal G^x<\bar a\tilde p\) if \(\tilde p>0\);

(c) for \(s>0\), \(x^*\left(s\tilde p,sr\right)=x^*\left(\tilde p,r\right)\) and \(\mathcal G^x\left(s\tilde p,sr\right)=s\,\mathcal G^x\left(\tilde p,r\right)\).

Proof. \(f\) is continuous on \(\left[0,\infty\right)\) with \(f\left(0\right)=0\). If \(\tilde p\le0\), \(a\tilde p\) is nonincreasing and \(-rx\) strictly decreasing, so \(f\) falls strictly and \(x^*=0\). If \(\tilde p>0\), \(f'\left(x\right)=a'\left(x\right)\tilde p-r\) is continuous and strictly decreasing on \(\left(0,\infty\right)\), with limit \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)\tilde p-r\) at the origin and \(-r<0\) at infinity. If \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)\tilde p>r\), exactly one \(x^*>0\) has \(f'\left(x^*\right)=0\), and \(f\) rises strictly on \(\left[0,x^*\right]\) and falls strictly on \(\left[x^*,\infty\right)\); otherwise \(f'<0\) on \(\left(0,\infty\right)\) and \(f\) falls strictly from \(x=0\). Either way the maximiser is unique. At an interior \(x^*\), \(r=a'\left(x^*\right)\tilde p\) gives \(f\left(x^*\right)=\left[a\left(x^*\right)-a'\left(x^*\right)x^*\right]\tilde p=\alpha\left(x^*\right)\tilde p\); at \(x^*=0\) both sides are zero.

  1. \(\mathcal G^x\ge f\left(0\right)=0\). If \(x^*>0\), then \(\tilde p>0\) and \(\alpha\left(x^*\right)>0\), so \(\mathcal G^x>0\); if \(x^*=0\), \(\mathcal G^x=0\). (b) \(\alpha\left(x^*\right)\le a\left(x^*\right)<\bar a\). (c) \(f\) at \(\left(s\tilde p,sr\right)\) is \(s\) times \(f\) at \(\left(\tilde p,r\right)\), so it has the same maximiser and \(s\) times the maximum.

The waste sector’s choice at a date

Lemma H. Take a date with \(H>0\), \(r^K>0\) and \(P^W>0\), any setting of the instruments and either ownership regime.

(a) The waste sector’s pair maximises over \(\left[0,1\right]\times\left[0,\infty\right)\) its operating profit, the part of its period cash flow that the pair moves, \[ \Pi^W\!\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\equiv\tilde p\,\mathcal R\left(\varpi H,K^R\right)+\tau^P\left(1-d^W\right)\varpi H-P^W\left[c^c\varpi+c^T\!\left(\varpi\right)\right]H-r^KK^R-\tau^PH . \]

(b) This problem has exactly one solution, \(\left(\varpi^*,\varpi^*x^*H\right)\), with \(x^*=x^*\left(\tilde p,r^K\right)\) of Lemma R and \(\varpi^*\) the unique maximiser over \(\left[0,1\right]\) of \(\hat\pi\left(\varpi\right)\equiv\varpi E-P^Wc^T\!\left(\varpi\right)-\tau^P\).

(c) \(\varpi^*=0\) if and only if \(E\le0\), and \(\varpi^*=1\) if and only if \(E\ge P^Wc^{T\prime}\!\left(1\right)\).

Proof.

  1. The period cash flow is \(\tau^WW+p^RR^R-P^WC^W-r^KK^R-\tau^P\Xi^h\). With \(\Xi^h=\left[1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\left(1-a\right)\right]H=\left(1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\right)H-d^WR^R\) it is \(\tau^WW+\Pi^W\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\). Where the stock is owned, the sector’s choice at date \(t\) maximises \(\Pi^W\left(\varpi,K^R\right)+\tau^W_tW_t+J^W_{t+1}\left(\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+W_t\right)/\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)\). The handled flow is fixed when the period opens and the pair is not in the transition, so the pair enters only through \(\Pi^W\); at a solution the other two terms are a real number, and a pair with a larger \(\Pi^W\), \(W_t\) kept, would raise the value. Where nobody owns the stock, the sector chooses the pair against \(\Pi^W\) alone.

  2. Fix \(\varpi\in\left(0,1\right]\). As \(\varpi H>0\), \(K^R=x\varpi H\) maps \(x\ge0\) one to one onto \(K^R\ge0\), and \(\mathcal R\left(\varpi H,x\varpi H\right)=a\left(x\right)\varpi H\), so \[ \Pi^W\!\left(\varpi,x\varpi H\right)=\varpi H\left[a\left(x\right)\tilde p-r^Kx\right]+\tau^P\left(1-d^W\right)\varpi H-P^W\left[c^c\varpi+c^T\!\left(\varpi\right)\right]H-\tau^PH , \] which by Lemma R is largest exactly at \(x=x^*\), with value \(H\hat\pi\left(\varpi\right)\). At \(\varpi=0\) nothing is recovered and \(\Pi^W\left(0,K^R\right)=-r^KK^R-\tau^PH\), largest exactly at \(K^R=0\), with value \(H\hat\pi\left(0\right)\). So for every \(\varpi\) the largest \(\Pi^W\) is \(H\hat\pi\left(\varpi\right)\), attained only at \(K^R=\varpi x^*H\). As \(P^W>0\) and \(c^T\) is strictly convex, \(\hat\pi\) is continuous and strictly concave, with a unique maximiser \(\varpi^*\) on \(\left[0,1\right]\). Every pair has \(\Pi^W\left(\varpi,K^R\right)\le H\hat\pi\left(\varpi\right)\le H\hat\pi\left(\varpi^*\right)=\Pi^W\left(\varpi^*,\varpi^*x^*H\right)\), and as \(H>0\) one of the two inequalities is strict unless \(\varpi=\varpi^*\) and \(K^R=\varpi^*x^*H\).

  3. \(\hat\pi\left(\varpi\right)-\hat\pi\left(0\right)=\varpi E-P^Wc^T\!\left(\varpi\right)\). If \(E\le0\) it is negative for \(\varpi>0\), as \(c^T\left(\varpi\right)>0\), so \(\varpi^*=0\). If \(E>0\) it equals \(\varpi\left[E-P^Wc^T\!\left(\varpi\right)/\varpi\right]\), positive for small \(\varpi>0\) since \(c^T\!\left(\varpi\right)/\varpi\to c^{T\prime}\!\left(0\right)=0\), so \(\varpi^*>0\). On \(\left(0,1\right]\) the derivative of \(\hat\pi\), \(E-P^Wc^{T\prime}\!\left(\varpi\right)\), falls strictly. If \(E\ge P^Wc^{T\prime}\!\left(1\right)\) it is positive on \(\left(0,1\right)\), \(\hat\pi\) rises strictly on \(\left[0,1\right]\) and \(\varpi^*=1\); if \(E<P^Wc^{T\prime}\!\left(1\right)\) it is negative at \(\varpi=1\), so some \(\varpi<1\) does better than \(1\).

Proof of Proposition 9

Proof. Item 1, the waste sector. By Lemma H, whenever the sector solves its problem its pair is \(\left(\varpi^*,\varpi^*x^*H\right)\): the treated share is unique, \(\varpi=0\) exactly when \(E\le0\), and \(\varpi=1\) exactly when \(E\ge P^Wc^{T\prime}\!\left(1\right)\). When \(E>0\), \(\varpi>0\) and \(K^R=x^*\varpi H\), so the intensity \(K^R/\left(\varpi H\right)\) is \(x^*\).

Item 1, a candidate planner path. Take a candidate and a date \(t\) with \(H_t>0\), \(\tilde F_{K,t}>0\) and \(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t>0\). As \(F_K\ne0\), \(R_t\ge\bar R\). And \(K^Y_t>0\), so \(K^R_t<K_t\), as Appendix A’s sentence on the upper bound states; Section 2.1’s Inada clause is its reason, \(\tilde F_{K,t}\) being a real number while \(\mathcal F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\) at any \(R^e>0\). By Lemma R at \(\left(\mathcal G^K_t,\tilde F_{K,t}\right)\), \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}_t=\max_{x\ge0}\Upsilon_t\left(x\right)\), with \[ \Upsilon_t\left(x\right)\equiv a\left(x\right)\mathcal G^K_t-x\tilde F_{K,t}+\left(1-d^W\right)p^P_t-\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right)c^c . \]

No treatment implies \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}_t\le0\). Let \(\varpi_t=0\). Then \(K^R_t=0\): with no treated flow nothing is recovered, so \(K^R\) moves the braces only through \(Y=F\left(K-K^R,R,P,t\right)\), directly and in \(\mathcal D\), at the rate \(-\tilde F_{K,t}<0\), and lowering a positive \(K^R_t\) would raise them, against the requirement. At the corner \(\left(0,0\right)\), \(K_t=K^Y_t>0\), so for every \(x\ge0\) the direction \(\left(1,xH_t\right)\) is feasible, and every feasible direction with \(d\varpi>0\) is a positive multiple of one of them. Along \(\left(\varpi,K^R\right)=\left(\epsilon,\epsilon xH_t\right)\), \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), the treated flow \(\epsilon H_t\) is positive, so \(R^R=\epsilon a\left(x\right)H_t\), \(R=N_t+R^R\ge\bar R\), \(K^Y=K_t-\epsilon xH_t\), \(C^W=\left[c^c\epsilon+c^T\left(\epsilon\right)\right]H_t\) and \(\Xi^h=\left[1-\left(1-d^W\right)\epsilon-d^Wa\left(x\right)\epsilon\right]H_t\); up to terms the pair does not move, the braces are \(\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)Y-\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)C^W+\left(\zeta-p^W\right)R-p^P\Xi^h\). Their derivative at \(\epsilon=0^+\) is, with \(c^{T\prime}\left(0\right)=0\) and (G), \[ H_t\Big\{a\left(x\right)\left[F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)+\zeta-p^W+d^Wp^P\right]-xF_K\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)+\left(1-d^W\right)p^P-\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)c^c\Big\}\Big|_t=H_t\,\Upsilon_t\left(x\right). \] The requirement on the pair makes it nonpositive for every \(x\ge0\), so \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}_t\le0\).

\(E^{\mathrm{pl}}_t\le0\) implies no treatment. Suppose \(\varpi_t>0\), and let \(x_t\equiv K^R_t/\left(\varpi_tH_t\right)\). Lowering \(\varpi\) is feasible, and with the shared page’s derivative in \(\varpi\) the requirement gives (T), or its corner at \(\varpi_t=1\), as \(\mathcal G^\varpi_t\ge\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right)\left[c^c+c^{T\prime}\left(\varpi_t\right)\right]\). If \(K^R_t>0\), lowering \(K^R\) is feasible and gives \(a'\left(x_t\right)\mathcal G^K_t\ge\tilde F_{K,t}\), so \(\alpha\left(x_t\right)\mathcal G^K_t\le a\left(x_t\right)\mathcal G^K_t-x_t\tilde F_{K,t}\); if \(K^R_t=0\), \(\alpha\left(x_t\right)=0\). Either way \(\alpha\left(x_t\right)\mathcal G^K_t\le\mathcal G^x\left(\mathcal G^K_t,\tilde F_{K,t}\right)\), by the definition of the maximum and Lemma R(a), and with (G) \[ E^{\mathrm{pl}}_t\;\ge\;\mathcal G^\varpi_t-\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right)c^c\;\ge\;\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right)c^{T\prime}\!\left(\varpi_t\right)\;>\;0 , \] since \(c^{T\prime}>0\) on \(\left(0,1\right]\), against \(E^{\mathrm{pl}}_t\le0\).

Item 2. Under open access, at a date with a positive rental the final-goods sector operates, so \(\tilde p=p^R=F_R\), \(r^K=F_K>0\), \(\tau^P=0\) and \(P^W=1\); then \(R\ge\bar R\), as \(F_K=0\) below the floor, and \(F_R>0\). Lemma R gives \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}=\mathcal G^x\left(F_R,F_K\right)-c^c=\alpha\left(x^*\right)F_R-c^c\), with \(x^*\) interior and \(a'\left(x^*\right)F_R=F_K\) exactly when \(a'\!\left(0^+\right)F_R>F_K\). By item 1 the economy treats only if \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}>0\), and by Lemma R(b) \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}<\bar aF_R-c^c\), so it treats only if \(\bar aF_R>c^c\). Where it extracts, the extraction condition with \(P^N=1\), \(\tau^P=0\) and \(p^R=F_R\) reads \(F_R=C^N_N+p^S\).

Item 3. At a date with a positive rental the final-goods sector operates, and at the owned stockpile \(\tau^P=z=0\), so \(\tilde p=p^R=sF_R\) and \(r^K=sF_K\), with \(s\equiv1-\tau^W\Omega^Y\ge1\) as the fee is nonpositive, and \(P^W=1+\tau^W\Omega^W\). The positive rental gives \(F_K>0\), so \(R\ge\bar R\) and \(F_R>0\). By Lemma R(c) the intensity \(x^*\left(sF_R,sF_K\right)\) is open access’s \(x^*\left(F_R,F_K\right)\) and \(\mathcal G^x\left(sF_R,sF_K\right)=s\,\alpha\left(x^*\right)F_R\), so \(E^{\mathrm{OS}}=s\,\alpha\left(x^*\right)F_R-\left(1+\tau^W\Omega^W\right)c^c\). Subtracting \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}=\alpha\left(x^*\right)F_R-c^c\) at the same \(\left(F_R,F_K,\Omega\right)\) gives the identity. Each of \(-\tau^W\), \(\alpha\left(x^*\right)\), \(F_R\), \(\Omega^Y\), \(\Omega^W\) and \(c^c\) is nonnegative, and so is the difference. It is positive when \(\tau^W<0\) and the bracket is positive, that is when \(\Omega^W>0\), as \(c^c>0\), or when \(\Omega^Y>0\) and \(\alpha\left(x^*\right)>0\), which by Lemma R(a) holds exactly when \(x^*>0\).

Item 4. Of an open-access path we use only that at every date the waste sector chooses its pair against its operating profit at open access’s prices. The proposition speaks of dates with a positive rental, at which the final-goods sector operates, so \(\tilde p=F_{R,t}\), \(r^K=F_{K,t}>0\), \(\tau^P=0\) and \(P^W=1\). As \(F_{R,t}\to\infty\), \(F_{R,t}>0\) from some date on, and when \(\bar R>0\) every such date has a positive rental: there \(R_t\ge\bar R>0\), so the date is not idle and \(r^K=F_{K,t}>0\). At a date with a positive rental and \(F_{R,t}>0\), and for any \(\bar x>0\), the definition of the maximum, a yield nondecreasing and a collection charge nonincreasing in time give \[ E^{\mathrm{OA}}_t\;\ge\;a\left(\bar x,t\right)F_{R,t}-\bar xF_{K,t}-c^c\left(t\right)\;\ge\;F_{R,t}\left[a\left(\bar x,0\right)-\bar x\,\frac{F_{K,t}}{F_{R,t}}\right]-c^c\left(0\right) . \] The bracket tends to \(a\left(\bar x,0\right)>0\) and \(F_{R,t}\) diverges, so \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}_t\to+\infty\) along the dates with a positive rental, and there is a date \(T_e\) after which \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}_t>0\) at each of them. At such a date with \(H_t>0\), item 1 gives \(\varpi_t>0\); as \(\tau^P=0\), \(\mathcal G^x=E^{\mathrm{OA}}_t+c^c\left(t\right)>0\), so \(x^*>0\) by Lemma R(a) and \(K^R_t=x^*\varpi_tH_t>0\). With \(W_t\ge0\) and \(\mu<1\), \(\mathcal W_{t+1}\ge\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t\), so a positive waste stock stays positive, and from the later of \(T_e\) and the first date at which the stock is positive the sector treats and rents recycling capital at every date with a positive rental. The marginal treatment cost at full treatment is \(P^Wc^{T\prime}\left(1,t\right)=c^{T\prime}\left(1,t\right)\); adding the collection charge, which lies in \(\left(0,c^c\left(0\right)\right]\), does not change whether it is bounded. If \(c^{T\prime}\left(1,t\right)\le\bar c\) at every date, there is a date \(T'_e\) after which \(E^{\mathrm{OA}}_t\ge\bar c\) at each date with a positive rental, and item 1 gives \(\varpi_t=1\) at each such date with \(H_t>0\).
