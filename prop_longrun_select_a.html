O.4 Selection against collapse, and when a collapse path has a finite value

Proposition 5 of the paper (selection against collapse) states that where the collapse ranking condition fails, no optimal path collapses as long as some path has a finite value, and that where the condition holds, a collapse path with a finite value exists. This page proves it in full. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

The statement

The paper’s long-run section (Section 3) calls the inequality \[ \left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}<1+\rho \tag{R} \] the collapse ranking condition.

Proposition 5 (selection against collapse). Let \(\bar R>0\) and let \(u\) be CRRA with elasticity \(\eta\).

(i) Where (R) fails, every feasible path in state A has value \(-\infty\); under the hard ceiling every feasible path does.

(ii) Where (R) fails and some feasible path has a finite value, every optimal path has a finite value and an infinite operating set. Under the soft ceiling and Assumption 1(i), an optimal path that converges in the sense of Assumption 1(ii) closes the loop and has \(R_\infty=\mathcal M_\infty/\mathcal T\ge\bar R\); it is in state C exactly when, in addition, \(R_\infty>\bar R\) and its goods block grows at a positive rate.

(iii) Let \(K_0>0\). Where (R) holds, a feasible path in state A with a finite value exists, so the condition holds exactly when some collapse path has a finite value.

We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions. No first-order condition is used, and (ii) uses optimality only through a comparison of two values. Technology, yield and costs are general throughout, the proof of (i) covers the general \(u\) of What the proof uses as well as CRRA, and Assumption 1(i) enters only in the last clause of (ii).

What the proof uses

We take from the page of lemmas shared by the long-run proofs what it restates of the paper. A feasible path meets every constraint and domain of Problem 1, the mass restrictions \(S_t\ge0\), \(X_t\le\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t\ge0\) included, from nonnegative initial stocks, and every cost flow on it is nonnegative. The operating set is \(\mathcal O\equiv\left\{t:R_t\ge\bar R\right\}\), and a path is in state A of Definition 1 exactly when \(\mathcal O\) is finite. The material budget \(\mathcal B\), state C, Assumption 1 and what it means for the loop to close are as quoted there, and Assumption 1(i) gives \(a\left(x,t\right)\le a^\infty\left(x\right)<1\) at every finite \(x\). Its Lemma A bounds what a feasible path extracts, discovers and leaks. The initial stocks meet the mass restrictions at date zero, or no path is feasible.

Section 2 of the paper writes welfare as \(U_0=\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^t\left[u\left(C_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right]\), with \(\beta\equiv1/\left(1+\rho\right)\), \(\rho>0\), \(u'>0\) and \(v'>0\); we take \(v\) real-valued on \(P\ge0\). CRRA utility is \(u\left(C\right)=C^{1-\eta}/\left(1-\eta\right)\) for \(\eta>0\), \(\eta\ne1\), and \(\ln C\) at \(\eta=1\), with \(u\left(0\right)=-\infty\) for \(\eta\ge1\). A general \(u\) is nondecreasing, real-valued at every \(C>0\), and satisfies \(u\left(C\right)\le-cC^{1-\eta}\) on some interval \(\left(0,\bar C\right]\), with \(c>0\) and \(\eta>1\); (R) is then read with that \(\eta\). It has \(u\left(0\right)=-\infty\) too, since \(u\left(0\right)\le u\left(C\right)\le-cC^{1-\eta}\) for every \(C\in\left(0,\bar C\right]\).

The value of a feasible path is the limit, as \(n\to\infty\), of the partial sums \(U_0^n\equiv\sum_{t=0}^{n}\beta^t\left[u\left(C_t\right)-v\left(P_t\right)\right]\), where that limit exists in \(\left[-\infty,\infty\right]\); the value is finite when the limit is a real number. An optimal path is a feasible path whose value exists and is at least the value of every feasible path whose value exists. A collapse path is a feasible path in state A.

From Little’s law, Proposition 3 of the paper, and from the paper’s Appendix A we take the retained material endowment \(\mathcal M_\infty\equiv\lim_t\left(M^K_t+\mathcal W_t\right)\), the storage share \(\sigma\equiv\Phi^IG/R\), and the residence time \(\mathcal T\equiv1/\mu+\sigma_\infty/\delta\), with \(\sigma_\infty\) the limit of \(\sigma_t\).

Pollution is nonnegative. The pollution transition reads \(P_{t+1}=\left[1-\theta\left(P_t\right)\right]P_t+\Xi_t\) with \(\theta\in\left(0,1\right)\), and its inflow \(\Xi=\Xi^h+\mathcal N\) is nonnegative: the emission of the handled stream, \(\Xi^h=\left[1-\varpi+d^W\varpi\left(1-a\right)\right]H\), has nonnegative factors, and the displaced mass \(\mathcal N=\Omega^{N,S}N+\Omega^{D,S}D\) is nonnegative. So \(P_t\ge0\) at every date, from \(P_0\ge0\), and \(v\left(P_t\right)\ge v\left(0\right)\), as \(v\) is increasing.

Lemma V. If \(u\le\bar u\) for a constant \(\bar u\), every feasible path has a value, and it is at most \(\left[\bar u-v\left(0\right)\right]/\left(1-\beta\right)\). CRRA utility with \(\eta>1\) is the case \(\bar u=0\).

Proof. The partial sums split as \[ U_0^n=\sum_{t=0}^{n}\beta^t\Big\{\left[u\left(C_t\right)-\bar u\right]-\left[v\left(P_t\right)-v\left(0\right)\right]\Big\}+\left[\bar u-v\left(0\right)\right]\sum_{t=0}^{n}\beta^t . \] No term of the first sum is positive, so the first sum is nonincreasing in \(n\) and converges in \(\left[-\infty,0\right]\); the second converges to \(\left[\bar u-v\left(0\right)\right]/\left(1-\beta\right)\). For \(\eta>1\), CRRA gives \(u\left(C\right)=-C^{1-\eta}/\left(\eta-1\right)<0\) at every \(C>0\).

Proof of (i)

Proof. Let (R) fail. Under CRRA this forces \(\eta>1\), since \(\eta\le1\) gives \(\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}\le1<1+\rho\), and then \(u\left(C\right)=-cC^{1-\eta}\) at every \(C>0\), with \(c=1/\left(\eta-1\right)\). So for CRRA and for a general \(u\) alike: \(\eta>1\), \(\beta\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}\ge1\), \(u\) is nondecreasing, and \(u\left(C\right)\le-cC^{1-\eta}\) on some interval \(\left(0,\bar C\right]\) with \(c>0\).

Step 1: after the last operating date, consumption is bounded by a stream falling at the factor \(1-\delta\). Let a feasible path be in state A, and let \(T^\dagger\equiv\max\mathcal O\), or \(T^\dagger\equiv-1\) if \(\mathcal O\) is empty. For \(t>T^\dagger\), \(R_t<\bar R\) and \(Y_t=0\), so the goods constraint, with \(I_t=K_{t+1}-K_t\) and nonnegative cost flows, gives \(C_t\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t-K_{t+1}\). As \(C_t\ge0\), \(K_{t+1}\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\); as \(K_{t+1}\ge0\), \(C_t\le\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\). By induction, with \(\bar K\equiv K_{T^\dagger+1}\ge0\) and \(j\equiv t-T^\dagger\), \[ C_t\;\le\;\left(1-\delta\right)^j\bar K\qquad\left(t>T^\dagger\right). \]

Step 2: the path has value \(-\infty\). If \(\bar K=0\), then \(C_t=0\) and \(u\left(C_t\right)=-\infty\) at every \(t>T^\dagger\), so \(U_0^n=-\infty\) for \(n>T^\dagger\). Let \(\bar K>0\), and take \(j_0\ge1\) with \(\left(1-\delta\right)^{j_0}\bar K\le\bar C\). At \(j\ge j_0\), either \(C_t=0\) and \(u\left(C_t\right)=-\infty\), or \(0<C_t\le\left(1-\delta\right)^j\bar K\le\bar C\), and then, as \(C^{1-\eta}\) decreases in \(C\), \[ \beta^tu\left(C_t\right)\;\le\;-c\bar K^{1-\eta}\beta^{T^\dagger}\Big[\beta\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}\Big]^j\;\le\;-c\bar K^{1-\eta}\beta^{T^\dagger} , \] a negative constant. The finitely many earlier terms are below \(+\infty\), as \(u\) is real-valued at \(C>0\), and the terms \(-\beta^tv\left(P_t\right)\le-\beta^tv\left(0\right)\) add at most \(\left|v\left(0\right)\right|/\left(1-\beta\right)\) to any partial sum. So \(U_0^n\to-\infty\).

Step 3: under the hard ceiling every feasible path is in state A. There \(a_t\varpi_t\le\bar a<1\), so Lemma A(b) gives \(\left(1-\bar a\right)\sum_tH_t\le\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t\le\mathcal B\). With \(R_t=N_t+a_t\varpi_tH_t\le N_t+\bar aH_t\) and Lemma A(a), \[ \bar R\cdot\#\mathcal O\;\le\;\sum_{t\ge0}R_t\;\le\;S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0+\frac{\bar a\,\mathcal B}{1-\bar a}\;<\;\infty , \] the first inequality because \(R_t\ge\bar R\) on \(\mathcal O\). So \(\mathcal O\) is finite, and step 2 applies.

Of \(u\), the proof of (i) uses only that it is nondecreasing with \(u\left(C\right)\le-cC^{1-\eta}\) near zero, for some \(c>0\) and \(\eta>1\).

Proof of (ii)

Proof. Step 4: the first sentence. Let (R) fail, let \(z^\circ\) be a feasible path with a finite value, and let \(z^*\) be optimal. Then \(U_0\left(z^*\right)\ge U_0\left(z^\circ\right)>-\infty\), so \(z^*\) is not in state A, by (i), and its operating set is infinite. As in the proof of (i), (R) fails only for \(\eta>1\), and Lemma V with \(\bar u=0\) gives \(U_0\left(z^*\right)\le-v\left(0\right)/\left(1-\beta\right)\): the value of \(z^*\) is finite. Under the hard ceiling the premise of (ii) never holds, by (i).

Step 5: the loop closes and \(R_\infty\ge\bar R\). Let the ceiling be soft, let Assumption 1(i) hold, and let \(z^*\) converge in the sense of Assumption 1(ii), so that every material flow and stock on it either decays at a constant rate, and then tends to zero, or converges. Material input is bounded, \(R_t\le N_t+\mu\mathcal B\) with \(N_t\to0\) by Lemma A, so either way \(R_t\to R_\infty\) for some finite \(R_\infty\ge0\). As \(\mathcal O\) is infinite, \(R_t\ge\bar R\) at infinitely many dates, so \(R_\infty\ge\bar R>0\). Lemma A(c) then closes the loop, \(\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)<\infty\), so \(a_t\varpi_t\to1\), and \(R^R_t=R_t-N_t\to R_\infty\).

Step 6: Little’s law, \(R_\infty=\mathcal M_\infty/\mathcal T\). The two anthropogenic stocks are material stocks, so they converge, to \(M^K_\infty\) and \(\mathcal W_\infty\), and \(\mathcal M_\infty=M^K_\infty+\mathcal W_\infty\). Since \(R^R_t=a_t\varpi_t\mu\mathcal W_t\), step 5 gives \(R_\infty=\mu\mathcal W_\infty\). The transition \(M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t+\Phi^I_tG_t\) gives \(\Phi^I_tG_t\to\delta M^K_\infty\), so, as \(R_\infty>0\), \(\sigma_t\to\sigma_\infty=\delta M^K_\infty/R_\infty\). Adding the two stocks, \(\mathcal M_\infty=R_\infty/\mu+\sigma_\infty R_\infty/\delta=R_\infty\mathcal T\), with \(\mathcal T\ge1/\mu>0\). So \(R_\infty=\mathcal M_\infty/\mathcal T\ge\bar R\).

Step 7: state C. We read “its goods block grows at a positive rate” as Definition 1(C) does: output, capital and consumption grow at a common rate \(g>0\). A path in state C has this and \(R_\infty>\bar R\). Every other clause of Definition 1(C) holds on \(z^*\): \(N,D\to0\) with \(\sum_tN_t<\infty\), as the two sums of Lemma A(a) converge; \(R\to R_\infty\) with \(R^R\to R_\infty\), and \(a\varpi\to1\) with the loop closed, by step 5; the two stocks converge, by step 6; and \(x\to\infty\). For the last, \(R^R_t>0\) from some date on, so \(\varpi_tH_t>0\) and \(x_t\) is defined; if \(x_t\le\tilde x<\infty\) at infinitely many dates, then at those dates \(a_t\le a\left(\tilde x,t\right)\le a^\infty\left(\tilde x\right)<1\), against \(a_t\ge a_t\varpi_t\to1\). So \(z^*\) is in state C exactly when, in addition, \(R_\infty>\bar R\) and its goods block grows at a positive rate.

Proof of (iii)

Proof. Step 8: where (R) holds, the aftermath is feasible and has a finite value. Let \(\gamma_C\equiv\left[\left(1-\delta\right)/\left(1+\rho\right)\right]^{1/\eta}\) and \(s^{\mathrm{stop}}\equiv\left(1-\delta\right)-\gamma_C\). Each of \(s^{\mathrm{stop}}>0\) and \(\beta\gamma_C^{1-\eta}<1\) is (R), since \(\gamma_C^\eta=\beta\left(1-\delta\right)\) gives \[ \gamma_C<1-\delta\iff\beta\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}<1, \qquad \beta\gamma_C^{1-\eta}=\Big[\beta\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}\Big]^{1/\eta}, \] and \(\beta\left(1-\delta\right)^{1-\eta}<1\) is (R). Let (R) hold, and let the stocks at a date \(\tau\ge0\) be the initial ones if \(\tau=0\), and otherwise those of a path feasible through \(\tau-1\), with \(K_\tau>0\). From \(\tau\) on, set \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=0\), \(K^R_t=0\), \(\Omega_t=0\), \(C_t=s^{\mathrm{stop}}K_t\) and \(K_{t+1}=\gamma_CK_t\), and let the states follow the transitions.

This is feasible. With no treated flow nothing is recovered, so \(R_t=0<\bar R\) and \(Y_t=0\). Every cost flow is zero: \(C^N\left(0,S,t\right)=C^D\left(0,X,t\right)=0\), and \(C^W_t=c^T\left(0\right)H_t=0\). The goods constraint reads \(C_t+I_t+\delta K_t=\left[s^{\mathrm{stop}}+\gamma_C-\left(1-\delta\right)\right]K_t=0=Y_t\). The intensity condition holds as \(0=0\). With \(\Omega_t=0\), \(M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t\ge0\), and the ledger gives \(W_t=R_t-\Delta M^K_{t+1}=\delta M^K_t\ge0\). \(K_t=\gamma_C^{t-\tau}K_\tau>0\), so \(C_t>0\), \(K_{t+1}>0\) and \(K^R_t=0\le K_t\); the other controls are zero; and \(S\) and \(X\) keep their values at \(\tau\), so every domain and mass restriction of Problem 1 holds. No date from \(\tau\) on operates.

Its value from \(\tau\) on is finite. With \(j\equiv t-\tau\), \(C_t=s^{\mathrm{stop}}K_\tau\gamma_C^j\), so for \(\eta\ne1\) the sum \(\sum_{t\ge\tau}\beta^tu\left(C_t\right)\) is \(\beta^\tau\left(s^{\mathrm{stop}}K_\tau\right)^{1-\eta}/\left(1-\eta\right)\) times \(\sum_{j\ge0}\left[\beta\gamma_C^{1-\eta}\right]^j<\infty\), and at \(\eta=1\) it is \(\beta^\tau\sum_{j\ge0}\beta^j\left[\ln\left(s^{\mathrm{stop}}K_\tau\right)+j\ln\gamma_C\right]\), which converges absolutely. For pollution, \(\varpi_t=0\) and \(\mathcal N_t=0\) give \(\Xi_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\), and the transitions of \(M^K\) and \(\mathcal W\) add to \(\mathcal M_{t+1}=\mathcal M_t-\mu\mathcal W_t\), with \(\mathcal M_t\equiv M^K_t+\mathcal W_t\ge0\). So \(\sum_{t\ge\tau}\Xi_t\le\mathcal M_\tau\), and \(P_{t+1}\le P_t+\Xi_t\) gives \(0\le P_t\le P_\tau+\mathcal M_\tau\) for \(t\ge\tau\). As \(v\) is increasing, \(v\left(P_t\right)\) lies between \(v\left(0\right)\) and \(v\left(P_\tau+\mathcal M_\tau\right)\), and \(\sum_{t\ge\tau}\beta^tv\left(P_t\right)\) converges absolutely.

Step 9: (iii). Let \(K_0>0\) and let (R) hold. The aftermath from \(\tau=0\) never operates, so it is a collapse path, and its value is finite by step 8. If (R) fails, every collapse path has value \(-\infty\), by (i). So (R) holds exactly when some collapse path has a finite value.
