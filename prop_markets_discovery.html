O.10 The discovery tax and the retained endowment: an exact split with no sign

Proposition 13 of the paper, stated in Appendix C, compares the retained material endowment of two feasible paths, which it calls the untaxed and the taxed path. It splits the difference exactly into a direct term, from extraction, and an indirect term, from the rates at which the two paths lose matter, signs each term under its premises, and shows that the accounting does not sign their sum. This page proves it in full. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; a symbol the proof needs beyond them is defined where it is first used.

The statement

Proposition 13 (the discovery tax and the retained endowment). General primitives. For any two feasible paths from the same initial stocks, each with nonnegative gross investment, the difference in the retained endowment decomposes exactly into a direct term, the extraction difference weighted by the untaxed path’s retention probabilities, and an indirect term, the change in the leakage of the taxed path’s inflows and of the initial stocks when the untaxed retention schedule replaces the taxed one. The direct term is nonnegative when the untaxed path extracts at least as much at every date; the indirect term is nonpositive when the untaxed path’s loss rates are at least as high at every date and the storage shares coincide, which they do at \(\phi^I=0\). The accounting does not sign the sum: there are two feasible paths meeting all three premises with the untaxed one retaining less.

The names of the two paths come from Section 4.5 of the paper, which reads the proposition as the effect of the discovery tax \(\tau^X\), levied per tonne discovered (Section 4.1). The proposition holds for any two feasible paths, and we label them 0, the untaxed path, and 1, the taxed path. We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions. The decomposition and the two signs are accounting on the constraints of Problem 1 and hold for general primitives; no price, tax or equilibrium condition enters. The example of the last sentence is built in the Cobb-Douglas form of the paper’s workhorse, without a floor, under the soft ceiling and with \(\phi^I=0\).

What the proof uses

From Section 2 of the paper. A feasible path meets every constraint of Problem 1, the goods constraint, the ledger, the intensity condition and the transitions, with the flows as Section 2 defines them, the domains \(C,N,D,K^R,W,\Omega\ge0\), \(K_{t+1}\ge0\) and \(\varpi_t\in\left[0,1\right]\), and the mass restrictions \(S_t\ge0\), \(X_t\le\bar X_{\max}\) and \(M^K_t\ge0\), from given initial stocks with \(\mathcal W_0\ge0\). On it \(\mathcal W_t\ge0\) at every date, by \(W_t\ge0\) and \(\mu<1\), so the handled flow \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\) is nonnegative. Every cost flow is nonnegative: \(C^N\left(0,S,t\right)=0\) with \(C^N_N>0\), likewise \(C^D\), and \(c^c>0\) and \(c^T\ge0\), as \(c^T\left(0\right)=0\) with \(c^{T\prime}>0\). The yield rises from \(a\left(0,t\right)=0\) towards \(\bar a\), so \(0\le a<\bar a\le1\). Recovered material is \(R^R_t=a_t\varpi_tH_t\) with \(a_t\equiv a\left(x_t,t\right)\); at a date with no treated flow, \(\varpi_tH_t=0\), nothing is recovered, and we set \(a_t\varpi_t\equiv0\). The displaced mass \(\mathcal N=\Omega^{N,S}N+\Omega^{D,S}D\) goes to the environment and enters neither \(M^K\) nor \(\mathcal W\), so the inflow of the two anthropogenic stocks is extraction alone.

From Section 3 and Appendices A and B. With \(\mathcal M_t\equiv M^K_t+\mathcal W_t\), the collected ledger of Section 2 reads \(\Delta\mathcal M_{t+1}=N_t-\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t\), and summed over the periods before a date \(\bar t\) it is the summed ledger of Appendix B.1, \[ \mathcal M_{\bar t}=\mathcal M_0+\sum_{t<\bar t}N_t-\sum_{t<\bar t}\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t . \tag{L} \] Proposition 1(i) bounds the inflow: summing the transitions of \(S\) and \(X\), \(\sum_{t<\bar t}N_t=S_0-S_{\bar t}+X_{\bar t}-X_0\), which \(S_{\bar t}\ge0\) and \(X_{\bar t}\le\bar X_{\max}\) bound by the virgin budget \(\bar N\equiv S_0+\bar X_{\max}-X_0\); so \(\sum_tN_t\le\bar N\). Proposition 3 calls \(\mathcal M_\infty\equiv\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal M_t\) the retained material endowment and states that it exists on every feasible path; Lemma M derives this again on the paths the proposition concerns. Appendix B calls \(\ell_t\equiv\mu\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)\) the loss rate of the waste stock, “the share of the stock at the start of period \(t\) that is handled and not recovered”; so \(\ell_t\in\left[0,\mu\right]\), with \(\ell_t=\mu\) at a date with no treated flow. Appendix A calls \(\sigma\equiv\Phi^IG/R\), with \(\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I\), the storage share, “the fraction of the material entering production that new capital embodies”; we set \(\sigma_t\equiv0\) at a date with \(R_t=0\). With a superscript naming the path, the three premises of the proposition read, at every date \(t\), \[ \text{(P1)}\ \ N^0_t\ge N^1_t, \qquad \text{(P2)}\ \ \ell^0_t\ge\ell^1_t, \qquad \text{(P3)}\ \ \sigma^0_t=\sigma^1_t . \]

Retention probabilities

Lemma M. Let a feasible path have \(G_t\ge0\) at every date.

(a) At every date \(\sigma_t\in\left[0,1\right]\) and \(\Phi^I_tG_t=\sigma_tR_t\), and with \(\mathbf z_t\equiv\left(M^K_t,\mathcal W_t\right)^\top\) \[ \mathbf z_{t+1}=\mathbf Q_t\mathbf z_t+\mathbf b_tN_t, \qquad \mathbf Q_t\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1-\delta&\sigma_t\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\\[2pt]\delta&1-\mu+\left(1-\sigma_t\right)\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf b_t\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_t\\[2pt]1-\sigma_t\end{pmatrix}. \tag{Z} \]

(b) For \(t\le\bar t\) let \(\varrho_{t,\bar t}^\top\equiv\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf Q_{\bar t-1}\mathbf Q_{\bar t-2}\cdots\mathbf Q_t\), with \(\mathbf 1\equiv\left(1,1\right)^\top\) and \(\varrho_{\bar t,\bar t}\equiv\mathbf 1\). Its entries lie in \(\left[0,1\right]\) and do not increase with \(\bar t\), so the limit \(\varrho_t\equiv\lim_{\bar t\to\infty}\varrho_{t,\bar t}\equiv\left(\varrho^M_t,\varrho^{\mathcal W}_t\right)^\top\) exists in \(\left[0,1\right]^2\).

(c) The retained endowment exists, and with \(\varrho^N_s\equiv\varrho_{s+1}^\top\mathbf b_s\in\left[0,1\right]\), \[ \mathcal M_\infty=\varrho^M_0M^K_0+\varrho^{\mathcal W}_0\mathcal W_0+\sum_{s\ge0}\varrho^N_sN_s , \tag{R} \] the series converging.

We call \(\varrho^M_t\) and \(\varrho^{\mathcal W}_t\) the retention probabilities of a tonne in \(M^K_t\) and in \(\mathcal W_t\), and \(\varrho^N_s\) that of a tonne extracted at \(s\); a path’s retention schedule is the sequence of them. They depend on the path only through its loss rates and storage shares. (Z) says how a tonne moves: one in \(M^K_t\) is released into the waste stock with probability \(\delta\), and one in \(\mathcal W_t\) is lost with probability \(\ell_t\) and is otherwise, at \(t+1\), in the waste stock or, recovered and stored, in capital; \(\varrho_{t,\bar t}\) is the probability that a tonne held at \(t\) is still held at \(\bar t\). The proof uses only the algebra.

Proof.

  1. Every term of \(\mathcal D_t\) is a relative intensity \(\omega^j\ge0\) times a nonnegative flow: consumption, a cost flow, or output, which is nonnegative because the goods constraint gives \(Y_t=C_t+G_t+C^N_t+C^D_t+C^W_t\ge0\) when \(G_t\ge0\). In the intensity condition \(R_t=\Omega_t\mathcal D_t+\Phi^I_tG_t\) both terms are then nonnegative, as \(\Omega_t\ge0\) and \(\phi^I\ge0\), so \(0\le\Phi^I_tG_t\le R_t\). This gives \(\sigma_t\in\left[0,1\right]\), and where \(R_t=0\) it gives \(\Phi^I_tG_t=0=\sigma_tR_t\). The transition of \(M^K\) becomes \(M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t+\sigma_tR_t\), the ledger \(W_t=R_t-\Delta M^K_{t+1}=\left(1-\sigma_t\right)R_t+\delta M^K_t\), and the transition of the waste stock \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_t+\delta M^K_t+\left(1-\sigma_t\right)R_t\). Substituting \(R_t=N_t+a_t\varpi_t\mu\mathcal W_t=N_t+\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\mathcal W_t\) gives (Z).

  2. As \(0\le\ell_t\le\mu<1\), \(0\le\sigma_t\le1\) and \(0<\delta<1\), \(\mathbf Q_t\) and \(\mathbf b_t\) are entrywise nonnegative, with column sums \(\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf Q_t=\left(1,\,1-\ell_t\right)\le\mathbf 1^\top\) and \(\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf b_t=1\). So \(\varrho_{t,\bar t}\ge0\), and for \(\bar t>t\) \[ \varrho_{t,\bar t}^\top=\left(\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf Q_{\bar t-1}\right)\mathbf Q_{\bar t-2}\cdots\mathbf Q_t\;\le\;\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf Q_{\bar t-2}\cdots\mathbf Q_t=\varrho_{t,\bar t-1}^\top , \] the matrices being nonnegative and an empty product being the identity. By induction \(\varrho_{t,\bar t}\le\varrho_{t,t}=\mathbf 1\), and a nonincreasing sequence in \(\left[0,1\right]\) converges.

  3. Iterating (Z) from \(0\) to \(\bar t\) and multiplying by \(\mathbf 1^\top\), since \(\mathcal M_{\bar t}=\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf z_{\bar t}\), \[ \mathcal M_{\bar t}=\varrho_{0,\bar t}^\top\mathbf z_0+\sum_{s<\bar t}f_s\left(\bar t\right), \qquad f_s\left(\bar t\right)\equiv\varrho_{s+1,\bar t}^\top\mathbf b_sN_s . \] By (b), \(f_s\left(\bar t\right)\in\left[0,N_s\right]\) and \(f_s\left(\bar t\right)\to\varrho^N_sN_s\) as \(\bar t\to\infty\), with \(\varrho^N_s\in\left[0,1\right]\). Since \(\sum_sN_s\le\bar N\), for every \(\upsilon>0\) there is a date \(s_\upsilon\) with \(\sum_{s\ge s_\upsilon}N_s<\upsilon\), and for \(\bar t>s_\upsilon\) \[ \Big|\sum_{s<\bar t}f_s\left(\bar t\right)-\sum_{s\ge0}\varrho^N_sN_s\Big|\;\le\;\sum_{s<s_\upsilon}\big|f_s\left(\bar t\right)-\varrho^N_sN_s\big|+2\upsilon , \] where the finite sum tends to zero. As \(\varrho_{0,\bar t}^\top\mathbf z_0\to\varrho_0^\top\mathbf z_0\) too, \(\mathcal M_{\bar t}\) converges, to the right side of (R).

At fixed loss rates and storage shares the right side of (R) is linear in the initial stocks and the inflow. And what is not retained leaks: letting \(\bar t\to\infty\) in (L), the cumulated leakage \(\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)H_t\) equals \(\mathcal M_0+\sum_tN_t-\mathcal M_\infty\), which by (R) is the initial stocks and the inflow each weighted by one minus its retention probability.

Proof of Proposition 13

Let paths 0 and 1 be feasible from the same initial stocks, so with a common \(\mathbf z_0=\left(M^K_0,\mathcal W_0\right)^\top\ge0\), and let each have \(G_t\ge0\) at every date; write \(\Delta Z\equiv Z^0-Z^1\). For a mass made of initial stocks \(\mathbf z\ge0\) and a nonnegative summable inflow \(e\), let \[ \mathcal E^j\left(\mathbf z,e\right)\equiv\left(\varrho^j_0\right)^\top\mathbf z+\sum_{s\ge0}\varrho^{N,j}_se_s , \] the part of it that path \(j\)’s retention schedule retains, at most \(\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf z+\sum_se_s\); the rest, \(\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf z+\sum_se_s-\mathcal E^j\left(\mathbf z,e\right)\), is the part that schedule lets leak. By Lemma M, \(\mathcal M^j_\infty=\mathcal E^j\left(\mathbf z_0,N^j\right)\), and by the paragraph after it the leakage of path 1’s own mass \(\left(\mathbf z_0,N^1\right)\) under its own schedule is what path 1 actually loses, \(\sum_t\left(1-a^1_t\varpi^1_t\right)H^1_t\).

Proof. Step 1: the decomposition is exact. Adding and subtracting \(\mathcal E^0\left(\mathbf z_0,N^1\right)\), \[ \Delta\mathcal M_\infty =\underbrace{\sum_{s\ge0}\varrho^{N,0}_s\,\Delta N_s}_{\text{direct}} +\underbrace{\big(\varrho^0_0-\varrho^1_0\big)^\top\mathbf z_0+\sum_{s\ge0}\big(\varrho^{N,0}_s-\varrho^{N,1}_s\big)N^1_s}_{\text{indirect}} , \tag{D} \] every series converging absolutely, its terms being bounded by \(N^0_s+N^1_s\). The direct term is the extraction difference weighted by the untaxed path’s retention probabilities. The indirect term is \(\mathcal E^0\left(\mathbf z_0,N^1\right)-\mathcal E^1\left(\mathbf z_0,N^1\right)\), which is the leakage of the taxed path’s inflow and of the initial stocks under the taxed schedule less their leakage under the untaxed schedule. It is the change in that leakage when the untaxed retention schedule replaces the taxed one, counted as what it does to the endowment, that is with a minus sign: more leakage, less endowment.

Step 2: under (P1) the direct term is nonnegative. Every weight \(\varrho^{N,0}_s\) is nonnegative by Lemma M, and every \(\Delta N_s\) is nonnegative under (P1).

Step 3: under (P2) and (P3) the indirect term is nonpositive. At a common \(\sigma_t\) the two entries of the second column of \(\mathbf Q_t\), \(\sigma_t\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\) and \(1-\mu+\left(1-\sigma_t\right)\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\), are nonincreasing in \(\ell_t\), and the first column does not involve \(\ell_t\); so (P2) and (P3) give \(\mathbf Q^0_t\le\mathbf Q^1_t\) entrywise. Backward from \(\varrho^0_{\bar t,\bar t}=\varrho^1_{\bar t,\bar t}=\mathbf 1\), if \(\varrho^0_{t+1,\bar t}\le\varrho^1_{t+1,\bar t}\), then \[ \varrho^0_{t,\bar t}=\left(\mathbf Q^0_t\right)^\top\varrho^0_{t+1,\bar t}\;\le\;\left(\mathbf Q^0_t\right)^\top\varrho^1_{t+1,\bar t}\;\le\;\left(\mathbf Q^1_t\right)^\top\varrho^1_{t+1,\bar t}=\varrho^1_{t,\bar t}, \] the first inequality because \(\mathbf Q^0_t\ge0\) and the second because \(\varrho^1_{t+1,\bar t}\ge0\). In the limit \(\varrho^0_t\le\varrho^1_t\), and with the common \(\mathbf b_s\), \(\varrho^{N,0}_s\le\varrho^{N,1}_s\). As \(\mathbf z_0\ge0\) and \(N^1_s\ge0\), every term of the indirect term is nonpositive. At \(\phi^I=0\), \(\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I=0\), so \(\sigma_t=0\) on every path and (P3) holds.

The last sentence of the proposition is proved in Two paths on which the untaxed one retains less. Discovery appears in neither (R) nor (D): its displaced mass goes to the environment, so exploration reaches the endowment only through the extraction of the reserves it finds, in the direct term, and through whatever differences in loss rates and storage shares accompany it, in the indirect one.

Two paths on which the untaxed one retains less

The example needs a loop that closes, and the economy below lets one be built from any date at which the waste stock is positive and capital is large enough.

The economy. There is no floor, \(\bar R=0\), and output is \[ \mathcal F\left(K^Y,R^e,P,t\right)=A_0\Gamma^te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(R^e\right)^{\gamma}, \qquad A_0>0,\ \ \Gamma>1,\ \ \kappa>0,\ \ \beta_K,\gamma>0,\ \ \beta_K+\gamma<1 , \] the Cobb-Douglas form of the paper’s workhorse with \(B_t\equiv1\) and \(A_t=A_0\Gamma^t\). It has the signs of Section 2, is concave in \(\left(K^Y,R^e\right)\), its Hessian there having negative diagonal and determinant proportional to \(\beta_K\gamma\left(1-\beta_K-\gamma\right)>0\), and has \(\mathcal F_K\to\infty\) as \(K^Y\to0\) at any \(R^e>0\). The yield is \(a\left(x\right)=1-e^{-x}\), which has the properties of Section 2 under the soft ceiling \(\bar a=1\); \(\phi^I=0\) and \(\omega^Y>0\). Every other primitive is any that Section 2 allows. The initial stocks are \(M^K_0=0\), \(\mathcal W_0>0\), \(S_0>0\), \(X_0\le\bar X_{\max}\), \(P_0\ge0\) and a capital stock \(K_0\) chosen below, as \(A_0\) is. With \(\phi^I=0\) the transition of \(M^K\) keeps \(M^K_t=0\), so \(\mathcal M_t=\mathcal W_t\), the ledger gives \(W_t=R_t\), and \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\ell_t\right)\mathcal W_t+N_t\). Fix \(\hat x\) with \(e^{-\hat x}\le\min\left\{\delta,\tfrac14\right\}\), let \(\hat x_t\equiv\hat x+t\), and let \(c^{\mathrm{full}}\equiv c^c\left(0\right)+c^T\left(1,0\right)\), which bounds the handling cost per handled tonne at full treatment at every date, both charges being nonincreasing in time.

Lemma K. In this economy take the stocks at a date \(t_0\ge0\), the initial ones if \(t_0=0\) and otherwise those left by a path that meets every constraint, domain and mass restriction of Problem 1 at the dates before \(t_0\), and let them have \(M^K_{t_0}=0\), \(S_{t_0}\ge0\), \(X_{t_0}\le\bar X_{\max}\), \(P_{t_0}\ge0\), \(w\equiv\mathcal W_{t_0}>0\) and \(K_{t_0}>\hat x_{t_0}\mu w\); let \(k\equiv K_{t_0}-\hat x_{t_0}\mu w\). For \(t\ge t_0\) set \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=1\), \(K^R_t=\hat x_t\mu\mathcal W_t\), \(K_{t+1}=k+K^R_{t+1}\) and \(I_t\equiv K_{t+1}-K_t\), and take \(C_t\) from the goods constraint, \(\Omega_t\equiv R_t/\mathcal D_t\), \(W_t\) from the ledger and the other states from the transitions. Then for \(t\ge t_0\), \(\ell_t=\mu e^{-\hat x_t}\) and \(\mathcal W_t\) is nonincreasing with \(\tfrac12w\le\mathcal W_t\le w\), so \(\tfrac12w\le\mathcal M_\infty\le w\). If moreover \[ A_0\,\underline y\;\ge\;\max\Big\{\delta k+\left(\hat x+1+c^{\mathrm{full}}\right)\mu w,\ \frac{\mu w}{\ln\Gamma}\Big\}, \qquad \underline y\equiv e^{-\kappa\left(P_{t_0}+w\right)}k^{\beta_K}\left(\tfrac38\mu w\right)^{\gamma}, \tag{K} \] every constraint, domain and mass restriction of Problem 1 holds at every \(t\ge t_0\), with \(G_t>0\), so that the path continued in this way is feasible.

Proof. The material block does not involve capital. At \(t\ge t_0\) the treated flow is \(\varpi_tH_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\), positive by induction, so capital per treated tonne is \(x_t=K^R_t/\left(\varpi_tH_t\right)=\hat x_t\), \(R_t=R^R_t=\left(1-e^{-\hat x_t}\right)\mu\mathcal W_t\), \(\ell_t=\mu e^{-\hat x_t}\) and \(\mathcal W_{t+1}=\left(1-\ell_t\right)\mathcal W_t\). So \(\mathcal W_{t+1}\), and with it \(K_{t+1}\), is fixed at \(t\), and the construction is recursive. As \(\hat x_s\ge\hat x+\left(s-t_0\right)\), \(\sum_{s\ge t_0}\ell_s\le\mu e^{-\hat x}\sum_{j\ge0}e^{-j}<2e^{-\hat x}\le\tfrac12\), and \(\prod_s\left(1-\ell_s\right)\ge1-\sum_s\ell_s\) by induction, so \(w\ge\mathcal W_t\ge\tfrac12w\) and \(R_t\ge\left(1-e^{-\hat x}\right)\tfrac12\mu w\ge\tfrac38\mu w\). No mass is displaced, and the handled stream emits \(\Xi_t=d^We^{-\hat x_t}\mu\mathcal W_t=d^W\ell_t\mathcal W_t\le\mathcal W_t-\mathcal W_{t+1}\), so from \(0\le P_{t+1}\le P_t+\Xi_t\), as \(\theta\in\left(0,1\right)\) and \(P_{t_0}\ge0\), \(0\le P_t\le P_{t_0}+w\).

Capital. \(K_t=k+K^R_t\) at every \(t\ge t_0\), at \(t_0\) by the definition of \(k\), so \(K^Y_t=k>0\) and \(K^R_t<K_t\). Since \(\ell_t<e^{-\hat x}\le\delta\) and \(\hat x_{t+1}=\hat x_t+1\), \[ G_t=K_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)K_t=\delta k+\mu\mathcal W_t\Big[\left(\hat x_t+1\right)\left(1-\ell_t\right)-\left(1-\delta\right)\hat x_t\Big]\;\ge\;\delta k>0 , \] and \(G_t\le\delta k+\hat x_{t+1}\mu\mathcal W_{t+1}\le\delta k+\left(\hat x+t+1\right)\mu w\).

Consumption. By the bounds on \(R_t\) and \(P_t\), \(Y_t\ge A_0\Gamma^t\underline y\). Extraction and exploration cost nothing, and \(C^W_t=\left[c^c\left(t\right)+c^T\left(1,t\right)\right]\mu\mathcal W_t\le c^{\mathrm{full}}\mu w\). With \(\Gamma^t\ge1+t\ln\Gamma\), \[ C_t=Y_t-G_t-C^W_t\;\ge\;\Big[A_0\underline y-\delta k-\left(\hat x+1+c^{\mathrm{full}}\right)\mu w\Big]+t\Big[A_0\underline y\ln\Gamma-\mu w\Big]\;\ge\;0 \] by (K).

The rest. \(\mathcal D_t\ge\omega^YY_t>0\), so \(\Omega_t\ge0\) is finite and the intensity condition holds; the ledger gives \(W_t=R_t\ge0\); \(N_t=D_t=0\), \(\varpi_t=1\), \(K^R_t\ge0\) and \(K_{t+1}>0\); \(S\) and \(X\) do not move and \(M^K=0\), so the mass restrictions hold; and the goods constraint holds by the definition of \(C_t\).

Proof of the last sentence of Proposition 13. Take the economy above. Choose \(n\in\left(0,S_0\right]\), an integer \(T_0\) with \(\left(1-\mu\right)^{T_0}\left(\mathcal W_0+n\right)<\tfrac12\mathcal W_0\), then \(K_0>\left(1-\delta\right)^{-T_0-1}\left(\hat x+T_0+1\right)\mu\left(\mathcal W_0+n\right)\), and last \(A_0\) large enough for the three inequalities named below, none of whose other terms involves \(A_0\).

Proof. Path 1, the taxed path, is Lemma K’s continuation from date 0, with \(w=\mathcal W_0\) and \(K_0>\hat x\mu\mathcal W_0\) by the choice of \(K_0\); (K) for it is the first inequality. It never extracts, has \(\ell^1_t=\mu e^{-\hat x_t}\) at every date, and retains \(\mathcal M^1_\infty\ge\tfrac12\mathcal W_0\).

Path 0, the untaxed path, extracts \(N^0_0=n\) at date 0 and treats nothing: \(D^0_0=0\), \(\varpi^0_0=0\), \(K^{R,0}_0=0\), \(G^0_0=0\), so \(K^0_1=\left(1-\delta\right)K_0\), and, \(C^D\) and \(C^W\) being zero, \(C^0_0=Y^0_0-C^N\left(n,S_0,0\right)\) with \(Y^0_0=A_0e^{-\kappa P_0}K_0^{\beta_K}n^{\gamma}\), nonnegative under the second inequality, \(A_0e^{-\kappa P_0}K_0^{\beta_K}n^{\gamma}\ge C^N\left(n,S_0,0\right)\). Then \(\mathcal D^0_0\ge\omega^YY^0_0>0\) and \(\Omega^0_0=n/\mathcal D^0_0\); \(S^0_1=S_0-n\ge0\); the ledger gives \(W^0_0=n\), and \(\mathcal W^0_1=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_0+n\). At the dates \(1\le t\le T_0\) the path is idle: it extracts, discovers and treats nothing, so \(R^0_t=0\), output, every cost flow, \(\mathcal D^0_t\), consumption and gross investment are zero, \(\Omega^0_t=0\), \(K^0_{t+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)K^0_t\) and \(\mathcal W^0_{t+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W^0_t\), and every constraint holds. From \(T_0+1\) on it is Lemma K’s continuation from \(w^0\equiv\mathcal W^0_{T_0+1}=\left(1-\mu\right)^{T_0}\left[\left(1-\mu\right)\mathcal W_0+n\right]\), with \(K^0_{T_0+1}=\left(1-\delta\right)^{T_0+1}K_0>\hat x_{T_0+1}\mu w^0\) by the choice of \(K_0\), \(M^K_{T_0+1}=0\), \(S^0_{T_0+1}=S_0-n\ge0\), \(X^0_{T_0+1}=X_0\) and \(P^0_{T_0+1}\ge0\), pollution staying nonnegative from \(P_0\ge0\) as \(\Xi\ge0\) and \(\theta<1\). (K) for it is the third inequality, in which \(P^0_{T_0+1}\) is fixed by the material choices before \(T_0+1\).

Both paths are feasible from the same initial stocks, with \(G\ge0\) at every date. (P1): \(N^0_0=n>0=N^1_0\), and neither path extracts later. (P2): \(\ell^0_t=\mu>\ell^1_t\) at \(t\le T_0\), where path 0 treats nothing, and \(\ell^0_t=\mu e^{-\hat x_t}=\ell^1_t\) after. (P3): \(\phi^I=0\). Yet, by Lemma K and the choice of \(T_0\), \[ \mathcal M^0_\infty\;\le\;w^0\;<\;\left(1-\mu\right)^{T_0}\left(\mathcal W_0+n\right)\;<\;\tfrac12\mathcal W_0\;\le\;\mathcal M^1_\infty . \] The untaxed path retains less although its direct term is positive. The definition of Lemma M(b) gives \(\varrho_{t,\bar t}=\mathbf Q_t^\top\varrho_{t+1,\bar t}\), whose second entry at \(\sigma\equiv0\) is \(\left(1-\ell_t\right)\varrho^{\mathcal W}_{t+1,\bar t}\); with \(\mathbf b_s=\left(0,1\right)^\top\) this gives \(\varrho^{N,0}_0=\prod_{t\ge1}\left(1-\ell^0_t\right)=\left(1-\mu\right)^{T_0}\prod_{t>T_0}\left(1-\ell^0_t\right)\ge\tfrac12\left(1-\mu\right)^{T_0}\), by the bound on \(\sum_{t>T_0}\ell^0_t\) in the proof of Lemma K. So the direct term is \(n\varrho^{N,0}_0>0\): path 0 keeps part of what it extracts, but its idle dates lose the initial stock that path 1 keeps.

The hard ceiling. Under the hard ceiling the proposition is empty, every retention probability being zero. If \(\bar a<1\), then \(\ell_t\ge\underline\ell\equiv\mu\left(1-\bar a\right)>0\) at every date, as \(a_t\varpi_t\le a_t<\bar a\) where a flow is treated and \(a_t\varpi_t=0\) where none is. Multiplying out, \[ \mathbf 1^\top\mathbf Q_{t+1}\mathbf Q_t=\Big(1-\delta\ell_{t+1},\ \ 1-\ell_t-\ell_{t+1}\left[1-\mu+\left(1-\sigma_t\right)\left(\mu-\ell_t\right)\right]\Big)\;\le\;\left(1-\delta\underline\ell\right)\mathbf 1^\top , \] the bracket being nonnegative, and since every \(\mathbf Q_t\) is nonnegative with \(\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf Q_t\le\mathbf 1^\top\), taking the factors of \(\varrho_{t,\bar t}^\top\) two at a time gives \(\varrho_{t,\bar t}\le\left(1-\delta\underline\ell\right)^{\lfloor\left(\bar t-t\right)/2\rfloor}\mathbf 1\to0\). So \(\varrho_t=0\) at every date, both terms of (D) vanish, and \(\mathcal M_\infty=0\) on every feasible path with nonnegative gross investment.
