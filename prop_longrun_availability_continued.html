O.1, continued: the workhorse’s balanced paths

The second sentence of item (iii) of Proposition 4 of the paper (availability) makes two claims about the Cobb-Douglas workhorse of Section 3.6: its circular balanced growth path is a candidate configuration, subject to two inequalities on its wedges, and a candidate state-B path with converging recycling margins has the rates of Appendix B.6, which exist exactly when \(\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)+\gamma>0\). This page proves both, as Lemmas W1 and W2. Symbols are the paper’s, as on the notation page; Lemma A, Lemma P and the conditions (C), (G), (KR), (T) and (PP) are on the page of lemmas shared by the long-run proofs. We take the simplest route for the paper’s assumptions: the workhorse’s exponential yield makes the recycling-capital margin explicit, and we take the two inequalities on the wedges as given, proving nothing about whether they hold.

What the lemmas use

From the paper’s long-run section, besides what the shared page restates, the material budget \(\mathcal B\) among it:

  • State B (Definition 1): “balanced dematerialization if \(C_t>0\) at every date and, asymptotically, \(C\), \(Y\) and \(K^Y\) grow at a common rate \(g\) with \(Y/C\) bounded above and away from zero, while \(R\) and \(\mathcal W\) decay at a common rate \(\nu\in\left(0,1\right)\)”.
  • Assumption 1(iii), first half: “\(\theta\left(P\right)\ge\theta_{\min}>0\) for all \(P\)”.
  • The closure criterion (Proposition 7): at a date with \(\varpi_tH_t>0\), \(x_t=K^R_t/\left(\varpi_tH_t\right)\) and the leakage rate is \(L_t\equiv1-a_t\); along a candidate whose margin (KR) is interior with \(\mathcal G^K_t\ne0\) from a date \(T_0\) on, the criterion is \(\sum_{t\ge T_0}L_t<\infty\).

The workhorse (Section 3.6 and Appendix B.6). For \(R\ge\bar R\), \(F=A_te^{-\kappa P}\left(K^Y\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR^e\right)^{\gamma}\) with \(\kappa\ge0\), \(\beta_K,\gamma>0\), \(\beta_K+\gamma<1\) and the trends of Assumption 1(i), so that above the floor \(F_K=\beta_KY/K^Y\), \(F_R=\gamma Y/R^e\) and \(F_P=-\kappa Y\). The yield is exponential, \(a\left(x\right)=\bar a\left(1-e^{-\xi x}\right)\) with \(\xi>0\); under the soft ceiling \(a\left(x\right)=1-e^{-\xi x}\), \(a'\left(x\right)=\xi e^{-\xi x}\) and \(\alpha=a-a'x=1-\left(1+\xi x\right)e^{-\xi x}\). Appendix B.6 takes the forms of Section 3.6 “with the marginal extraction cost proportional to \(\left(S_{\mathrm{ref}}/S\right)^{\mu_N}\) as the reserve falls”, which we read as \(C^N_N=c_N\left(N,t\right)\left(S_{\mathrm{ref}}/S\right)^{\mu_N}\) with a factor \(c_N\) that does not depend on \(S\) and tends to a positive limit \(c_{N\infty}\) as \(N\to0\) and \(t\to\infty\). Utility is CRRA, \(u'\left(C\right)=C^{-\eta}\) with \(\eta>0\). Write \(\Gamma\equiv\left(1+g_A\right)\left(1+g_B\right)^{\gamma}\), which exceeds one since \(g_A+g_B>0\), and \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}\) for the rate with \(\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\Gamma\). Section 2 has the collection and treatment charges “both charges positive and non-increasing in time”, so the handling cost per handled tonne at full treatment is at most \(c^c\left(0\right)+c^T\left(1,0\right)\).

The first-order conditions of Appendix A used. Besides (C), (G), (KR), (T) and (PP), Appendix A states the conditions in \(\Omega\), \(I\) and \(W\), \[ \zeta=\sigma\left(p^W-p^M\right), \tag{$\Omega$} \] \[ q=1-\Phi^I\left(1-\sigma\right)\left(p^W-p^M\right), \tag{I} \] \[ p^W_t=-p^{\mathcal W}_t , \tag{W} \] with the storage share \(\sigma\equiv\Phi^IG/R\); the costate recursions of capital, embodied material and the waste stock, with \(1+r_{t+1}\equiv\lambda_t/\left(\beta\lambda_{t+1}\right)\), \[ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)q_t=\tilde F_{K,t+1}+\left(1-\delta\right)q_{t+1}, \tag{Q} \] \[ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^M_t=\delta\,p^W_{t+1}+\left(1-\delta\right)p^M_{t+1}, \tag{M} \] \[ \left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^{\mathcal W}_t=\mu\,h_{t+1}+\left(1-\mu\right)p^{\mathcal W}_{t+1}, \qquad h\equiv\alpha\varpi\left(\Psi-p^W\right)-c^W\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)-p^P\left[1-\varpi\left(1-d^W\right)-d^W\alpha\varpi\right]. \tag{H} \] The treatment margin is at its upper corner, \(\varpi=1\), when \(\mathcal G^\varpi\ge\left[c^c+c^{T\prime}\left(1\right)\right]\left(1+\zeta\Omega^W\right)\). Appendix B.6: “Where a balanced price block is used we add two regularity conditions on the preference pair: \(1+r\equiv\left(1+\rho\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta}>1+g\), with \(\eta\) the elasticity of marginal utility, which also keeps lifetime utility finite on a path growing at \(g\)”. We use this first condition, which is \(\beta\left(1+g\right)^{1-\eta}<1\).

The circular balanced growth path. Appendix B.6 defines it and says what makes it a candidate configuration: “In full, the circular balanced growth path of Section 3.6 is a path along which, asymptotically, \(R_t\to R_\infty>\bar R\), \(\varpi_t=1\), \(a_t\to1\) with \(x_t\to\infty\), \(N,D\to0\) with \(\sum_tN_t<\infty\), \(P\to0\), and \(C\), \(K\), \(Y\) and every final-good flow grow at the common rate \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}\). […] Deflated by output per effective tonne, the prices of Appendix A solve a linear block in closed form, […]. The block is admissible only if the material value and the consumption wedge stay positive, inequalities that hold for small \(\phi^I\) and are properties of a calibration, not of the primitives. Treatment is full from a finite date on, by Proposition 7(b).” We take \(\varpi_t=1\) from a date \(T_1\) on, and \(C_t/Y_t\) and \(K_t/Y_t\) converging to positive limits \(c_Y\) and \(k\), as Assumption 1(ii) gives for a goods block growing at a common rate. We read “every final-good flow” as the uses of output other than the three cost flows \(C^N\), \(C^D\) and \(C^W\): the handling cost \(C^W\) stays bounded on the path, so the clause cannot cover it. Step 1 of the proof of Lemma W1 shows that \(C^W\) vanishes against output; part (c) of the lemma takes as a premise that the extraction and discovery costs do too, \(\left(C^N_t+C^D_t\right)/Y_t\to0\). We write \(g\) for the common rate until step 3 of the proof of Lemma W1 shows that the production function forces it to be \(g^{\mathrm{circ}}\). Write \(R^e_\infty\equiv R_\infty-\bar R>0\). For a price \(m\) in final goods per tonne, its deflated price is \(\hat m_t\equiv m_tR^e_\infty/Y_t\); and \(\hat\Omega_t\equiv\Omega_tY_t/R^e_\infty\), so that \(\zeta\Omega^j=\hat\zeta\hat\Omega\,\omega^j\) for every use \(j\). Lemma W1 states the block exactly, the two inequalities on its wedges being \[ \hat\Psi\equiv\lim_t\hat\Psi_t>0, \qquad \lim_t\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^C_t\right)>0 . \tag{WI} \]

The circular balanced growth path

Lemma W1. Take the workhorse under the soft ceiling, and let the regularity condition \(r>g\) hold.

(a) Let a candidate be on the circular balanced growth path, with \(q_t\) and the deflated prices \(\hat\Psi_t\), \(\hat p^W_t\), \(\hat p^M_t\), \(\hat\zeta_t\) convergent, and with the wedges \(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\) and \(1+\zeta_t\Omega^C_t\) converging to positive limits, which is the wedge provision of Appendix A in the limit. Then \(\left(1+g\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\Gamma\), so \(g=g^{\mathrm{circ}}>0\), and the limits are \[ \hat\Psi=\frac{\gamma}{1+\Upsilon\left(1-\gamma\hat\Omega\omega^Y\right)}, \quad \hat\zeta=-\Upsilon\hat\Psi, \quad \hat p^W=-\Theta_{\mathcal W}\hat\Psi, \quad \hat p^M=\Theta_M\hat p^W, \quad q_\infty=1+\phi^I\left(1-\sigma_\infty\right)\Theta_\sigma\hat\Omega\hat\Psi, \tag{B} \] with \(\Theta_{\mathcal W}\equiv\mu\left(1+g\right)/\left(r-g\right)\), \(\Theta_M\equiv\delta\left(1+g\right)/\left[1+r-\left(1-\delta\right)\left(1+g\right)\right]\in\left(0,1\right)\), \(\Theta_\sigma\equiv\left(1-\Theta_M\right)\Theta_{\mathcal W}>0\), \(\Upsilon\equiv\Theta_\sigma\sigma_\infty\ge0\), and \(\sigma_\infty\), \(\hat\Omega\) the limits of \(\sigma_t\), \(\hat\Omega_t\). The capital-output ratio satisfies the capital arbitrage \(\left(r+\delta\right)q_\infty k=\beta_K\left(1-\hat\zeta\hat\Omega\omega^Y\right)\). The limit of \(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\) is positive exactly when \(\hat\Psi>0\), so the two positive limits are the inequalities (WI).

(b) Along the candidate of (a), from a finite date on, the recycling-capital margin (KR) has the unique root \(x_t=\xi^{-1}\ln\left(\xi\mathcal G^K_t/\tilde F_{K,t}\right)>0\); the leakage rate \(L_t\) falls at least geometrically, so the closure criterion holds and the loop closes along the margin; and \(\mathcal G^\varpi_t\to+\infty\), so the condition for the treatment margin’s upper corner holds if the marginal handling charge at full treatment, \(c^c\left(t\right)+c^{T\prime}\left(1,t\right)\), is bounded over \(t\).

(c) Conversely, take \(g=g^{\mathrm{circ}}\) and \(R_\infty>\bar R\), and let the extraction and discovery costs vanish against output, \(\left(C^N_t+C^D_t\right)/Y_t\to0\). The capital arbitrage, with the block (B) and the limits \(\sigma_\infty\) and \(\hat\Omega\) of step 1 below, has a root \(k\) with \(\left(g+\delta\right)k\in\left(0,1\right)\); and at a root where (WI) holds, the limits (B) satisfy the limit forms of (C), (\(\Omega\)), (I), (W), (Q), (M), (H) and of the definition of \(\Psi\).

Proof. Step 1: the intensities and the discount rate. By (C), \(\lambda_t=C_t^{-\eta}/\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^C_t\right)\); with \(C\) growing at \(g\) and the consumption wedge converging to a positive limit, \(1+r_{t+1}=\lambda_t/\left(\beta\lambda_{t+1}\right)\to\left(1+\rho\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta}=1+r\). Steps 2 and 3 take only this limit from this step, and step 3 shows that \(g>0\), which the rest of this step uses. Since \(G_t/Y_t=\left[K_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)K_t\right]/Y_t\to\left(g+\delta\right)k>0\), and \(C^W_t/Y_t\to0\) as \(C^W_t=\left[c^c\left(t\right)+c^T\left(1,t\right)\right]H_t\le\left[c^c\left(0\right)+c^T\left(1,0\right)\right]\mu\mathcal B\) for \(t\ge T_1\), by the bound on the charges and Lemma A(b), the goods constraint gives \(c_Y=1-\left(g+\delta\right)k\) under the premise of (c). If \(\phi^I=0\), (\(\Omega\)) gives \(\zeta_t=0\) at every date, every wedge is one and \(\sigma_t=0\), and \(\hat\Omega\) enters what follows only multiplied by \(\hat\zeta\) or by \(\phi^I\). If \(\phi^I>0\), the material stock \(M^K\) converges, as Assumption 1(ii) has it decay at a constant rate or converge, and so does \(\Phi^I_tG_t=M^K_{t+1}-\left(1-\delta\right)M^K_t\), by the transition of embodied material; hence \(\sigma_t=\Phi^I_tG_t/R_t\), which the intensity condition writes as \(\phi^IG_t/\left(\mathcal D_t+\phi^IG_t\right)\in\left[0,1\right]\), converges to some \(\sigma_\infty\in\left[0,1\right]\), and \(\hat\Omega_t=\sigma_tR_tY_t/\left(\phi^IG_tR^e_\infty\right)\) converges to some \(\hat\Omega\ge0\). Under the premise of (c), as \(\mathcal D=\omega^YY+\omega^NC^N+\omega^DC^D+\omega^WC^W+\omega^CC\), \(\left(\mathcal D_t+\phi^IG_t\right)/Y_t\to\bar d\equiv\omega^Y+\omega^Cc_Y+\phi^I\left(g+\delta\right)k\ge\phi^I\left(g+\delta\right)k>0\), so that \(\hat\Omega=R_\infty/\left(\bar dR^e_\infty\right)\) and \(\sigma_\infty=\phi^I\left(g+\delta\right)k/\bar d\). Deflating a recursion of the form (Q), (M) or (H) multiplies its right side by \(Y_{t+1}/Y_t\to1+g\).

Step 2: the material-adjusted marginal product of capital. By (Q), \(\tilde F_{K,t+1}=\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)q_t-\left(1-\delta\right)q_{t+1}\to\left(r+\delta\right)q_\infty\). Let \(\vartheta_Y>0\) be the limit of \(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\). Since \(K^Y_t\le K_t\), \(\tilde F_{K,t}=\beta_K\left(Y_t/K^Y_t\right)\left(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\right)\ge\beta_K\left(Y_t/K_t\right)\left(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\right)\to\beta_K\vartheta_Y/k>0\). So \(\tilde F_{K,t}\ge\underline F\) for a constant \(\underline F>0\) from some date on, and \(\left(r+\delta\right)q_\infty\ge\underline F>0\).

Step 3: the growth rate. By step 2, \(K^Y_t/Y_t=\beta_K\left(1-\zeta_t\Omega^Y_t\right)/\tilde F_{K,t}\to k_Y\equiv\beta_K\vartheta_Y/\left[\left(r+\delta\right)q_\infty\right]>0\), so \(K^Y\) grows at \(g\), and the production function gives \[ \frac{Y_{t+1}}{Y_t}=\Gamma\,e^{-\kappa\left(P_{t+1}-P_t\right)}\left(\frac{K^Y_{t+1}}{K^Y_t}\right)^{\beta_K}\left(\frac{R^e_{t+1}}{R^e_t}\right)^{\gamma}\;\longrightarrow\;\Gamma\left(1+g\right)^{\beta_K}, \] since \(P_t\to0\) and \(R^e_t\to R^e_\infty>0\). The left side tends to \(1+g\), so \(\left(1+g\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\Gamma>1\) and \(g=g^{\mathrm{circ}}>0\): output grows geometrically.

Step 4: the root of (KR) grows at most linearly. Since \(x_t\to\infty\), from some date \(T_2\ge T_1\) on \(x_t\) is defined, finite and positive, so \(\varpi_tH_t>0\) and \(K^R_t>0\); and \(K^R_t<K_t\), since \(Y_t>0\) needs \(K^Y_t>0\). So (KR) holds, \(\xi e^{-\xi x_t}\mathcal G^K_t=\tilde F_{K,t}\); its root is unique, \(a'\) being strictly decreasing; and \(\mathcal G^K_t=\tilde F_{K,t}e^{\xi x_t}/\xi>0\), \(x_t=\xi^{-1}\ln\left(\xi\mathcal G^K_t/\tilde F_{K,t}\right)\). The pollution price grows at most geometrically: by (PP), \(\chi_{t+1}p^P_{t+1}=\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^P_t-\mathcal V_{t+1}\le\left(1+r_{t+1}\right)p^P_t\), since \(\mathcal V>0\) on a candidate, as the first part of the proof of Lemma P shows; and \(\chi_{t+1}=1-\theta\left(P_{t+1}\right)-\theta'\left(P_{t+1}\right)P_{t+1}\ge1-\theta\left(P_{t+1}\right)\to1-\theta\left(0\right)>0\) as \(P_t\to0\). So \(p^P_{t+1}\le\bar c\,p^P_t\) for a constant \(\bar c\) from some date on. With \(\hat\Psi_t\) and \(\hat p^W_t\) convergent and \(Y_t\) geometric, \(0<\mathcal G^K_t\le\left|\Psi_t\right|+\left|p^W_t\right|+d^Wp^P_t\) grows at most geometrically, and \(x_t\le\xi^{-1}\ln\left(\xi\mathcal G^K_t/\underline F\right)\) grows at most linearly. As \(H_t=\mu\mathcal W_t\le\mu\mathcal B\) by Lemma A(b), \(K^R_t=x_tH_t\) grows at most linearly, \(K^R_t/Y_t\to0\), and \(k_Y=k\): \(\left(r+\delta\right)q_\infty k=\beta_K\vartheta_Y\).

Step 5: the waste price. By (W), \(\hat p^{\mathcal W}_t=-\hat p^W_t\). For \(t\ge T_1\), \(\varpi_t=1\), the last bracket of \(h\) is \(d^W\left(1-\alpha_t\right)\), and \(\alpha_t=1-\left(1+\xi x_t\right)e^{-\xi x_t}\to1\). The handling charge per tonne is at most \(c^c\left(0\right)+c^T\left(1,0\right)\), so its deflated value tends to zero. For the pollution term, Lemma P applies, since \(\varpi_tH_t>0\) from \(T_2\) on, so a handled tonne emits and pollution is positive at a date, and it gives \(p^P_t\ge0\); \(d^Wp^P_t=\mathcal G^K_t-\left(\Psi_t-p^W_t\right)\) by (G), and \(\mathcal G^K_t\left(1-\alpha_t\right)=\left(1+\xi x_t\right)\tilde F_{K,t}/\xi\) by (KR); so \[ 0\;\le\;d^W\hat p^P_t\left(1-\alpha_t\right)\;\le\;\frac{\left(1+\xi x_t\right)\tilde F_{K,t}R^e_\infty}{\xi\,Y_t}+\left|\hat\Psi_t-\hat p^W_t\right|\left(1-\alpha_t\right)\;\longrightarrow\;0 \] by steps 2 to 4. So the deflated dividend tends to \(\hat\Psi+\hat p^{\mathcal W}\), and the deflated (H) tends to \(\left(1+r\right)\hat p^{\mathcal W}=\left(1+g\right)\left[\mu\left(\hat\Psi+\hat p^{\mathcal W}\right)+\left(1-\mu\right)\hat p^{\mathcal W}\right]\), that is \(\left(r-g\right)\hat p^{\mathcal W}=\mu\left(1+g\right)\hat\Psi\): \(\hat p^W=-\Theta_{\mathcal W}\hat\Psi\).

Step 6: the block. The deflated (M) tends to \(\left(1+r\right)\hat p^M=\left(1+g\right)\left[\delta\hat p^W+\left(1-\delta\right)\hat p^M\right]\), and \(1+r>1+g>\left(1-\delta\right)\left(1+g\right)\) gives \(\hat p^M=\Theta_M\hat p^W\) with \(\Theta_M\in\left(0,1\right)\). (\(\Omega\)) gives \(\hat\zeta=\sigma_\infty\left(1-\Theta_M\right)\hat p^W=-\Upsilon\hat\Psi\). The definition \(\Psi=F_R\left(1-\zeta\Omega^Y\right)+\zeta\) with \(F_R=\gamma Y/R^e\) and \(R^e_t\to R^e_\infty\) gives \(\hat\Psi=\gamma\left(1-\hat\zeta\hat\Omega\omega^Y\right)+\hat\zeta\), so \(\hat\Psi\left[1+\Upsilon\left(1-\gamma\hat\Omega\omega^Y\right)\right]=\gamma\). As \(\gamma>0\) the bracket is not zero, and \(\hat\Psi\) is the first entry of (B). With \(\hat\zeta=-\Upsilon\hat\Psi\) the same identity reads \[ \vartheta_Y\;=\;1-\hat\zeta\hat\Omega\omega^Y\;=\;\frac{\left(1+\Upsilon\right)\hat\Psi}{\gamma}, \] so \(\vartheta_Y>0\) exactly when \(\hat\Psi>0\), and step 4’s relation is the capital arbitrage. Since \(\Phi^I\left(p^W-p^M\right)=\phi^I\hat\Omega\left(\hat p^W-\hat p^M\right)\), (I) in the limit gives \(q_\infty\) of (B), and \(q_\infty\ge1\). This proves (a).

Step 7: part (b). By Lemma P, which applies since \(\varpi_tH_t>0\) from \(T_2\) on, \(p^P_t\ge0\), so \(\mathcal G^K_t\ge\Psi_t-p^W_t=\left(\hat\Psi_t-\hat p^W_t\right)Y_t/R^e_\infty\), where \(\hat\Psi_t-\hat p^W_t\to\left(1+\Theta_{\mathcal W}\right)\hat\Psi>0\). So \(\xi\mathcal G^K_t>\tilde F_{K,t}\) at every late date, the root of step 4 is positive, and \[ L_t\;=\;1-a\left(x_t\right)\;=\;e^{-\xi x_t}\;=\;\frac{\tilde F_{K,t}}{\xi\,\mathcal G^K_t}\;\le\;\frac{\tilde F_{K,t}\,R^e_\infty}{\xi\left(\hat\Psi_t-\hat p^W_t\right)Y_t}, \] whose right side, with \(\tilde F_{K,t}\) convergent and \(Y_t\) growing at \(g>0\), is below \(\bar L\left(1+g/2\right)^{-t}\) for a constant \(\bar L\). So \(\sum_tL_t<\infty\): the closure criterion holds; and as \(\varpi_t=1\) from \(T_1\) on, \(\sum_t\left(1-a_t\varpi_t\right)<\infty\): the loop closes along the margin. By (G), Lemma P and \(d^W<1\), \(\mathcal G^\varpi_t=\alpha_t\mathcal G^K_t+\left(1-d^W\right)p^P_t\ge\alpha_t\mathcal G^K_t\to+\infty\). If the marginal handling charge at full treatment, \(c^c\left(t\right)+c^{T\prime}\left(1,t\right)\), is bounded over \(t\), as the last clause of (b) supposes, then, since the wedge \(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t=1+\hat\zeta_t\hat\Omega_t\omega^W\) converges, by step 1 and the convergence of \(\hat\zeta_t\), the marginal handling cost at full treatment, \(\left[c^c+c^{T\prime}\left(1\right)\right]\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right)\), is bounded across dates. So \(\mathcal G^\varpi_t\ge\left[c^c+c^{T\prime}\left(1\right)\right]\left(1+\zeta_t\Omega^W_t\right)\) from a finite date on, which is the condition for the upper corner.

Step 8: part (c). At \(\phi^I=0\), \(\sigma_\infty=\Upsilon=0\), (B) gives \(\hat\Psi=\gamma\), \(\hat\zeta=0\) and \(q_\infty=1\), (WI) holds, and the capital arbitrage has the root \(k=\beta_K/\left(r+\delta\right)\), with \(\left(g+\delta\right)k<\beta_K<1\) since \(r>g\). Let \(\phi^I>0\), and write \(s\equiv\left(g+\delta\right)k\). Through \(c_Y=1-s\), step 1 makes \(\bar d\), \(\sigma_\infty\) and \(\hat\Omega\) continuous functions of \(s\in\left(0,1\right]\): \(\bar d\left(s\right)=\omega^Y+\omega^C\left(1-s\right)+\phi^Is\), \(\sigma_\infty\left(s\right)=\phi^Is/\bar d\left(s\right)\le1\) and \(\hat\Omega\left(s\right)=R_\infty/\left[\bar d\left(s\right)R^e_\infty\right]\). Write \(D\left(s\right)\equiv1+\Upsilon\left(1-\gamma\hat\Omega\omega^Y\right)\). Where \(D\ne0\), (B) gives \(1-\hat\zeta\hat\Omega\omega^Y=\left(1+\Upsilon\right)/D\) and \(q_\infty=1+\phi^I\left(1-\sigma_\infty\right)\Theta_\sigma\hat\Omega\gamma/D\), and the capital arbitrage, multiplied by \(\left(g+\delta\right)D\), reads \(f\left(s\right)=0\) with \[ f\left(s\right)\equiv\left(r+\delta\right)s\left[1+\Theta_\sigma\sigma_\infty+E\left(s\right)\right]-\left(g+\delta\right)\beta_K\left(1+\Theta_\sigma\sigma_\infty\right), \qquad E\left(s\right)\equiv\frac{\Theta_\sigma\gamma\phi^IR_\infty\left(1-s\right)\left(\omega^Y+\omega^C\right)}{\bar d\left(s\right)^2R^e_\infty}\ge0, \] because \(\phi^I\left(1-\sigma_\infty\right)-\sigma_\infty\omega^Y=\phi^I\left(1-s\right)\left(\omega^Y+\omega^C\right)/\bar d\). \(f\) is continuous on \(\left(0,1\right]\). If \(\omega^Y+\omega^C>0\), then \(\bar d\left(s\right)\ge\omega^Y+\min\left\{\omega^C,\phi^I\right\}>0\) on \(\left(0,1\right]\) and \(E\) is bounded; otherwise \(E=0\). Either way \(sE\left(s\right)\to0\) as \(s\to0\), and since \(\sigma_\infty\le1\), \(f\left(s\right)\le\left(r+\delta\right)s\left[1+\Theta_\sigma+E\left(s\right)\right]-\left(g+\delta\right)\beta_K<0\) for small \(s\). And \(E\left(1\right)=0\), so \(f\left(1\right)=\left(1+\Theta_\sigma\sigma_\infty\left(1\right)\right)\left[\left(r+\delta\right)-\left(g+\delta\right)\beta_K\right]>0\), since \(r>g\) and \(\beta_K<1\). So \(f\) has a root \(s\in\left(0,1\right)\), and \(k=s/\left(g+\delta\right)\). At a root where (WI) holds, \(D>0\), and \(f=0\) is the capital arbitrage itself. Steps 1 to 6 obtain (B) by solving the limit forms of (C), (\(\Omega\)), (I), (W), (Q), (M), (H) and of the definition of \(\Psi\), one entry at a time, so the limits (B) satisfy each of them: the limit form of (C), \(1+r=\left(1+\rho\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\eta}\), needs the limit consumption wedge \(1-\Upsilon\hat\Psi\hat\Omega\omega^C\) to be positive, which is the second inequality of (WI), and \(c_Y=1-s>0\).

Step 8 shows that the limit system has a solution at a capital-output ratio in the admissible range, and part (b) that along the path the recycling margins are met with the path’s own intensity and, when the marginal handling charge at full treatment is bounded, with full treatment. Of the extraction, exploration and pollution-price margins, the path asks only that extraction and discovery die out, and step 8 asks also that their costs vanish against output.

The rates of a state-B candidate

Appendix B.6 takes a state-B path at \(\bar R=0\) whose material flows all decay at \(\nu\), whose recycling margins converge and whose exploration ends no later than its extraction, and fixes its rates by the production function and by the extraction margin, on which the marginal cost of a virgin tonne tracks the material value \(\Psi\sim\gamma Y/R\). We write \(\mathfrak e\equiv\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)+\gamma\).

Lemma W2. Take the workhorse at \(\bar R=0\) and a candidate state-B path with rates \(\left(g,\nu\right)\) along which \(R\) and \(N\) decay at \(\nu\), the recycling margins converge, exploration ends no later than extraction, \(D_t/N_t\to0\), and the marginal cost of a virgin tonne tracks the material value: \(C^N_{N,t}R_t/Y_t\) converges to a positive limit. Then \[ \left(1+g\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\left(1+g_A\right)\left[\left(1+g_B\right)\left(1-\nu\right)\right]^{\gamma}, \qquad \left(1-\nu\right)^{1-\mu_N}=1+g , \tag{R2} \] and this pair has a solution with \(\nu\in\left(0,1\right)\) exactly when \(\mathfrak e>0\), namely \[ 1-\nu=\Gamma^{-1/\mathfrak e}, \qquad 1+g=\left(1-\nu\right)^{1-\mu_N}=\Gamma^{\left(\mu_N-1\right)/\mathfrak e} . \]

Proof. Pollution converges. \(\Xi_t=\Xi^h_t+\mathcal N_t\le\mu\mathcal W_t+\Omega^{N,S}N_t+\Omega^{D,S}D_t\to0\), since \(\mathcal W\) decays and \(N_t,D_t\to0\) by Lemma A(a). By Assumption 1(iii), \(P_{t+1}=\left[1-\theta\left(P_t\right)\right]P_t+\Xi_t\le\left(1-\theta_{\min}\right)P_t+\Xi_t\); for any \(b>0\), once \(\Xi_s\le b\,\theta_{\min}\) for all \(s\ge t\), \(P_{t+n}\le\left(1-\theta_{\min}\right)^nP_t+b\), so \(P_t\to0\).

The production row. At \(\bar R=0\), \(Y_t=A_te^{-\kappa P_t}\left(K^Y_t\right)^{\beta_K}\left(B_tR_t\right)^{\gamma}\). By Definition 1(B), \(Y\) and \(K^Y\) grow at \(g\) and \(R\) decays at \(\nu\), so \[ \frac{Y_{t+1}}{Y_t}=\left(1+g_A\right)\left(1+g_B\right)^{\gamma}e^{-\kappa\left(P_{t+1}-P_t\right)}\left(\frac{K^Y_{t+1}}{K^Y_t}\right)^{\beta_K}\left(\frac{R_{t+1}}{R_t}\right)^{\gamma} \;\longrightarrow\;\left(1+g_A\right)\left(1+g\right)^{\beta_K}\left[\left(1+g_B\right)\left(1-\nu\right)\right]^{\gamma}, \] and the left side tends to \(1+g\): the first equation of (R2). It gives \(\left(1+g\right)^{1-\beta_K}=\Gamma\left(1-\nu\right)^{\gamma}>\left(1-\nu\right)^{\gamma}>\left(1-\nu\right)^{1-\beta_K}\), since \(\gamma<1-\beta_K\) and \(1-\nu<1\); so \(1+g>1-\nu\).

The reserve is exhausted. By the tracking property \(C^N_{N,t}\) grows at the factor \(\left(1+g\right)/\left(1-\nu\right)>1\) of output per tonne, so \(C^N_{N,t}\to\infty\). As \(c_N\left(N_t,t\right)\to c_{N\infty}>0\), \(\left(S_{\mathrm{ref}}/S_t\right)^{\mu_N}=C^N_{N,t}/c_N\left(N_t,t\right)\to\infty\): \(S_t\to0\), and \[ \left(\frac{S_t}{S_{t+1}}\right)^{\mu_N}=\frac{C^N_{N,t+1}}{C^N_{N,t}}\cdot\frac{c_N\left(N_t,t\right)}{c_N\left(N_{t+1},t+1\right)}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{1+g}{1-\nu}. \]

The reserve decays at the rate of extraction. Summing \(S_{s+1}=S_s+D_s-N_s\) over \(s\ge t\) with \(S_s\to0\) gives \(S_t=\sum_{s\ge t}\left(N_s-D_s\right)\), both sums being finite by Lemma A(a). \(N\) has the factor \(1-\nu\), so for \(\bar\varrho\in\left(1-\nu,1\right)\) and large \(t\), \(N_{t+j}/N_t\le\bar\varrho^{\,j}\) for every \(j\), while \(N_{t+j}/N_t\to\left(1-\nu\right)^j\) for each \(j\); by dominated convergence \(\sum_{s\ge t}N_s/N_t\to1/\nu\), and \(D_s/N_s\to0\) gives \(\sum_{s\ge t}D_s/\sum_{s\ge t}N_s\to0\). So \(S_t/N_t\to1/\nu\), and \(S_{t+1}/S_t=\left(S_{t+1}/N_{t+1}\right)\left(N_{t+1}/N_t\right)\left(N_t/S_t\right)\to1-\nu\). With the previous display, \(\left(1-\nu\right)^{-\mu_N}=\left(1+g\right)/\left(1-\nu\right)\): the second equation of (R2).

The solution. Write \(u\equiv\ln\left(1+g\right)\) and \(w\equiv\ln\left(1-\nu\right)\), so that \(\nu\in\left(0,1\right)\) is \(w<0\). (R2) is linear in them: \(\left(1-\beta_K\right)u=\ln\Gamma+\gamma w\) and \(u=\left(1-\mu_N\right)w\). Substituting, \(\left[\left(1-\beta_K\right)\left(1-\mu_N\right)-\gamma\right]w=\ln\Gamma\), that is \(-\mathfrak e\,w=\ln\Gamma\), with \(\ln\Gamma>0\). If \(\mathfrak e>0\), \(w=-\ln\Gamma/\mathfrak e<0\) and \(u=\left(1-\mu_N\right)w\), which is the displayed solution. If \(\mathfrak e=0\) there is no solution, and if \(\mathfrak e<0\) the only solution has \(w>0\), which is not a decay rate in \(\left(0,1\right)\). So a candidate of the kind described exists only if \(\mathfrak e>0\), and then it has these rates.

The rates use the production function, the decay of the flows, the end of exploration and the tracking property; the convergence of the recycling margins is not used. The growth rate is the \(g^{\mathrm{dem}}\) of Appendix B.6: since \(\Gamma=\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{1-\beta_K}\), \(1+g=\left(1+g^{\mathrm{circ}}\right)^{\left(\mu_N-1\right)\left(1-\beta_K\right)/\mathfrak e}\).
